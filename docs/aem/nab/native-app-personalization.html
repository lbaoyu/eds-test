Overview

Yes, your native app screens can be personalized

You asked whether your team can test different copy and links on native mobile app screens, such as the Explore page, the way you already do on nab.com.au. This guide sets out a recommended direction and the design principles for doing that with Adobe Target and AEM as a Cloud Service. We'll work through the implementation detail together once the direction is agreed.

Feasibility

It's achievable

Native screens can be personalized with Target. Unlike on the web, Target doesn't edit the screen; it chooses which content to show.

The model

Three clear roles

AEM authors the content, Target decides which version each customer sees, and your app displays it with its own native components.

Foundation

You already have the building blocks

The Content Fragments you use today for rates, fees and disclaimers are the right foundation, and the same approach can later fix NAB Connect activities that break when page markup changes.

First step

Start small

A single A/B test on the Explore hero card proves the model before it scales to more slots, screens and channels.

How it works

Slots, not page edits

On your website, Target changes the page directly. A native app can't be edited that way, because its screens are built into the app. Instead, the app reserves specific areas of a screen as personalization slots.

When a customer opens the screen, the app asks Target which version of each slot to show. Target decides based on the customer's audience, the active tests and the targeting rules. The content itself comes from AEM, so your authors keep working in a familiar tool, and the app displays it in its own native design. Every view and tap flows back to Target to measure results.

What changes from your website

The goals stay the same; the mechanics change. The biggest shift is that the app decides what can vary. Each slot exposes a defined set of fields, and switching on a slot takes one app release. After that, your team can run tests and change content without further releases.

Design principles

Six principles to design around

Principle 01

Each product does one job

AEM owns content, Target owns decisions, and the app owns presentation. No one copies content into Target by hand.

Principle 02

Design slots, not screens

Personalize defined areas of a screen, each with a clear set of fields that can change, rather than varying whole screens.

Principle 03

Keep layout and content separate

Layout lives in the app; content lives in AEM. Test variants are variations of the same content, not new builds.

Principle 04

Reference regulated content

Rates, fees and disclaimers are pulled from your existing Content Fragments, so a test can never show an out-of-date rate.

Principle 05

Always have a safe default

Every slot has default content, and the screen never waits for Target to decide.

Principle 06

Author once, use everywhere

The same content structures can serve the app, Internet Banking, NAB Connect and nab.com.au.

Starting point

Start with the Explore screen

Your Explore screen breaks down naturally into slots. The hero promo card is the best place to start: it's the most visible area, it stands on its own, and its results are easy to measure. The product tiles come next. Changing their order, labels or links puts the most relevant products first for each segment. The section header has lower impact and can be bundled with the tiles.

The Personal / Business toggle isn't a slot. It's context about what the customer is browsing, and it lets each tab have its own experience.

A design point to settle early: once labels are tested remotely, every slot needs length limits and text that wraps or resizes cleanly. Longer tile labels such as “Offers & promotions” should always break between words, never mid-word.

Content and delivery

Separate layout from content

Each type of slot gets one content structure in AEM. A promo card, for example, has a headline, body copy, image, button, link and badge, plus references to the relevant rate and disclaimer fragments. Test variants are then variations of the same fragment, so a single layout supports many content combinations without a new build each time.

Less duplication

One structure with many variations replaces a new Experience Fragment for every small content difference.

A built-in template

Fields, limits and badges are predefined, so authors only fill in content: the personalization-specific template you asked for.

Easier reuse

A consistent folder and tagging scheme by channel, screen and slot makes existing content easy to find and reuse.

Two ways to deliver AEM content

Because you're on AEM as a Cloud Service, both options are available, and they can be used side by side.

Option A

Content travels with the offer Fastest to start

How
AEM content is exported to Target as an offer; Target delivers it to the app.
Strength
Quick to set up; your authors can run it themselves.
Watch-out
Content must be exported again whenever it changes.
Best for
Pure copy and link tests.
Option B

Target decides, AEM delivers Regulated content

How
Target tells the app which variant to show; the app fetches the live content from AEM.
Strength
Always current; AEM stays the single source.
Watch-out
Content must be published before a test launches.
Best for
Anything with rates, fees, eligibility or legal copy.
Our recommendation

Pilot with Option A for speed, then standardize on Option B for anything that mentions rates, fees, eligibility or legal copy.

Getting started

From first test to scale

How a test runs, from author to customer

  1. Author

    Create the content variants in AEM.

  2. Review and approve

    App content carries legal exposure, so this is a natural place for a light approval step in AEM.

  3. Publish or export

    Publish the content (Option B) or export it to Target (Option A).

  4. Set up the activity

    Choose the audience, variants and success metric in Target.

  5. QA on a device

    Check every variant on a real device before launch.

  6. Launch and learn

    The winning variant becomes the new default in AEM.
