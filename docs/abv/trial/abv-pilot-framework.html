The engagement

A customer-owned, Adobe-supported pilot

Sixty days to move from an unknown — how the brand appears in AI-powered experiences — to a defensible baseline, one completed optimization cycle, and a repeatable operating model with a clear next step.

Pilot principle

Discover the opportunity, define what matters, activate meaningful improvements, and prove the outcome.

Days 1–60

The five steps, on one timeline

Each segment below is sized to its real share of the sixty days — note how Activate & Measure dominates. Select a step to see its focus, output, and gate.

Day 1Day 60

01 Frame · Days 1–5

Establish the business and operational foundation for the pilot.

Primary outcome Approved pilot charter

Focus

  • Select one brand, business unit, market, or region.
  • Choose one or two priority customer journeys.
  • Define the business outcome the pilot should influence.
  • Confirm priority domains, URLs, products, services, and competitors.
  • Identify customer owners for marketing, content, web, analytics, CDN, and approvals.
  • Confirm the ABV capabilities available to the customer.
  • Select the primary KPI and supporting measures.
  • Agree on the Day 60 decision criteria.
  • Establish the customer and Adobe working cadence.

Output

  • Pilot objective and value hypothesis.
  • Scope and exclusions.
  • Customer and Adobe responsibilities.
  • Priority journeys and business questions.
  • KPI and measurement approach.
  • Feature availability confirmation.
  • Working cadence and decision date.

Step gate

The customer approves:

  • Pilot scope
  • Business objective
  • Ownership model
  • Measurement approach
  • Day 60 decision criteria

02 Discover · Days 6–14

Understand the external market and find where the customer has the greatest opportunity to improve its AI visibility.

Primary outcome Market opportunity map

Focus

  • Identify high-value topics and customer questions.
  • Discover relevant prompt patterns.
  • Understand how competitors appear in AI-generated answers.
  • Identify visibility, citation, content, and source gaps.
  • Compare brand presence across relevant topics and markets.
  • Separate customer-controlled opportunities from those that depend on external sources.
  • Analyze relevant web, community, video, and other cited sources where available.
  • Use market and competitive intelligence to validate the customer’s initial assumptions.

Output

  • Priority topics and high-value prompt groups.
  • Competitor gaps and citation opportunities.
  • Relevant source patterns.
  • Potential owned-content and brand-claim actions.
  • Potential technical, edge, and off-site actions.

Step gate

Customer and Adobe agree on the limited set of opportunities that will enter the pilot — not every opportunity discovered.

Prompt Research

03 Define · Days 15–22

Turn market insight into a customer-specific monitoring strategy, an approved baseline, and a focused action plan.

Primary outcome Approved baseline & action plan

Focus

  • Build and approve the Prompt Library.
  • Organize prompts by journey, topic, market, product, and business priority.
  • Configure customer-specific Brand Presence monitoring.
  • Review visibility, mentions, citations, sentiment, and competitive position.
  • Review Brand Claims and relevant Off-site Opportunities, where available.
  • Validate referral and agentic traffic measurement, where included.
  • Select the opportunity for the mandatory optimization cycle.
  • Define the expected improvement and measurement method.
  • Lock the baseline before production changes begin.

Output

  • Versioned Prompt Library and approved monitoring strategy.
  • Visibility and citation baseline; Brand Claims baseline, where available.
  • Off-site opportunity snapshot.
  • Traffic and business-impact baseline, where available.
  • Prioritized opportunity backlog.
  • Selected mandatory optimization strategy.
  • Measurement and comparison rules.

Step gate

No production optimization begins until the customer approves:

  • The prompt set
  • The monitoring scope
  • The baseline
  • The measurement method
  • The selected mandatory opportunity

Key distinction

IdentifyVisibility Overview, Prompt Research, and Market Comparison surface the market landscape and opportunity areas.
DefinePrompt Strategy, Prompt Library, and Brand Presence set what the customer will monitor and measure during the pilot.

04 Activate & Measure · Days 23–50

Turn the highest-priority opportunity into a customer-owned action and measure the result. This is the widest step by design — it holds the mandatory optimization cycle.

Primary outcome Measured optimization results

Focus

  • Complete one mandatory optimization cycle, end to end.
  • Run an optional second cycle in parallel only if it is clearly appropriate.
  • Address a real gap: a content or citation gap, an underrepresented page, a brand-claim correction, a supported technical or edge optimization, a high-value prompt cluster, or an opportunity surfaced by market analysis.
  • Validate and monitor the deployed change against the locked baseline.

Output

  • A documented change: what was changed and why it was selected.
  • Who approved and implemented it.
  • How the change was measured.
  • What improved — or did not.
  • What to repeat or change in the next cycle.

Cycle requirement

One complete, measured optimization cycle is required before the pilot can be judged. A second parallel cycle is optional evidence — never a prerequisite. See the optimization cycle below.

05 Prove & Decide · Days 51–60

Evaluate value, repeatability, and readiness to scale — then make the call.

Primary outcome Final readout & scale recommendation

Focus

  • AI visibility and share of voice; citation rate and cited sources.
  • Brand-claim accuracy, sentiment, and competitive position.
  • Prompt and topic coverage; agentic or AI referral traffic.
  • Engagement, conversions, and revenue where measurement is available.
  • Time and effort required to move from insight to action.
  • Customer ability to repeat the process independently.
  • Adobe support required to operate the workflow.

Output

  • Before-and-after scorecard and mandatory-cycle results.
  • Optional parallel-cycle results, if performed.
  • Business-impact assessment; risks and limitations.
  • Recommended operating model and next-stage roadmap.
  • A scale, iterate, hold, or stop recommendation.

Decision

Deliver a decision-ready recommendation — scale, iterate, hold, or stop — grounded in the scorecard and the pilot’s demonstrated repeatability.

The finish line

Day 60 success

The pilot succeeds when the customer holds each of these — and can answer the framework’s core question at every step. The optional parallel cycle can strengthen the evidence, but is never required.

The customer has

  • A market-informed ABV opportunity
  • An approved and defensible baseline
  • One completed and measured optimization cycle
  • A clear understanding of what improved and why
  • Visibility into available traffic or business impact
  • A documented, customer-owned operating process
  • A decision-ready recommendation to scale, iterate, hold, or stop

Optional second cycle

The optional second cycle

A second cycle may run in parallel with the mandatory one. It does not extend the 60-day pilot or create a separate step — and it is never required for the pilot to succeed.

Begin only when
  • A second opportunity is clearly defined.
  • The customer has sufficient implementation capacity.
  • The first cycle’s measurement remains valid.
  • The two cycles can be measured independently.
  • There is enough observation time before the final readout.
  • Running both will not reduce the quality of the mandatory cycle.
Suitable parallel opportunities
  • A second owned-content or technical improvement
  • A Brand Claims improvement
  • An off-site authority or reputation opportunity
  • A competitive visibility gap
  • A separate topic or customer journey
  • A refinement that tests a different optimization approach

The second cycle is optional evidence, not a requirement for pilot success. If it isn’t appropriate, use the time to strengthen the mandatory cycle, complete measurement, resolve implementation issues, validate results, and prepare the final decision.

Customer-safe trial positioning

Position a trial as a focused proof of value. Typical limits — confirm the exact set during setup:

  • One brand
  • One domain
  • One market
  • Limited prompt set
  • Limited AI models
  • Capped optimization deployments
  • Reduced reporting / integration depth

A trial should not be judged against capabilities it does not include.
