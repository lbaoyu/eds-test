01 — Thesis

The bottom line

Over the next three to five years, GEO evolves from a tactical push to "get cited by ChatGPT" into a broader discipline of AI discoverability, brand representation, and agentic conversion.

The larger change is that GEO likely stops being a separate label at all. Its capabilities get absorbed into SEO, digital PR, content operations, product-data management, analytics, and brand governance — the way "mobile optimisation" stopped being a project and simply became how the web is built.

The future objective

Not merely to be visible in an AI answer — but to be correctly understood, trusted, recommended, and usable by the AI systems that help people make decisions and take actions.

Part of this is already explicit. In May 2026, Google published its first official guidance on generative-AI search and stated plainly that "AEO" and "GEO" are still SEO — its AI features run on the same core index and ranking systems as classic Search. The discipline is not new. The surface is.

03 — What changes

Eight shifts inside the discipline

1

From pages to entities and knowledge

Teams optimise pages today. Increasingly they will optimise how an AI system understands the company itself: what it is, which products belong to it, which use cases each supports, how they compare with alternatives, which claims are verified, and which markets, prices, policies and capabilities are current.

In practice that means maintaining a consistent entity, product and claims graph across the website, documentation, reviews, media, communities, product feeds and structured data — an ongoing representation system rather than a set of isolated pages.

2

From citation to influence

Citations stay useful, but become only one layer of performance. A brand can earn many mentions and still be described inaccurately or negatively — so the target moves up a ladder:

PresentMentioned in the answer at all.
ProminentMentioned early, or ranked highly among options.
PortrayedDescribed accurately, currently, and favourably.
PersuasiveRecommended — and linked to a meaningful action.

Measurement follows suit: visibility, citations, share of voice, sentiment, accuracy, referral and agent traffic, and business impact — not citation count alone.

3

From search answers to AI agents

The next frontier isn't only an AI answering "which product is best?" It's an agent deciding which product fits this user, is available in this region, meets these constraints, and can be bought or configured now. That requires information machines can use: accurate product and service feeds; current pricing, inventory, availability and eligibility; structured policies and terms; clear APIs and agent interfaces; machine-readable documentation; secure authentication; and reliable workflows for booking, purchasing, configuring or requesting service.

This layer is already materialising. Through 2025–2026 several checkout protocols shipped — including OpenAI's Agentic Commerce Protocol with Stripe, Google's Universal Commerce Protocol, Microsoft's Copilot Checkout, and Perplexity's Instant Buy with PayPal. The instructive episode was OpenAI's: it launched ChatGPT Instant Checkout in September 2025 and sunset it in March 2026. The blocker wasn't the model — it was reconciling real-time inventory, pricing and sales-tax across millions of merchants. An architectural settlement is emerging from that lesson: AI systems own discovery and intent; merchants keep control of checkout. At this point GEO overlaps with product-information management, digital commerce, conversion optimisation and API strategy.

4

From prompt lists to journey and intent modelling

Representative prompt libraries are necessary, but static lists become insufficient. Teams will model AI discovery across the whole journey rather than optimising isolated phrases:

Explore · what problem is this? Evaluate · which options exist? Compare · what are the trade-offs? Decide · which should I choose? Act · can the agent complete it? Use · how do I get value?

The focus shifts from ranking for a keyword to owning the decision narrative across these stages.

5

Measurement becomes more scientific — and more contested

Ranking position and click-through become incomplete indicators. Pew Research put a number on the gap: when an AI summary appears, users click a traditional result far less often, and the citation footer inside the summary is almost never used. Visibility and influence increasingly happen without a conventional click.

First-party measurement is starting to catch up — Google added a generative-AI performance report to Search Console from mid-2026. The mature model blends AI-answer visibility, mention and citation rate, prominence and recommendation strength, accuracy and sentiment, referral and agent traffic, branded-search lift, assisted conversions and revenue influence — with controlled experiments to prove causation. That causal step is the hard problem: showing that improved AI visibility caused commercial impact rather than merely correlating with it.

6

Off-site authority matters even more

AI systems synthesise from a network of sources — independent reviews, industry publications, research reports, forums and communities, video and transcripts, reference sources, and partner and analyst content. And citation concentration is real: Pew found that Wikipedia, YouTube and Reddit dominate the sources AI summaries draw on.

That makes GEO partly a digital-PR and reputation discipline. The winning move is to create genuinely useful evidence and earn credible coverage — not to flood communities with promotional content, which Google explicitly warns against.

7

Governance and factual control become central

As AI answers increasingly influence buying and business decisions, organisations must monitor whether those answers carry outdated pricing, wrong feature descriptions, unsupported performance claims, incorrect product associations, misleading comparisons, negative or stale community narratives, or confusion between similarly named products.

This creates a new operational capability — AI representation governance — with a shared process across marketing, product, legal, communications, support and web: define the official claim, provide evidence, detect incorrect external representations, update owned and earned sources, and measure whether the correction propagates.

8

Paid and organic GEO converge

As platforms add sponsored placements, product recommendations and commercial integrations, teams will need to distinguish organic mention, organic citation, sponsored placement, product-feed inclusion, agent recommendation and agent-completed transaction.

This pulls GEO closer to paid media, retail media, commerce feeds and journey analytics — and brings disclosure and trust requirements with it. The formats are still unsettled, so avoid building strategy around any single platform's current ad or citation behaviour.

04 — Horizon

A realistic timeline

Phase 1
Next 12–18 months

Operationalisation

SEO and GEO teams begin sharing data and workflows. Prompt libraries and AI-visibility dashboards become standard. Content adds answer summaries, clearer structure, comparisons and evidence. Teams monitor citations, sentiment, accuracy and AI referral traffic. Off-site authority becomes a formal workstream.

Phase 2
Years 2–3

Platform integration

GEO data connects with web analytics, CRM, content systems and product catalogues. Brand claims and product facts are managed centrally. Optimisation expands to video, community, reviews, feeds, APIs and documentation. AI visibility joins campaign and launch measurement. "Share of model" becomes a planning metric, though methodologies vary.

Phase 3
Years 3–5

Agentic optimisation

Agents research, compare, recommend and take actions. Sites must serve both people and machine customers. GEO includes agent access, tool calls, APIs, feeds, policies and transaction readiness. Paid and organic AI discovery become distinct but connected channels. GEO is embedded within a broader discipline — AI Search Optimisation, AI Brand Visibility, or AI Discovery Operations.

A note on pace. With agentic-commerce protocols already shipping in 2026, the infrastructure for Phase 3 is arriving ahead of the trust-and-adoption curve. The binding constraint is confidence and clean data, not raw capability.
05 — Stop investing

What to de-prioritise

Do not overinvest in tactics that assume yesterday's mechanics:

×Keyword stuffing
×Generic AI-generated articles
×Large volumes of near-duplicate FAQ pages
×One-off prompt testing
×Optimising for a single chatbot
×Treating schema markup as a magic citation mechanism
×Chasing citations without checking accuracy or sentiment
×Artificial Reddit, review or forum activity
×A single "GEO score" with no reproducible methodology

Google's 2026 guidance sharpens this: there is no llms.txt file, content "chunking", AI-specific rewriting or special schema that unlocks its AI features — they run on the same fundamentals as Search. Structured data still earns its place, but as an accuracy aid, not a citation shortcut.

The nuance worth holding. For agentic commerce, clean machine-readable product feeds do matter — they are what let an agent actually transact. But that is a different job from being cited in an answer, and it is worth keeping the two straight.
06 — Start now

What to build now

None of this waits for the long-term shift — it's table stakes now. Seven priorities, roughly in build order: nail the foundations, then the work that sets you apart, then the measurement and governance that keep it honest.

  1. Get the technical fundamentals right.AI Overviews and AI Mode pull from the same index as classic search, so crawlability, indexability, site speed, clean internal linking and text that's visible in the raw HTML still decide whether you're eligible to surface at all. Fix eligibility before you touch the content — no amount of GEO copy rescues a page the crawlers can't read.
  2. Build a single source of truth for brand facts.Keep one authoritative, up-to-date record of your products, pricing, policies, proof points and comparisons, and have every page, feed and support answer inherit from it. When your facts are inconsistent across the web, models fill the gaps with someone else's version of you — and that's how competitors end up in your answers.
  3. Cover the full buyer journey.Map your prompt and topic coverage across awareness, consideration, comparison and purchase — that's how people actually query an assistant. Optimising only for "is [brand] any good?" leaves the high-intent "best X for Y" and comparison prompts wide open for competitors to win.
  4. Publish content only you can create.Original research, first-party data, tested methodology, real customer outcomes and honest comparisons are what generative engines cite, precisely because they can't be synthesised from everyone else's pages. Commodity, AI-spun content just echoes what the model already knows, so it rarely earns a citation.
  5. Build authority off your own domain.LLMs assemble answers from earned media — reviews, editorial coverage, analyst reports, communities, video and reference sites — and a handful of high-authority sources carry most of the weight. Treat digital PR, reviews and third-party mentions as core GEO work: if you're absent from the sources models trust, you're absent from the answer.
  6. Measure outcomes, not just visibility.Go past citation counts and share of voice and tie AI visibility to what it drives — AI referral and agent traffic, branded-search lift, assisted conversions and revenue. Stand up at least one incrementality test, because attribution is where GEO budgets are won or lost.
  7. Govern how AI represents your brand.Answers go stale, models hallucinate, and outdated pricing or wrong feature claims spread quickly. Give marketing, product, legal, support and web one loop to agree the facts, catch where AI gets you wrong, correct the source it pulls from, and confirm the fix lands. Accuracy is a channel you manage, not a one-off audit.
07 — The final view

The loop

GEO will not replace SEO. It absorbs SEO into a larger AI-discovery discipline. SEO remains responsible for making content accessible and authoritative; GEO increasingly governs what happens after retrieval.
