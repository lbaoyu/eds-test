How to use this roadmap

The enterprise transformation in one view

This roadmap is for an enterprise adopting the Content Supply Chain as an operating model — not merely purchasing several Adobe products. The recommended sequence: align the business → establish the foundation → prove one valuable workflow → scale adoption → introduce AI automation → orchestrate with agents.

Do not begin with maximum automation. Begin with a reliable, measurable, governed content operation.

Phase 0

Mobilize and align

Objective: create the business case, sponsorship, team structure, and transformation boundaries before configuring technology. Treat this phase as decision preparation, not a generic product workshop.

Primary activities

  1. Appoint an executive sponsor.
  2. Establish a cross-functional transformation team.
  3. Agree on the business outcomes to improve.
  4. Document the current content lifecycle.
  5. Identify the biggest delays, costs, risks, and duplication.
  6. Select 3–5 candidate use cases.
  7. Define baseline metrics.
  8. Agree on the initial scope and decision rights.

Recommended participants

CSC product owner / CoEOwns the roadmap, standards, and backlog

Required outputs

  • Current-state content lifecycle map
  • Stakeholder and role map
  • Business outcome definition
  • Initial maturity assessment
  • Prioritized use-case backlog
  • KPI baseline
  • North Star architecture
  • Initial risk and dependency register

Questions to answer

  • Where does a content request originate?
  • How many intake systems exist?
  • Which teams create, review, approve, store, and activate content?
  • Which handoffs are manual?
  • Which assets are difficult to find or reuse?
  • Where does legal or brand approval slow the process?
  • Can a published asset be connected to campaign performance?
  • Which processes are common enough to standardize?

Phase 1

Establish the foundation

Objective: create a dependable operational backbone for planning, production, review, asset management, and reuse. Start with a small but complete flow rather than deploying every capability at once.

Architectural rule

Workfront owns the work context. AEM Assets owns the approved asset. GenStudio uses both to create activation-ready content — connected, but not one undifferentiated repository.

Foundation workstreams

01

Intake & planning

One preferred intake path, a minimum viable brief, campaign/project templates, required objective/audience/channel/timing fields, prioritization rules, capacity visibility.

02

Workflow & approval

Standard vs. expedited workflows, review stages, brand/legal checkpoints, approval authorities, escalation, versioning, approval evidence and retention.

03

Asset governance

Folder/collection structure, metadata schema, taxonomy and controlled vocabularies, naming standards, rights/expiration, status model, ownership, retirement rules.

04

Integration & identity

IMS org and identity model, provisioning, role-based access, Workfront–AEM relationships, metadata sync, agency access, activation connections, compliance.

05

Adoption

Role-based training, super-user network, job aids, office hours, migration/coexistence guidance, communications plan, adoption dashboard.

Foundation exit criteria

Do not move to broad AI or enterprise rollout until the customer can demonstrate all of the following:

  • A request can be submitted and prioritized.
  • A campaign can be planned with structured context.
  • Work can be assigned and tracked.
  • Content can be created and reviewed.
  • Approval status is visible.
  • Approved assets can be found and reused.
  • Metadata and permissions are applied consistently.
  • The process has an owner.
  • Baseline metrics can be reported.

Phase 2

Select and run a focused pilot

Objective: prove measurable value through one end-to-end workflow before expanding across the enterprise. A good pilot is high-volume, repetitive, easy to measure, important to the business, bounded to one team/market/campaign type, supported by mature brand guidelines, and connected to an activation channel — capable of producing results within one planning cycle.

Strong pilot examples

  • Paid social campaign variants — high variation, clear metrics
  • Regional campaign localization — measurable cycle time
  • Display advertising production — standard dimensions, high volume
  • Product-launch asset reuse — DAM findability → speed
  • Briefing and approval — visible bottlenecks, easy baseline
  • Seasonal refresh — clean before/after comparison

Poor first pilots

  • Every region and BU simultaneously
  • High-risk regulated content with undefined approvals
  • A campaign with no baseline performance data
  • A workflow whose ownership is disputed
  • A use case needing many unresolved integrations
  • A broad “transform everything” program
  • Agent autonomy before the process is standardized

Phase 4

Introduce AI-powered production

Objective: increase content velocity and variation while preserving human judgment and enterprise controls. Introduce AI where the process is already structured enough to evaluate its output.

  1. Assist individual creators

    Firefly and Creative Cloud for ideation, background generation, image extension, resizing, copy/visual variations, and first-draft exploration.

  2. Enable marketer self-service

    GenStudio for approved teams to find assets, use brand and campaign context, create channel-specific variants from templates, submit for review, and activate.

  3. Automate repetitive production

    Firefly Services for bulk resizing, localization, background replacement, format adaptation, product-image variants, channel production, and campaign refreshes.

  4. Evaluate specialized models

    Consider custom models or Foundry when the use case needs deep understanding of proprietary products, brand identity, specialized visual systems, franchise universes, or highly consistent multimodal output.

AI governance checklist

Before scaling AI-generated content, define:

  • Permitted and prohibited use cases
  • Approved models
  • Approved data and content sources
  • Human review requirements
  • Brand validation rules
  • Legal and rights checks
  • Recordkeeping requirements
  • Model evaluation criteria
  • Escalation procedures
  • Output monitoring
  • Rollback or withdrawal procedures

Control principle

AI may accelerate creation, but it should not silently redefine strategy, brand standards, approval authority, or accountability.

Phase 6

Progress toward agentic operations

Objective: use agents to coordinate bounded, repeatable workflows after the customer has reliable context, rules, and measurement. Treat agentic adoption as a separate readiness track — not an automatic consequence of buying GenStudio.

Good first agentic use cases

  • Route work based on campaign type, market, or priority.
  • Check whether a brief contains required information.
  • Identify missing metadata or rights information.
  • Recommend existing approved assets for reuse.
  • Generate standard channel variants.
  • Monitor approval bottlenecks.
  • Flag overdue tasks or blocked dependencies.
  • Assemble performance summaries.
  • Recommend campaign refreshes based on agreed rules.

Agentic readiness requirements

  • Structured campaign and asset context
  • Defined business rules
  • Clear human owners
  • Reliable metadata
  • Standard approval paths
  • Role-based permissions
  • Auditability
  • Exception handling
  • Data and security controls
  • Agent performance KPIs

Human–agent operating model

Human responsibilityAgent responsibility
Set strategy and objectivesInterpret structured goals
Define brand and risk boundariesExecute within those boundaries
Approve high-impact decisionsPerform routine steps
Handle exceptionsMonitor status and identify exceptions
Own accountabilityProvide traceability and recommendations
Change policies and prioritiesApply current rules consistently

Target state

Humans lead the intent, judgment, and accountability; agents accelerate and coordinate execution. The goal is not to remove humans from the process.

Executive governance

Stage gates

Each gate is a go/no-go decision point. Proceed only when its criteria are met.

1

Ready to design

  • Business outcomes agreed; executive sponsor active
  • Current state documented; pilot owner identified
  • Customer can provide process, architecture, and performance inputs
2

Ready to build

  • Target workflow agreed; systems of record defined
  • Metadata and approval requirements documented
  • Integration dependencies understood; customer/partner responsibilities assigned
3

Ready to pilot

  • Users provisioned; templates and brand rules configured
  • Test assets and briefs available; approval paths work
  • Activation and measurement instrumented; support/escalation exist
4

Ready to scale

  • Pilot meets operational and business KPIs; users follow the process
  • Governance exceptions manageable; adoption capacity exists
  • Next rollout wave has a named owner and funding
5

Ready for agentic expansion

  • Process is repeatable; required context is structured
  • Rules and permissions clear; human escalation defined
  • Agent performance measurable; consequential actions auditable and reversible

The recommended order

Product sequence

Workfront plans it. Creative Cloud creates it. AEM Assets governs it. GenStudio activates it. Analytics measures it. Firefly Services scales it. Agents coordinate it.

  1. Workfront & planning

    Establish demand, campaign, and workflow context.

  2. AEM Assets & metadata

    Establish trusted, findable, reusable content.

  3. Creative Cloud & review

    Connect professional production and approval.

  4. GenStudio

    Enable governed marketer self-service and activation.

  5. Firefly Services

    Automate high-volume variation and adaptation.

  6. Content Analytics

    Connect assets and content attributes to outcomes.

  7. Agent orchestration

    Coordinate repeatable work across the established foundation.

This prevents the common failure mode of treating GenStudio or generative AI as a standalone application instead of part of an integrated Content Supply Chain.

The short version

Executive summary

An enterprise should adopt the Content Supply Chain in this order:

  1. Align on business outcomes and establish sponsorship.
  2. Map the current content lifecycle and prioritize 3–5 use cases.
  3. Define the North Star architecture and operating model.
  4. Create a governed foundation for planning, production, review, and assets.
  5. Run one measurable end-to-end pilot.
  6. Scale through waves, not a single big-bang rollout.
  7. Add AI where it accelerates controlled, repeatable work.
  8. Connect content performance to future briefs and investment decisions.
  9. Introduce agents only when context, rules, permissions, and measurement are mature.

Definition of success

The transformation succeeds when the customer produces more useful, on-brand, measurable content with less friction and less manual coordination — not simply when more Adobe applications are active.
