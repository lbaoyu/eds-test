Verdict

Low near-term impact, a signal worth tracking

This is not evidence of marketing budget shifting from social algorithms to GEO. The more important implication: governments are starting to apply formal duties, risk management, and accountability to AI-mediated experiences.

Why

Two things to hold together

The weak part

The feed effect is indirect

Social feeds and generative engines serve different needs — turning off a personalized feed doesn’t send someone to an AI assistant. And any spend displaced from algorithmic reach has several destinations (paid, creators, CRM, search), not just GEO.

The consequential part

AI systems are now in scope

The Duty of Care extends beyond feeds to AI chatbots, and requires providers to document risk-mitigation measures and keep them effective. AI-generated experiences are entering formal risk-and-accountability frameworks.

Keep separate. AI adoption is the growth engine for GEO. Regulation is a force shaping what GEO becomes — not what is driving it today.

Implication

GEO broadens beyond optimization

If AI providers face more accountability, value tilts from “rank us higher” toward measuring and governing how AI represents a brand. The discipline splits into layers — worth separating rather than treating everything as “GEO”:

VisibilityWhere and how often does the organization appear?
IntelligenceWhy is it appearing? Which sources influence the answer?
OptimizationWhat content, entities, or data should change?
ActivationHow are those changes created, updated, and distributed?
GovernanceIs the AI representation accurate, current, substantiated, and appropriate?

Stronger AI governance could make generative systems harder to optimize even as it makes understanding and managing AI representation more important — tilting the market toward monitoring, measurement, and governance.

Bottom line

Bottom line

Net

Don’t characterize “My Feed, My Way” as a major tailwind for GEO. The consequential signal is that AI chatbots are beginning to fall within formal regulatory frameworks — which makes managing and measuring AI representation more important, even if it makes deliberate optimization harder. Treat Australia as a precedent to monitor, not a demand catalyst.
