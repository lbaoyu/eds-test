Your 60-day Brand Visibility pilot, step by step

Start here

Before you begin

Over the next 60 days you’ll run a focused Adobe Brand Visibility pilot — set a dated baseline, pick one high-value opportunity, ship one supported optimization, and measure it against agreed criteria. Your team owns the decisions and execution; Adobe supports the setup and method.

Use the model below to connect the pilot’s measurements — from AI-readable content to AI visibility, citations, traffic, and trust — to measurable business outcomes. You won’t optimize every layer in one pilot; use the baseline to identify the highest-value next change.

The pilot establishes the baseline, improves one high-value point in this chain, and measures the result against the starting position.

What you’ll walk away with

  • A dated baseline across an agreed prompt set, market, domains, and competitors.
  • One implemented optimization with a measured readout (where data allows).
  • A repeatable playbook — prompts, changes, owners, and results.
  • A Day-60 recommendation: scale, iterate, hold, or stop.

Assign an owner for each responsibility

One person can hold several; each deliverable and decision needs a named owner.

  • Pilot lead — runs the pilot and owns decisions.
  • SEO / marketing / content — journeys, prompts, content changes.
  • Web / development — source/CMS changes and technical review.
  • Analytics — baseline, KPI, and business impact.
  • CDN / security — log forwarding, crawler access, WAF, edge routing.
  • ABV administrator — access, configuration, and integrations.
  • Approver(s) — brand, legal, compliance, or technical sign-off.

Have these ready before Day 1

  • Access to Adobe Brand Visibility, with a user who can configure and, if applicable, deploy.
  • The brand, market, primary domain, and supporting URLs.
  • One or two priority journeys and their key products or services.
  • A shortlist of 3–5 competitors.
  • The data sources for your measurement plan:
    • Google Search Console setup — to ground prompts in real search behavior.
    • Adobe Analytics, CJA, or GA4 — for business-impact measurement.
    • CDN log forwarding — for agentic/referral traffic or edge optimization.
  • A technical owner to confirm crawler access and handle robots.txt, WAF, bot-management, or CDN changes.
  • An agreed primary KPI, supporting measures, baseline period, and Day-60 criteria.
  • Someone who can approve content and technical changes on cadence.

Step 1 · Days 1–5

1 Frame the pilot

Define a small, testable scope, name the intended outcome and KPI, and agree how you will decide on Day 60.

ABV features in use

Brands Management
Use the information gathered here to complete the applicable brand, market, domain, alias, competitor, analytics, and CDN configurations.
Capability check
With Adobe, confirm the capabilities, access, and integrations available for this pilot.

Do this

  1. Choose one brand, one market, and a defined domain scope; note any business-unit boundary.
  2. Pick one or two priority customer journeys.
  3. Write, in one sentence, the business outcome and pilot hypothesis.
  4. List priority domains and URLs, key products or services, and 3–5 competitors.
  5. Name owners for marketing/content, web, analytics, approvals, and — when in scope — CDN/security.
  6. Use the approved scope and data-source decisions to complete the applicable configurations in Brands Management.
  7. Start CDN log forwarding during Frame, and complete the pilot’s CDN targeting/configuration before Define.
  8. If Deploy at Edge is in scope, start Optimize at Edge onboarding and CDN routing.
  9. Confirm CDN logs are flowing.
  10. Agree your Day-60 decision criteria and set a weekly working rhythm.

You’ll need

Brand and market decision-makers, a competitor shortlist, access to the required ABV capabilities, and owners who can approve content or technical changes — plus CDN ownership and routing access when Deploy at Edge is in scope.

You’ll produce

An approved pilot charter, completed applicable Brands Management configurations, and CDN log forwarding started — with targeting/configuration ready before Define.

Who’s involved

Pilot lead, marketing/content, web and analytics owners, approver(s), Adobe, and — when relevant — CDN/security.

Ready for Discover when your team has approved

  • Pilot scope
  • Business objective and hypothesis
  • Ownership model
  • Required ABV capabilities and access
  • Applicable Brands Management configurations
  • Measurement approach
  • CDN log-forwarding owner and implementation plan

Before Define

  • CDN targeting/configuration is complete for the pilot scope
  • CDN logs are confirmed to be flowing
Tip
  • Keep it small — one brand, one market, and one or two journeys. A tight scope is what makes 60 days enough.

Step 2 · Days 6–14

2 Discover the opportunity

Build the Prompt Library from AI market signals, real search behavior, and citation activity. By the end of Discover, the tracking set should be ready for Define to lock.

ABV features in use

AI Visibility
Review the three Semrush-powered views:
  • Visibility Overview — identify visibility, mentions, cited pages, topics, and gaps.
  • Prompt Research — discover relevant prompts, questions, and topics.
  • Market Comparison — compare your brand with configured competitors and identify areas where they are stronger.
Prompt Strategy
Review and prioritize recommendations from:
  • Semrush Suggestions — identifies topics and competitor gaps.
  • Google Search Console — adds prompts from real search queries.
  • Synthetic Personas — creates prompts for target audience segments.
  • Citation Attempts — surfaces prompts from AI-agent citation opportunities.
Select recommendations that match the approved journeys, markets, topics, and intents.
Prompt Library
Add the selected prompts to the Prompt Library. Organize them by journey, topic, market, language, intent, and branded or unbranded coverage. Available in two versions:
  • Prompt Library (Trial)
  • Prompt Library (Paid)

Do this

  1. Review all three AI Visibility views and capture relevant topics, prompts, competitors, and cited sources.
  2. In Prompt Strategy, evaluate each recommendation source and remove duplicates or out-of-scope suggestions.
  3. Use Citation Attempt recommendations when applicable; confirm CDN log forwarding is flowing and complete the required CDN targeting before Define.
  4. Add selected prompts to the Prompt Library and check coverage, organization, status, and gaps.
  5. Confirm the tracking set is complete and ready for Define to lock.

You’ll need

The approved Brand Management configuration, Frame scope and journeys, configured competitors, GSC access where applicable, and flowing CDN logs when Citation Attempts are in scope.

You’ll produce

A reviewed and organized Prompt Library containing selected prompts, source rationale, journey and market coverage, and any remaining gaps.

Who’s involved

Pilot lead, SEO/content, marketing, Brand Visibility administrator, and — when applicable — GSC, analytics, and CDN/security owners.

Ready for Define when

  • AI Visibility and Prompt Strategy are reviewed
  • The Prompt Library is selected, deduplicated, and in scope
  • Coverage and tracking are in good shape
  • CDN logs are flowing and Optimize at Edge routing is configured and verified, when applicable
  • The library is ready to lock
Tip
  • Use AI Visibility to understand the market, Prompt Strategy to select what matters, and the Prompt Library to prepare the tracking set.

Step 3 · Days 15–22

3 Define & baseline

Lock the Prompt Library and capture a complete baseline benchmark to guide the next change.

ABV features in use

Prompt Library
Use the processed prompt set from Discover for baseline tracking. Available in two versions:
  • Prompt Library (Trial)
  • Prompt Library (Paid)
Brand Visibility Overview
Establish the overall baseline.
  • Brand Presence (Trial) — capture visibility, mentions, citations, sentiment, and competitor comparison.
  • Brand Presence (Paid) — capture Brand Visibility, mentions, citations, Source Visibility, Share of Voice, and sentiment.
Brand Claims
Identify how AI describes the brand and any perception gaps.
URL Inspector
Baseline citations, agentic hits, and referral hits for priority URLs.
Agentic Traffic
Baseline AI-agent requests and top URLs.
Referral Traffic
Baseline traffic from AI citations and related business impact.
Opportunities
Capture and rank opportunities for the next change.

Do this

  1. Confirm the processed Prompt Library from Discover and record its version and lock date.
  2. Use consistent markets, platforms, competitors, filters, and date ranges.
  3. Capture the applicable Trial or Paid Brand Presence baseline.
  4. Record Brand Claims, URL Inspector, Agentic Traffic, Referral Traffic, and Opportunities findings.
  5. Confirm CDN data is flowing for traffic views and record the baseline period.
  6. When Deploy at Edge is in scope, confirm that Optimize at Edge routing is configured and verified.
  7. Export key reports and dashboard data for the baseline record.
  8. Use the benchmark to select the next change, target URLs or prompts, KPI, and measurement period.

You’ll need

A processed Prompt Library, configured competitors, priority URLs, flowing CDN logs, and analytics access where required.

You’ll produce

A complete baseline benchmark, ranked opportunity set, and approved change brief.

Who’s involved

Pilot lead, SEO/content, analytics, web/product, CDN/security, and approver(s).

Ready to activate when

  • The Prompt Library is confirmed
  • The Brand Presence baseline is recorded
  • Claims, URL, traffic, and opportunity baselines are captured
  • The next change, KPI, and measurement period are approved
Tips
  • Lock the baseline before you change anything — it’s what makes your Day-60 result credible.
  • When helpful, export reports and dashboard data to use AI tools such as Claude or ChatGPT to generate clearer reporting; validate the output against the source data.

Step 4 · Days 23–50

4 Activate & measure

Implement one approved GEO change and measure its effect against the locked baseline.

ABV features in use

Opportunities
Prioritize and select the opportunity to implement this cycle.
Recover Content Visibility RCV
Finds content that AI agents cannot access or read.
Optimize at Edge
Delivers supported changes to AI agents through the CDN.
Opportunity Workspace
Groups related changes around one optimization goal, assigns ownership, and tracks progress and outcomes as one strategy.
Impact Measurement Engine
Measures eligible changes through baseline and post-deployment comparison.

GEO change types

  • On-page GEO — improve content clarity, structure, FAQs, naming, and prompt alignment.
  • Technical GEO — remove barriers that prevent AI agents from reading or interpreting content.
  • Offsite GEO — improve third-party content and distribution that can generate citations and referral traffic.

Recommended pilot path

Use Opportunity Workspace to group related changes that serve one optimization goal into a single strategy, keeping the target URLs, prompts, owners, progress, KPI, and outcome together.

When a content-visibility gap and the required CDN setup both exist, pair Recover Content Visibility with Optimize at Edge. RCV surfaces content hidden from AI agents; Optimize at Edge delivers the change to agentic traffic at the CDN layer only — no CMS changes, and no impact on human or SEO traffic — giving a clean treatment you can preview, validate, deploy quickly, and roll back. For eligible RCV deployments, the Impact Measurement Engine captures the baseline, measures a fixed period, and reports the before-and-after impact.

RCV is a recommended path, not a requirement. Implement any valid on-page, technical, or offsite GEO change once the conditions, owners, approvals, and measurement approach are in place. When several changes serve one goal, run them as a single strategy and read the result as that strategy’s combined impact.

Do this

  1. Confirm the selected change, target URLs, prompts, KPI, and measurement period.
  2. Create an Opportunity Workspace strategy for the selected optimization goal.
  3. Group related changes under the same strategy and document the target URLs, prompts, owners, KPI, and measurement period.
  4. For RCV, select high-impact URLs with sufficient agentic traffic and deploy through Optimize at Edge.
  5. Validate routing, preview the change, deploy it, and confirm the optimized experience is live for AI agents.
  6. For on-page, technical, or offsite GEO changes, implement the approved change through the responsible owner.
  7. Avoid unrelated changes to the selected URLs during the measurement period.
  8. Monitor citations, Content Visibility, agentic traffic, referral traffic, and the agreed guardrails.
  9. Compare results with the locked baseline and document the outcome.

You’ll need

A locked baseline, approved change, target URLs and prompts, an Opportunity Workspace strategy, responsible owners, and — when using RCV — completed CDN routing and Optimize at Edge readiness.

You’ll produce

One Opportunity Workspace strategy containing the deployed, measured, and documented GEO changes. Additional valid changes may be included when conditions permit and their combined impact can be measured clearly.

Who’s involved

Pilot lead, SEO/content, web or engineering, offsite content owners, analytics, CDN/security, and approver(s).

Ready to prove when

  • The change is deployed and validated
  • The measurement period is complete
  • Results are compared with the locked baseline
  • The outcome and learnings are documented
Tip
  • Keep the target URLs, Prompt Library, and measurement period stable. A controlled change makes the Day-60 result easier to interpret.

Step 5 · Days 51–60

5 Prove & decide

Compare the results with the locked baseline, evaluate the grouped strategy, and make the Day-60 decision.

Do this

  1. Build a before-and-after scorecard using the same Prompt Library, prompts, filters, markets, platforms, and date ranges.
  2. Compare the applicable Brand Presence (Trial) or Brand Presence (Paid) results.
  3. Recheck affected Brand Claims, priority URLs, citations, and traffic signals.
  4. Review the grouped strategy in Opportunity Workspace.
  5. Use the Impact Measurement Engine when the optimization is eligible; evaluate other changes against the locked baseline.
  6. Add business impact from Analytics, CJA, or GA4 when available.
  7. Document risks, limitations, dependencies, and anything not yet measurable.
  8. Assess whether the process and strategy can be repeated.
  9. Present one recommendation: scale, iterate, hold, or stop.

Your recommendation is one of four calls

Scale

The strategy improved the agreed measures and can be repeated or expanded.

Iterate

The result is promising, but the change, scope, or measurement approach needs refinement.

Hold

The result is inconclusive because more data, access, or time is needed.

Stop

The change did not produce meaningful improvement or is not the right fit now. Capture the learnings and close out.

You’ve finished when

  • You have a decision-ready recommendation supported by the scorecard, strategy outcome, business impact, limitations, and repeatability assessment
Tips
  • Be honest about limits. A well-reasoned iterate or hold is a successful pilot outcome, not a failure.
  • Export relevant data from reports and dashboards and use approved AI tools such as Claude or ChatGPT to help create readouts, summarize findings, and identify patterns. Validate the output against the source data and protect sensitive information.
