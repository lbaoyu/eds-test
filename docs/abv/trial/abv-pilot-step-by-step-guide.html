Start here

Before you begin

Over the next 60 days you’ll run one complete cycle of Adobe Brand Visibility — from understanding how your brand shows up in AI answers, to making one real improvement and proving its impact. Your team owns the work; Adobe supports the method and the tooling. Here’s how to get set up.

What you’ll walk away with

  • A defensible baseline of how your brand appears in AI answers.
  • One completed, measured optimization — proof the loop works.
  • A documented process your team can repeat.
  • A clear recommendation: scale, iterate, hold, or stop.

Name one person for each role

  • Pilot lead — runs the pilot and owns decisions.
  • Marketing / content — journeys, prompts, content changes.
  • Web / development — deploys changes at edge or source.
  • Analytics — baseline and business impact.
  • CDN / infrastructure — log forwarding and edge routing.
  • Approver(s) — brand, legal, or compliance sign-off.

Have these ready before Day 1

  • The brand, market, and priority domain(s) the pilot will cover.
  • A shortlist of competitors to benchmark against.
  • Access to Google Search Console and, if used, Adobe Analytics / CJA.
  • Your CDN details — start log forwarding early; it’s the most common cause of delay.
  • Someone who can approve content and technical changes quickly.

Step 1 · Days 1–5

1 Frame the pilot

Agree what you’re testing and how you’ll judge it — a small, clear scope and the criteria you’ll decide against on Day 60.

ABV features in use

Brands Management
Set up your brand, domains, and competitors — the account foundation for everything that follows.
Capability check
Confirm with Adobe which ABV features your entitlement includes; this shapes what the pilot can do.

Do this

  1. Choose one brand or business unit and one market for the pilot.
  2. Pick one or two priority customer journeys to focus on.
  3. Write, in a single sentence, the business outcome you want to influence.
  4. List your priority domains and URLs, key products/services, and 3–5 competitors.
  5. Name the owners for marketing, content, web, analytics, CDN, and approvals.
  6. With Adobe, confirm which ABV capabilities you have available.
  7. Choose your primary KPI and a few supporting measures.
  8. Agree your Day-60 decision criteria and set a weekly working rhythm.

You’ll need

Brand/market decision-makers, a competitor shortlist, and owners who can grant access.

You’ll produce

An approved pilot charter: scope, objective, owners, KPI, and decision criteria.

Who’s involved

Pilot lead and marketing, with Adobe.

Ready for Discover when your team has approved

  • Pilot scope
  • Business objective
  • Ownership model
  • Measurement approach
  • Day-60 criteria
Tip. Keep it small — one brand, one market, one or two journeys. A tight scope is what makes 60 days enough.

Step 2 · Days 6–14

2 Discover the opportunity

Understand the market and shortlist a few high-value opportunities — the places where you have the most to gain in AI answers.

ABV features in use

Visibility Overview
See your overall AI visibility and where you’re weak.
Prompt Research
Find the high-value questions and topics customers actually ask.
Market Comparison
Benchmark your presence against competitors.
External-source analysis where available
Review the web, community, and video sources AI cites.

Do this

  1. Review your overall AI visibility landscape in Visibility Overview.
  2. Pull the high-value questions and topics your customers ask, in Prompt Research.
  3. Compare your presence to competitors in Market Comparison.
  4. Note the topics where competitors are cited and you’re missing.
  5. Separate what you control (your own pages) from what depends on third parties.
  6. Where available, review the external sources AI cites (web, community, video).
  7. With Adobe, agree a short list of opportunities to carry forward — not everything you find.

You’ll need

The scope and competitors from Step 1, and time with your SEO/content owner.

You’ll produce

A market opportunity map and a shortlisted set of opportunities.

Who’s involved

Pilot lead and SEO/content, with Adobe.

Ready for Define when

  • You and Adobe agree the limited set of opportunities entering the pilot
Tip. Prioritize opportunities that have a clear owner and a measurable upside. Park the rest for later cycles.

Step 3 · Days 15–22

3 Define & baseline

Lock what you’ll measure, capture your starting point, and choose the one opportunity you’ll optimize first.

ABV features in use

Prompt Strategy & Prompt Library
Build, organize, and version the prompts you’ll monitor.
Brand Presence
Capture your baseline — visibility, mentions, citations, sentiment.
Brand Claims where available
Baseline the accuracy of what AI says about your brand.
Off-site Opportunities where available
Snapshot relevant external-source opportunities.

Do this

  1. Build and approve your Prompt Library, organized by journey, topic, and priority.
  2. Turn on Brand Presence monitoring and record your starting numbers — visibility, mentions, citations, sentiment.
  3. Where available, capture a Brand Claims baseline and note off-site opportunities.
  4. Where included, confirm referral and agentic traffic measurement is flowing.
  5. Choose the single opportunity for your mandatory optimization cycle.
  6. Write down the improvement you expect and exactly how you’ll measure it.
  7. Lock the baseline. No production changes happen before this point.

You’ll need

Sign-off from your approver(s); analytics and CDN connections if in scope.

You’ll produce

A versioned Prompt Library, an approved baseline, and a selected first opportunity.

Who’s involved

Pilot lead and content/product, with Adobe.

Ready to activate when you’ve approved

  • The prompt set
  • Monitoring scope
  • The baseline
  • Measurement method
  • The selected opportunity
Tip. Lock the baseline before you change anything — it’s what makes your Day-60 result credible.

Step 4 · Days 23–50

4 Activate & measure

Turn your chosen opportunity into a deployed, measured change. This step gets the most time on purpose — it’s where the pilot is won.

ABV features in use

Opportunity Workspace
Plan the opportunity, assign owners, and track it through to done.
Optimize at Edge & source
Deploy the fix to AI agents at the CDN edge, or at source for humans and SEO — reversible, no CMS release.
Impact Measurement
Measure the deployed change against your locked baseline.
Run one optimization cycleRequired
  1. Confirm the baseline for the pages and prompts in scope.
  2. Select the opportunity you chose in Define.
  3. Define the improvement you expect to see.
  4. Get the change approved by the right owner.
  5. Deploy it — through Optimize at Edge for AI agents, or at the source for humans and SEO.
  6. Validate and monitor — agents receive the fix, humans and SEO are unaffected, rollback is ready.
  7. Measure the impact against your locked baseline.
  8. Document it — what changed, who approved it, how you measured, and what moved.

Optional

Run a second cycle in parallel only if it won’t compromise the first. It’s bonus evidence, never required.

You’ll produce

One measured, documented optimization result.

Who’s involved

Web/dev and content, with your approver and Adobe.

Ready to prove when

  • One complete cycle is deployed
  • Measured against the baseline
  • Documented
Tip. One solid, measured cycle beats three half-finished ones. If a second cycle would stretch your team, use the time to make the first one airtight.

Step 5 · Days 51–60

5 Prove & decide

Show what changed against your baseline, judge how repeatable it was, and make the call on what to do next.

ABV features in use

Impact Measurement
Compare your cycle results against the baseline.
Brand Presence
Read your endline visibility, share of voice, citations, and sentiment.
Referral Traffic → Business impact where connected
Tie visibility gains to engagement, conversion, and revenue via Adobe Analytics / CJA.

Do this

  1. Build the before-and-after scorecard — visibility, share of voice, citation rate, claim accuracy, and traffic where available.
  2. Add business impact if Adobe Analytics or CJA is connected.
  3. Note risks, limitations, and anything you couldn’t measure yet.
  4. Judge how repeatable the process was for your team, and what Adobe support it needed.
  5. Write your recommendation: scale, iterate, hold, or stop.
  6. Present the readout to your decision-makers.

Your recommendation is one of four calls

Scale

The loop works and your team can repeat it — expand to more journeys.

Iterate

Promising, but run another cycle or refine before committing to scale.

Hold

Inconclusive — pause and revisit with more data, access, or time.

Stop

Not the right fit right now; capture the learnings and close out.

You’ve finished when

  • You have a decision-ready recommendation grounded in the scorecard and demonstrated repeatability
Tip. Be honest about limits. A well-reasoned “iterate” or “hold” is a successful pilot outcome, not a failure.
