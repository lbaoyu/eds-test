Decision rights

Separate accountability, execution, and participation

The model below assigns decision accountability, day-to-day responsibility, and business participation separately. These are functional responsibilities; an Admin Console profile does not automatically make someone the owner of a governance decision.

Accountable ownerDecidesOwns the outcome and the policy decision for the area.
Day-to-dayOperatesRuns the routine work, checks, and exception handling.
AEM AdministratorImplementsConfigures approved changes; does not decide business meaning alone.
Lifecycle decision to make explicit

AEM can show an asset's expiry status, but AMP should define what happens next—such as review, renewal, withdrawal from distribution, or archiving. Do not assume that adding an expiry date alone completes every downstream action.

Area 01

DAM strategy and governance

Accountable owner

DAM Product Owner

Day-to-day responsibility

Convene a small cross-functional governance group, set priorities, resolve policy decisions, and ensure standards are reviewed.

Practical AMP example

The Product Owner decides whether a proposed field or approval step supports AMP's business need and belongs in the DAM standard.

Area 02

Metadata standards

Accountable owner

DAM Product Owner for policyDAM Librarian / DAM Operations Lead for stewardship

Day-to-day responsibility

Maintain the field dictionary: definitions, required or optional status, approved values, owners, and how each field supports search, rights, workflow, or reporting. The AEM Administrator configures approved changes.

Practical AMP example

A campaign image might require Title, Campaign, Asset owner, Market, Usage rights, and Expiry date when applicable. The metadata steward documents what each field means and when it is required.

Area 03

Taxonomy and tags

Accountable owner

DAM Product OwnerWith Marketing, Creative, Legal, and other subject-matter experts

Day-to-day responsibility

The taxonomy steward, typically in DAM Operations, maintains the approved vocabulary, reviews requests, and merges or retires redundant terms. The AEM Administrator implements approved changes.

Practical AMP example

Contributors use an approved market tag rather than creating variants such as “US,” “USA,” and “United States” for the same value.

Area 04

Asset quality and approval

Accountable owner

Business asset owner for accuracyDesignated Brand / Legal / Compliance approver for required sign-off

Day-to-day responsibility

DAM Operations checks intake quality, metadata completeness, organization, duplicates, and workflow status. The designated reviewer checks content against applicable brand, legal, or other requirements.

Practical AMP example

Before broad availability, the creator confirms the correct version and information; the assigned reviewer confirms approval for the intended use.

Area 05

Rights and lifecycle

Accountable owner

Business asset owner for continued validityDAM Product Owner for AMP's lifecycle policy

Day-to-day responsibility

DAM Operations monitors ownership, rights, status, and expiry, then routes exceptions to the owner. The AEM Administrator configures supporting fields, workflows, or notifications as needed.

Practical AMP example

If an image license is nearing expiry, the owner decides whether to renew, replace, or retire it; Operations follows AMP's process to update status and availability.

Area 06

Platform, configuration, and access

Accountable owner

AEM AdministratorTechnical owner

Day-to-day responsibility

Implement approved schemas, workflows, permissions, and integrations; maintain technical documentation and support reporting.

Practical AMP example

An Administrator implements an approved Campaign field or workflow; they do not decide the field's business meaning alone.

Adobe guidance: Executive Guide to Success with Experience Manager Assets and Building your Adobe Experience Manager Assets Team recommend staffing Product Owner and DAM Librarian functions. The Product Owner aligns the DAM to business goals; the Librarian supports the metadata, taxonomy, ingestion, and workflows needed to operate the library. Adobe also distinguishes this governance work from the creative and business users who create, review, approve, or use assets.

Who does the work

Hand off cleanly between operations and users

DAM operations maintains the shared rules and routine controls. Marketing and Creative users own the accuracy of what they contribute.

DAM / content operations

Maintain shared rules and routine controls

  • Own the metadata dictionary, schemas, taxonomy, folder and naming conventions, and change-request process.
  • Define ingestion checks and help ensure assets are organized and described consistently.
  • Review exceptions such as missing metadata, duplicate files, unclear ownership, or incomplete rights information.
  • Coordinate approval and lifecycle processes, escalating content and rights decisions to the appropriate business owner.
  • Provide role-appropriate guidance and training for operators, contributors, reviewers, and asset consumers.
Marketing and Creative users

Own the accuracy of contributions

  • Submit assets through the agreed intake process and location.
  • Complete required fields and apply approved tags.
  • Provide accurate campaign, source, owner, and usage information when known.
  • Review or approve assets only when assigned that responsibility.
  • Request new shared fields or tags through the governance process rather than creating new standards independently.
Worked example

Creative brings a batch of assets from SharePoint into AEM

  1. Submitting teamIdentifies the source and business owner; provides available campaign and rights information.
  2. DAM OperationsMaps legacy values to AMP's approved metadata and taxonomy; identifies duplicates and assets with unclear rights.
  3. Business ownerReceives routed exceptions and resolves them before assets are made available for wider reuse.

Adobe guidance: Metadata best practices for AEM Assets and Metadata management and best practices recommend deciding which metadata fields are required, using controlled vocabularies, and scheduling metadata reviews.

Operating controls

Build governance into the asset journey

Define the steps from intake to retirement, who approves each asset type, and what makes an asset ready for broader use.

  1. IntakeSubmitting team uses the agreed location and process.
  2. Metadata and quality checkDAM Operations checks completeness, organization, and duplicates.
  3. ReviewDesignated reviewer checks brand, legal, or other requirements.
  4. ApprovalApprover signs off; the approved latest version becomes available.
  5. Reuse / distributionTeams find and use approved assets.
  6. Expiry or retirementOwner decides; Operations updates status and availability.

Adobe describes approval as a governance checkpoint that helps make the approved latest version available for use.

01

Give a governance group decision rights

Include the DAM Product Owner, DAM Librarian / Operations, AEM Administrator, and relevant Marketing, Creative, and Legal / Compliance representatives. Define which changes need group approval: new metadata fields, shared taxonomy terms, lifecycle policy, or folder and access changes.

02

Start with useful minimum metadata

Identify a small set of required fields by asset type or use case. For a rights-controlled campaign asset, that might include owner, intended market or channel, usage rights, and expiry where applicable. Avoid mandatory fields that users cannot reliably supply or that no AMP process uses.

03

Keep taxonomy controlled and understandable

Name one steward to review additions and clean up duplicate or obsolete terms. Users can request a new term, but should not independently expand the shared vocabulary.

04

Document the approval gate

Clarify who approves each asset type and what “ready for broader use” means, so approval is a consistent checkpoint rather than an informal handoff.

05

Define expiry, archive, and deletion for AMP

AEM supports an expiry date and displays an expired status; AMP should define and test the operational steps for withdrawal, archiving, or other downstream handling. Specify:

  • Who reviews an expiring asset?
  • How is renewal or replacement recorded?
  • When should an asset stop being available for use?
  • What must AMP retain?

Adobe guidance: Taxonomy and tagging best practices for AEM Assets; Approve assets in Experience Manager; and Digital Rights Management in Assets.

Keep it clean

Review on a light rhythm and act on exceptions

As a starting point—not a product requirement—DAM Operations could use the cadence below. Adjust it to AMP's volume and risk.

Weekly

New-upload exceptions

Catch missing metadata, duplicates, and unclear ownership early.

Monthly

Rights and expiry items

Route upcoming and overdue items to the business owner.

Quarterly

Standards, taxonomy, access groups

Review the metadata dictionary, vocabulary, and group membership.

Starting cadence only; Adobe does not prescribe these intervals as a product requirement.

Measure quality

Useful measures to track

Assign a person and next action to each exception.

  • Required-metadata completeness
  • Assets with no identified owner
  • Rights or expiry gaps
  • Duplicates
  • Assets awaiting review

Reporting without follow-up will not keep the DAM clean. Every exception needs an owner and a next step.

Adobe guidance: Governance best practices for AEM Assets emphasizes setting clear goals and standards, documenting workflows, training users, and continually monitoring and optimizing the DAM.

Access follows accountability

Governance model

Admin Console profiles

Grant only the access needed

Profiles, AEM permissions, and group membership give people the access to perform the assigned work.

Role-profile note

A Power User can be appointed as AMP's DAM Librarian or taxonomy steward, but the profile itself does not assign that accountability. The AEM Administrator implements approved technical changes.
