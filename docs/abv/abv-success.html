The operating model

One connected programme, two complementary engines

Adobe Brand Visibility treats AI visibility as a closed loop: Discover → Prioritise → Optimise → Deploy → Measure. Semrush AIO powers the front of that loop as the discovery and intelligence layer — surfacing which prompts matter, where competitors outperform, and where the market opportunity sits. Adobe LLM Optimizer powers the middle and back — deciding what to change, pushing GEO improvements live, and connecting those changes to downstream traffic and business outcomes. Success is the loop running, not any single number moving.

Prompt strategy

Why prompt tracking alone is not enough

Most teams begin by selecting prompts on internal judgement. That is a sensible start — but it is a hypothesis, not a final answer. No single source reveals every prompt consumers ask across LLMs, so prompt strategy cannot rest on intuition. It has to be refined against market signal and competitive intelligence. The right question is not whether the initial set is perfectly representative; it is whether it captures the most commercially relevant prompts to win right now.

A strong prompt library draws on four inputs

01

Market demand

Which topics and prompts genuinely matter in the category.

02

Competitive gaps

Where rivals show up in AI answers and the brand does not.

03

Existing strengths

Prompts where the brand already performs — worth defending.

04

Business value

Prompts tied to acquisition journeys, not broad awareness alone.

Not all prompts deserve equal attention — tier them

Tier 1 · High-intent
Directly tied to acquisition. The prompts a gain matters most on.
Tier 2 · Competitor-gap
Strategically important prompts where rivals currently lead.
Tier 3 · Exploratory
Sensing prompts to detect emerging opportunity — cheap to hold, quick to drop.

Over time, low-demand, low-significance, low-value prompts get deprioritised and replaced. Prompt strategy is dynamic — refreshed as AI-search behaviour changes, not set once.

From insight to action

The step most programmes skip: deployment

The common failure in AI-visibility work is stopping at diagnosis — identifying the prompts that matter, monitoring mentions and citations, but never building an operating model to act. That is exactly where programmes stall. Once priority prompts and journeys are known, the essential next move is to deploy GEO optimisation changes. This is where insight turns into potential business impact.

What the changes look like

Prescriptive improvements on the pages and journeys tied to target prompts:

  • Clearer page structure and answer-oriented content
  • Citation-ready summaries and stronger FAQ patterns
  • Improved technical accessibility for AI crawlers
  • Tighter alignment between prompt intent and page content
  • Off-site presence where third-party signals drive citation
  • Remediation of issues that make priority pages hard to discover or cite

How it goes live

Adobe LLM Optimizer's differentiator is that recommendations don't sit in a report. Changes deploy at the CDN edge or push into AEM / source workflows through a one-click-style optimisation path — so the fix lands on the live experience tied to the prompt, fast.

Without the deploy step, you are measuring a problem. With it, you start to manage performance.

Reading the numbers

How to know an uplift is actually meaningful

If citation score moves from 80% to 90%, that is positive — but no universal threshold tells you whether the gain is commercially important. A flat "+10 points is good" is too blunt: industries, prompt sets, and competitive conditions differ too much. Assess against three benchmarks at once.

1

Historical baseline

Has the brand improved versus its own starting point, on the same prompt set?

2

Competitive benchmark

Has the brand closed the gap — or widened the lead — versus relevant rivals?

3

Business-priority

Did the gain land on the prompts that matter most commercially?

A citation gain is most meaningful when it happens on high-intent prompts, is sustained across measurement cycles, and improves position relative to competitors. Treat generic cross-industry benchmarks as a secondary reference — own baseline first, competitors second, priority prompt clusters third.

The metrics landscape

The metrics that matter — from AI-readable content to growth

Brand-visibility metrics form a hierarchy, not a flat dashboard. It starts with a foundation most programmes never check — whether AI systems can even access your content — and builds up through visibility, accuracy, citations, and referral traffic to the only tier the business ultimately cares about: outcomes. Each layer is a precondition for the one above it.

Confidence without a lab

Attribution without a perfect A/B test

GEO work is rarely a classical A/B test, so it is fair to ask how confidently improvement can be attributed to the programme rather than seasonality or external shifts. The honest answer: GEO measurement gives directional and increasingly strong evidence, not perfect experimental proof — and there are concrete ways to strengthen it.

Confidence rises when these align

  • The same fixed prompt set is measured before and after deployment
  • GEO changes are actually live on the relevant experience
  • The improvement is sustained across repeated measurements
  • Better visibility is followed by traffic, engagement and conversion gains

The method re-runs the same prompts before and after — which strips out far more seasonality noise than a raw month-over-month traffic comparison.

Want stronger attribution? Use a holdout

Optimise and deploy one cluster of priority pages first; leave a comparable cluster untouched for a period. The delta between the two groups materially strengthens the case for incremental impact — short of a lab test, it is the most defensible design available.

A signal others can't see

Adobe LLM Optimizer reads agentic traffic from server-side / CDN signals — real AI-agent activity on owned properties, not just inferred market estimates. That gives attribution a data source most GEO tooling simply doesn't have.

The defensible claim is rarely that the programme caused a precise number of acquisitions. An important caveat

Not every interaction ends in a click

AI discovery differs from traditional search: an LLM can shape a customer's consideration, shortlist, or preference before they ever visit the site. Some GEO value is therefore zero-click, and will not always appear in referral-traffic metrics. The most realistic view of impact blends two kinds of evidence.

Direct evidence

  • AI-referred traffic to priority pages
  • On-site conversion from AI sources
  • Agentic traffic captured server-side

Supporting evidence

  • Improved visibility and citations
  • Stronger competitive position
  • Better performance on high-intent prompts after deployment

Read together, these give the most accurate reflection of how AI-driven discovery influences business outcomes.
