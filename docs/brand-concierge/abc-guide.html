The concept

What is Adobe Brand Concierge?

Adobe Brand Concierge is a conversational experience that helps visitors explore a brand's information through natural-language questions. Instead of relying only on website navigation or search results, visitors can ask questions, receive relevant responses, and continue exploring through follow-up questions.

Depending on its configuration, a concierge may support activities such as answering questions, helping visitors discover or compare products, and offering a next step such as contacting a person.

Brand Concierge is not simply a generic chatbot or an automatic replacement for website search, support, or sales teams. Its usefulness depends on the visitor need, the content and integrations available to it, and how well the experience is designed and evaluated.

A demo can illustrate what a configured experience may look like. It does not mean the same content, integrations, or results will be available in every organization's setup.

The building blocks

How it works

A Brand Concierge experience brings together four elements:

  • Knowledge sources provide information the concierge can use, such as approved website or product content. The quality, accuracy, and coverage of this information affect the experience.
  • Skills define tasks the concierge is configured to perform, such as answering general questions or helping visitors explore products.
  • Integrations connect a skill to information or services. Depending on the setup, these may include a website, product catalog, live chat, or meeting-booking service.
  • Brand expression and guardrails help shape how the concierge communicates, what it should handle, and how it should respond when it cannot provide a reliable answer.

A typical process is:

  1. A team selects a visitor need to address.
  2. It identifies suitable knowledge sources and any required skills or integrations.
  3. It reviews the experience and tests it with representative questions.
  4. It addresses content, configuration, or integration issues.
  5. It coordinates deployment with the relevant website or technical team.
  6. It monitors the experience and updates it as content and visitor needs change.

Creating or previewing a concierge is not the same as deploying it to a live website. Deployment and any optional integrations require planning and configuration.

Fictional product facts, not Adobe offerings. Monthly billing, before tax. In this scenario, sales chat is unavailable and meeting booking is enabled.

Visitor · Question 1

We have eight people and need to manage projects together. Which plan should we choose?

Concierge · Answer 1

Both Standard and Premium support shared projects, task assignments, and dashboards. Do you also need audit-log exports or priority support?

Why this works: Clarifies the requirement before recommending a plan.
Visitor · Question 2

No, we only need shared projects and task assignments. What would Standard cost for eight people?

Concierge · Answer 2

Standard fits those requirements. At S$19 per user per month, eight users cost S$152 per month, billed monthly before tax. It also includes dashboards.

Visitor · Question 3

What would we get with Premium, and how much more would it cost?

Concierge · Answer 3

Premium adds audit-log exports and priority support. It costs S$29 per user per month, so eight users cost S$232 per month before tax—S$80 more than Standard.

Why this works: Connects the additional features to the total cost and price difference.
Visitor · Question 4

Can you guarantee a 10% discount if we sign up today?

Concierge · Answer 4

I can’t confirm or promise a 10% discount. The plan information available to me doesn’t include that offer. Would you like to discuss pricing with our sales team?

Why this works: Acknowledges the missing offer instead of making an unsupported commitment.
Visitor · Question 5

Yes. Can I speak with someone now?

Concierge · Answer 5

No sales representative is available for live chat right now. Would you like to view available consultation times and book a 30-minute meeting instead?

Why this works: Offers the configured booking fallback. No meeting is confirmed until the visitor selects a valid slot and completes the booking.
Illustrative dialogue, not recorded product output. In a real deployment, validate the content, price calculation, skill routing, representative availability, and booking fallback.

Start focused

Plan the experience around a clear use case

Before configuring an experience, define:

  • Visitor: Who is the intended audience?
  • Need: What are they trying to find, understand, or decide?
  • Current friction: Where does the existing website, search, or chat experience fall short?
  • Helpful content: What approved information would support the visitor?
  • Boundaries: What should the concierge handle, defer, decline, or route to a person?
  • Outcome: What would indicate that the visitor was better served?

Begin with one focused journey. A narrow use case is easier to prepare and evaluate than a broad goal such as answering every question on a website.

Example use-case brief · Fictional B2B site

Help a small team choose its next step

Audience
Business buyers comparing two service plans.
Friction
Features are spread across several pages.
Content
Approved plan details, comparisons, and FAQs.
Boundary
No unapproved discounts or contractual commitments.
Outcome
Complete a plan-selection task or book an appropriate consultation—not simply start a conversation.

Content operations

Prepare and maintain the content

The concierge's usefulness depends on the information it can access. Before testing, identify the sources it should use and check that the content is:

  • Approved for the intended use.
  • Accurate and current.
  • Relevant to the visitor journey.
  • Clear enough to support the questions visitors are likely to ask.
  • Owned by someone who can maintain it.

Pay particular attention to information that changes frequently, such as product details, policies, pricing, availability, or links.

Content work continues after launch. Assign an owner, choose a review cadence, and define what should trigger an update. For example, a policy change or a newly launched product may require content review and retesting. Visitor questions and feedback can also reveal missing or unclear information.

Brand and trust

Configure the experience and its boundaries

Decide how the experience should communicate and what it should do when it reaches a limit. Consider:

  • The desired brand voice and response style.
  • Topics it is intended to handle.
  • Topics it should defer or decline.
  • How it should respond when a question is unclear or the required information is missing.
  • When it should offer a human handoff or another next step.

Review responses against the organization's actual brand and business requirements. Involve appropriate brand, privacy, legal, and compliance reviewers where needed. A product configuration does not replace an organization's own review of its policies and obligations.

Example instruction · Review before applying

Make the limit useful

“Explain plan differences using approved information. Ask a clarifying question when the visitor’s needs are unclear. Do not promise custom pricing or legal terms. If a requested detail is missing, say so and offer the configured consultation route.”

Quality before scale

Test realistic questions and edge cases

A polished demonstration shows only one path. Before deployment, test questions that reflect how visitors may actually behave, including questions that are unclear, incomplete, unsupported, or sensitive.

A useful evaluation set includes:

Test typeExampleWhat to review
Common questionA visitor asks about a product or service.Is the response relevant and supported by approved information?
Ambiguous request"Which option is best for me?"Does the experience ask for useful clarification rather than making unsupported assumptions?
Missing informationA visitor asks for a detail not covered by the content.Does the experience acknowledge the gap appropriately?
Conflicting informationTwo sources appear to provide different details.Does the response avoid presenting uncertain information as fact, and does the team address the content issue?
Out-of-scope questionA visitor asks about an unrelated topic.Does the experience defer or redirect appropriately?
Sensitive or adversarial inputA visitor requests restricted information or tries to bypass the experience's boundaries.Does the response stay within the organization's approved safeguards?
Handoff scenarioA visitor asks to speak to a person.Does the route work, and is there a clear fallback if nobody is available?

Review test results rather than relying on an automated score alone. Add questions learned from real visitor interactions and retest after meaningful changes to content, instructions, guardrails, skills, or integrations.

Inspection cards

Turn a test result into a decision

Expand each lens. Use the visitor scenario, expected behavior, actual response, and corrective action as your review record.

01Functional: can it help with the intended task?+

Try a typical visitor question and an ambiguous version of the same question. Check whether the answer is useful, supported, and clear about the next step.

Expected

Relevant answer or useful clarification.

Action if weak

Review content coverage, instructions, and configured skills; retest.

02Out of scope: can it recognize a limit?+

Ask a harmless question outside the intended scope, such as an unrelated travel recommendation. Look for a clear limit or suitable redirection. A missing detail about an otherwise supported product is a content-gap test, not automatically an out-of-scope question.

Expected

Appropriate deferral or redirection.

Action if weak

Clarify scope and instructions; distinguish missing content from intentionally unsupported topics.

03Safeguard: does it respect approved boundaries?+

Test requests for restricted information or attempts to bypass the intended rules. Review responses with the relevant brand, privacy, legal, or compliance owners.

Expected

Behavior consistent with approved safeguards.

Action if weak

Resolve the issue before broader deployment and rerun the affected and adjacent cases.

The human connection

Plan human handoffs

A handoff needs an operational plan as well as a configured connection. Decide:

  • What visitor needs should lead to a human option.
  • Which team or representative receives the request.
  • When that team is available.
  • What happens if nobody is available or the connection fails.
  • What information the receiving team needs to continue the conversation.
  • What alternative the visitor can use if they do not complete the handoff.

Test the full visitor journey, including availability and fallback behavior. Do not assume that a handoff shown in a demonstration is enabled in another configuration.

From preview to production

Understand implementation effort

The work involved depends on the chosen use case, content readiness, integrations, approval needs, and website environment. Planning may include:

  • Defining the visitor journey and success criteria.
  • Preparing and reviewing content sources.
  • Configuring the concierge's experience, skills, and guardrails.
  • Testing expected questions and edge cases.
  • Confirming technical dependencies and any optional integrations.
  • Coordinating deployment with website or IT teams.
  • Establishing ongoing content and performance reviews.

An initial preview is only one step; it does not include all the work required for a production deployment. Confirm scope, owners, dependencies, and timeline with the teams responsible for the organization's configuration.

Deployment planning

Bring the website team in early

Adobe's deployment guidance separates marketer configuration from website installation. Coordinate the datastream ID with IT or analytics, identify the site or tag-manager owner, and agree on target domains, page paths, and installation approach.

Preview≠Production

A datastream identifies where visitor interaction activity is sent within Adobe systems. Confirm technical requirements and optional integrations with the responsible teams.

Example deployment acceptance test

Include product pages. Exclude careers.

After installing the component, configure a path rule for the intended product area—for example, paths starting with /products/. Verify an included product page, an excluded careers page, and visitor activity reaching the configured datastream. A working preview is not evidence that these deployment checks passed.

Evidence of value

Measure performance against existing search or chat

Before launch, document how visitors use the current experience. Decide how you will compare Brand Concierge with the existing search, chat, or website journey.

Agree on:

  • Baseline: What happens today for the selected visitor journey?
  • Primary outcome: What visitor or business result matters most for this use case?
  • Supporting measures: What additional signals will help explain the result?
  • Guardrails: What would indicate that the experience is creating friction?
  • Comparison method: How will comparable visitors or journeys be assessed?
  • Owner and review date: Who will review the results, and when?

Potential measures include:

MeasureWhat it can help you understand
Task completionWhether visitors complete the activity the use case is intended to support.
Search effectivenessWhether visitors find what they need through the current search journey.
Concierge engagementWhether visitors choose to use the conversational experience. Engagement alone does not prove value.
Visitor feedbackHow visitors rate responses, alongside how many chose to provide feedback.
Handoff outcomeWhether visitors successfully reach the intended team or next step.
Conversion or lead outcomeWhether the experience is associated with a relevant business outcome, if it can be measured reliably.
Guardrail measuresWhether unresolved questions, failed handoffs, or other signs of friction increase.

Report the audience, time period, and number of visitors or interactions behind each measure. Where practical, compare similar visitor groups or journeys. If using a before-and-after comparison, account for other factors that may affect results, such as traffic changes, seasonality, promotions, or content updates.

Engagement is not the same as lift. A credible assessment requires a defined baseline and a comparison method appropriate to the use case.

An operating rhythm

Keep improving after launch

Once the experience is in use, review performance and visitor feedback regularly. Look for patterns such as repeated unanswered questions, confusing responses, stale information, or unsuccessful handoffs.

Use a simple improvement cycle:

  1. Review performance signals, feedback, and representative interactions.
  2. Identify whether an issue relates to content, configuration, integration, or the visitor journey.
  3. Assign an owner and make a targeted change.
  4. Retest the affected questions and relevant safeguards.
  5. Compare results with the agreed baseline and guardrails.
