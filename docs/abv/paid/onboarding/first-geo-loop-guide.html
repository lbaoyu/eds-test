Start here

One strategy. A complete optimization cycle.

This onboarding activity is complete when your team has implemented an approved initiative, reviewed the available evidence, and recorded a decision about what happens next.

An ABV Opportunity Workspace Strategy is the initiative-level record for a defined optimization goal. It brings related opportunities, scope, ownership, implementation progress, and outcomes together. It is not the same as Prompt Strategy, a prompt list, or a single content edit.

Plan and coordinate

Opportunity Workspace

The parent initiative: objective, related automatically surfaced or manually created opportunities, owners, status, and supported outcomes.

Discover and select

Prompt Strategy

Review suggested conversations and choose which prompts should be monitored.

Monitor over time

Prompt Library

Your curated ongoing tracking set, used to measure Brand Presence.

A deployed change is an action.
A completed strategy connects that action to evidence and a next decision.

Completion is not a promise of uplift

A supported scale, iterate, hold, or stop decision is a valid outcome. If the evidence is insufficient, say so, keep measurement-dependent items open, and assign a follow-up date. Do not treat a deployed optimization or a UI status alone as completion of this activity.

Set your working rhythm

Use a weekly working session to review blockers, implementation, and evidence. Agree the baseline and follow-up periods before deployment. This is paid-customer onboarding, not a fixed 60-day pilot: timing depends on approval cycles, deployment route, and data sufficiency. RCV experiments typically take about two weeks; that is not a guaranteed completion time for every strategy.

Frame the strategy

Define the goal before choosing prompts

Choose a business-relevant question your team can improve, not a broad ambition to “win in AI.” Your scope will guide prompt selection, opportunity prioritization, and measurement.

Lead: Strategy ownerSupport: SEO / content + analyticsOutput: Agreed scope
  1. Choose one priority journey and a specific gap. For example: AI answers fail to describe a supported product capability, rarely cite a priority page, or cannot read essential content. Connect the gap to an audience, use case, and business objective.
  2. Write an objective and a testable hypothesis. State what you intend to improve and why the proposed work could help. Separate the hypothesis from a result: “clearer capability content may improve answer representation” is not proof that it will.
  3. Set the strategy boundary. Record brand, domain, target URL or edge-deployment batch, topic, audience, market-language combination, platform, and exclusions. For competitive work, record the compared brands and keep them consistent.
  4. Choose execution and measurement separately. Use the route table above. Select the opportunity type, edge or owner-managed execution, and manual or supported automatic evaluation. Choose a primary outcome, supporting diagnostics, data requirements, and guardrails. Agree what would justify scale, iterate, hold, or stop; do not invent universal uplift thresholds.
  5. Name the accountable owner and reviewers. One person can hold several roles, but every action and decision needs an owner. Agree who approves content, technical changes, and the final interpretation.
  6. Choose the correct parent record. Reuse an existing Workspace strategy when its objective and scope match. Otherwise use the strategy-creation flow once you have valid details and any required opportunities; these may be existing or newly created manual opportunities. If an empty record is not allowed, maintain the brief in the companion worksheet until it can be created. If the flow establishes a baseline, confirm the intended measurement scope is ready before proceeding.

Worked example · Fictional, not a benchmark

Northstar Workforce: Improve how AI answers describe shift scheduling for distributed US retail teams. Scope: https://northstar.example/products/workforce/scheduling, shift scheduling, US-English, and ChatGPT if supported in this account. Hypothesis: clearer capability information, relevant FAQs, and improved page structure may help answers represent the product accurately and cite the source page. Exclude payroll, non-US markets, and other workforce products.

Grouping rule: Keep related actions together when they support the same objective and measured scope. Split strategies when the target URL, topic, platform, or measurement objective materially differs, or when changes need independent evaluation. Multiple actions under one strategy do not establish which action caused a result.

Keep as evidence

A dated brief, scope and exclusions, hypothesis, chosen route, proposed metrics, agreed decision criteria, strategy owner, approver, and review cadence.

Move on when

The strategy objective and scope are defined before prompt sourcing, with an owner, measurement route, and agreed decision criteria.

Curate the tracked cohort

Select conversations that match the strategy

Use discovery signals to choose relevant conversations, then add the selected prompts to the paid Prompt Library. A discovery suggestion is not automatically part of ongoing measurement.

Lead: SEO / contentSupport: Strategy ownerOutput: Tracked cohort
  1. Review market intelligence through the strategy lens. In AI Visibility, use Visibility Overview, Prompt Research, and Market Comparison where available to identify relevant topics, cited sources, and competitive gaps. Keep discovery findings separate from the strategy's tracked-prompt results.
  2. Review Prompt Strategy suggestions by source. Use Semrush Suggestions for search/competitive inputs, Google Search Console for search behavior, Synthetic Personas for audience-led questions, and Citation Attempt for citation-related signals where configured. Record why a selected prompt belongs in this strategy.
  3. Select a focused, balanced set. Deduplicate exact duplicates and near-identical intent; exclude out-of-market and unrelated products. Include relevant discovery, evaluation, or comparison intents and an appropriate branded/unbranded mix. There is no universal prompt count or mix for every customer.
  4. Add selected prompts to the paid Prompt Library. In Prompt Strategy, review the recommendation, choose the relevant prompts, select Track, and confirm. Use the library's supported add/import flow for approved custom prompts. Paid prompts appear in a flat list organized by Category, Subcategory, Market, and Language; do not use a trial-only topic-nesting procedure.
  5. Verify processing and measurement availability. Newly added prompts can take up to 24 hours to process. Confirm they appear in the intended library and contribute to the applicable Brand Presence scope before locking a baseline. Review branded/unbranded balance, intent coverage, and Agentic URL coverage where available.
  6. Record the core cohort and version. Save prompt IDs where available, exact wording, category/subcategory, intent, branded status, market, language, source/rationale, selected platform filters, and a version/date. Label exploratory additions separately so you can keep the original comparison stable.

Keep as evidence

A prompt export or exact-wording register, cohort version and lock date, sourcing rationale, exclusions, processing check, and selected Brand Presence filters. Record any supported experiment's generated prompts separately when available.

Move on when

Strategy-aligned prompts are processed and recorded as a versioned ongoing cohort; any experiment-specific prompt set is distinguished from that cohort.

Establish the starting position

Capture the baseline before related changes

A defensible baseline records not only the number, but the scope, date range, metric definition, coverage, and evidence behind it.

Lead: Analytics / measurement reviewerSupport: SEO + strategy ownerOutput: Starting evidence
  1. Record the full measurement configuration. Use the correct brand, exact cohort/category/subcategory selection, market-language, platform or platform aggregate, domains, comparison brands, and date range. Verify the view can isolate the intended cohort; if it cannot, record the broader scope and do not label an account-wide number a strategy-specific result.
  2. Capture paid Brand Presence results. Record Brand Visibility and Source Visibility as displayed, including units and the relevant scope. Add Share of Voice only when competitive visibility is part of the objective and settings are stable.
  3. Capture evidence for the actual gap. Review prompt-level answers and cited sources where available. For accuracy/perception work, save Brand Claims examples and the approved facts they should reflect. For readability work, save URL Inspector or RCV evidence of what agents cannot access.
  4. Add only relevant supporting signals. Record agentic hits, referrals, or analytics outcomes when the corresponding connections and dates are valid. Separate requests, visits, mentions, citations, and conversions: they represent different events, not one interchangeable measure of success.
  5. Approve the comparison protocol. Agree baseline and follow-up windows, timezone, cadence, evidence sufficiency, guardrails, and decision criteria. Keep comparable window lengths and scope for manual measurement, including edge deployments without automatic evaluation. Where automatic impact measurement is supported and eligible, preserve the product's own baseline and evaluation period separately; confirm applicability before deploying.
  6. Save the baseline record before optimization. Export available reports or capture screenshots with filters and timestamps. Include source links and configuration notes. If the related change has already shipped, record that limitation; do not label a post-change snapshot a pre-change baseline.

Move on when

A pre-change baseline and comparison protocol are approved for the selected route; any automated experiment baseline is separately identified and verified before or through the supported deployment flow.

Organize the work

Turn the gap into an owned action plan

Select work that addresses the documented gap and can be delivered, validated, and evaluated. Keep the strategy as the parent; add existing opportunities or create new manual opportunities as related child actions.

Lead: Strategy owner + SEO / contentSupport: Implementers + approversOutput: Owned actions
  1. Review Opportunities and the baseline together. Include both ABV's automatically surfaced suggestions and gaps identified by your team. For each candidate, confirm business relevance, measured gap, affected URLs or claims, implementation feasibility, owner, and measurement fit. Prefer a focused change your team can complete over a large backlog with no approvals or evaluable scope.
  2. Choose the opportunity and execution route. Define the work that addresses the measured gap. Supported onsite opportunity types can be deployed through Optimize at Edge—not just RCV. Other content, technical, catalog, or offsite actions may require the responsible CMS, engineering, Commerce, or publisher workflow. Neither automatic discovery nor manual creation makes every opportunity edge-deployable.
  3. Reuse or create the correct Workspace strategy. Open an existing strategy when its objective and scope match, or create a manual strategy through Opportunity Workspace. Use a generated strategy when the workflow provides one; the documented RCV deployment flow creates it automatically. Do not create duplicates or assume every edge type creates an automatic strategy or experiment.
  4. Add existing opportunities or create new manual ones. Within the strategy, open Add Opportunity. Under Add from existing opportunities, search and select the relevant existing opportunities. Alternatively, under Create New Opportunities, choose Create opportunity, complete the required details, and add the new manual opportunity to this strategy. Confirm the addition with Add Opportunities. You do not need to create a new parent strategy just to add a manual opportunity.
  5. Build an action register before publishing. For each opportunity, record its real ABV ID or reference, whether it was surfaced automatically or created manually, proposed change, target area, owner, approver, planned dates, actual completion/deployment date, and validation evidence. Use the UI's actual statuses; the “planned / in progress / complete” labels in the companion worksheet are planning conventions, not guaranteed product enums.
  6. Approve the implementation and fallback. Review facts, brand/legal requirements, technical risks, measurement dependencies, and rollback or correction plan. Avoid adding unrelated work during the comparison period.
A manual opportunity is a work item, not an automatic deployment or experiment. Creating it records the work within the strategy; publish through the appropriate supported route and validate the live result. If the parent strategy already has an automatic experiment, do not assume the new opportunity enters its measured URL or prompt scope. Record the experiment's actual coverage and evaluate other work manually where needed.

Check automatic measurement separately

Impact-measurement eligibility matters

For the documented RCV impact-analysis path, current Adobe guidance calls for at least 20 URLs from the High Impact URLs filter, combining a recoverable visibility gap with sufficient agentic traffic. You can deploy optimizations to other pages without necessarily receiving an analysis. This is an impact-measurement condition, not a minimum for simply deploying at the edge. Confirm measurement support and current eligibility for the selected type; do not apply RCV-specific metrics, timing, or trial limits to every opportunity.

If there are too few qualifying pages, do not pad the deployment with irrelevant URLs or promise automatic measurement. Choose a valid manual evaluation approach, narrow the objective, or record the eligibility blocker and agree the next action with Adobe.

Keep as evidence

A ranked opportunity shortlist, selected actions and rationale, parent strategy reference or planned auto-creation route, owners/approvers, target scope, implementation dates, and route-specific eligibility checks.

Move on when

Real, in-scope opportunities have owners and approvals, with a valid deployment route and a documented measurement approach.

Implement and validate

Deploy the approved work and verify it is live

Publishing, marking an opportunity fixed, and validating the intended experience are different steps. Keep evidence of each one.

Lead: Content / web / CDN ownerSupport: Approver + strategy ownerOutput: Validated delivery

Route A · Owner-managed publishing and other non-edge work

  1. Confirm the approved brief, baseline, and publish window. Check the exact target URL or third-party claim, approved changes, responsible owner, guardrails, and rollback/correction plan.
  2. Implement through the responsible publishing system. For owner-managed onsite work, use the applicable CMS, engineering, or Commerce workflow, including direct catalog deployment when that is the opportunity's supported action. For offsite work, coordinate with the actual publisher or partner. A drafted request to a third party is not a completed offsite change. Supported Optimize at Edge execution follows Route B below.
  3. Validate the resulting content and access. Confirm factual accuracy, live status, expected headings/answers, public reachability, and the intended human and agent experience where relevant. Save the changed page or published third-party evidence and actual timestamp.
  4. Update the child actions and strategy progress. Record actual implementation dates, references, completed checks, and remaining work. Start the agreed follow-up window; do not treat a progress status as a measured outcome.

Route B · Supported opportunities with Optimize at Edge

  1. Open the selected opportunity in Opportunities. Review its surfaced suggestions, affected URLs, and analysis. Match the type to the objective: recover unreadable content, add summaries or FAQs, simplify copy, improve structure, add media text, or enrich Commerce product pages. Check the specific type's measurement support separately; use the current eligibility criteria if pursuing automatic analysis.
  2. Preview, edit where supported, and approve. Use Preview to compare the current page with the proposed agent experience. Use Edit where available to refine generated text before publishing. Confirm factual accuracy, public-content boundaries, and brand/legal approval. Validate that human visitors and SEO bots retain the original experience.
  3. Use the one-click deployment action after review. Select approved suggestions/URLs and use the opportunity's Deploy or Deploy optimizations control, then review and confirm the selected changes. RCV's flow uses a Deploy to Edge confirmation; follow the actual labels for other types. No origin CMS change is required for supported edge delivery. Retain per-URL results and investigate failed or partial deployments.
  4. Verify live delivery and the applicable strategy. Use View Live under Fixed Suggestions to confirm the agent-visible result. RCV's documented flow marks successful suggestions Optimized and generates a Workspace strategy with the relevant scope and measurements. For other types, use a generated strategy if provided or maintain the manual strategy; do not assume an automatic record or impact report exists.
  5. Protect the evaluation period and monitor content drift. Avoid unrelated changes to measured pages. Record source updates, paused suggestions, rollbacks, routing changes, and other events. For content opportunities such as summaries, source changes can pause delivery for human review; Adobe documents reliance on the source Last-Modified header, with optimization skipped when that signal is unavailable. Recheck live delivery after changes and use the supported rollback flow if needed.
Edge delivery boundary: Optimize at Edge serves supported optimized HTML to agentic traffic at the CDN layer without changing the origin CMS. Adobe documents no change to human or SEO-bot delivery for this route; validate that separation in your own configuration. Ordinary CMS edits, by contrast, may affect all visitors.

One-click deployment is not automatic impact measurement

Optimize at Edge is the execution path for multiple supported opportunity types. Impact Measurement Engine support is a separate capability and eligibility check. Use its analysis only where provided; otherwise evaluate the edge-deployed strategy manually against the recorded baseline. Automatically surfaced access errors, catalog updates, or offsite recommendations may follow a different action path rather than Optimize at Edge.

Keep as evidence

Final approval, change/version or deployment ID, actual timestamp, successful and failed URL results, live validation, strategy reference, rollback/correction procedure, and an interference log covering other changes.

Move on when

Approved actions are implemented, live behavior is validated, and the Workspace record and opportunity evidence reflect what actually shipped.

6A · Measure

Compare like with like, then state what the evidence supports

Maintain two separate measurement lanes: ongoing Brand Presence for your curated cohort, and supported Workspace experiment outcomes for the deployed optimization.

Lane 1 · Ongoing monitoring

Paid Brand Presence

Prompt set: Your recorded Prompt Library cohort.
Measures: Brand Visibility, Source Visibility, and selected supporting diagnostics.
Use: Observe movement for a consistent monitored scope. Results do not automatically isolate causation.

Lane 2 · Supported experiment

Workspace / Impact Engine

Prompt set: Dedicated prompts generated for supported, eligible deployments.
Measures: Copy the actual displayed metrics; the documented RCV analysis reports Citation Rate and Content Visibility.
Use: Read the product's analysis within its measured scope. Do not assume every edge type provides this lane.

For strategies without automatic impact measurement

This includes owner-managed onsite/offsite work and supported edge deployments that do not receive an automatic impact analysis. One-click implementation can still be followed by manual evaluation.

  1. Confirm the follow-up period is ready. Use the agreed window after validated deployment. Check data freshness and coverage before comparing; do not compare a complete baseline to an incomplete current period without explicitly qualifying it.
  2. Reapply the recorded configuration. Keep exact core wording and cohort, brand/aliases, market-language, platform scope, comparison brands, filters, and definitions stable. Use comparable date-window lengths, not identical historical dates. Record configuration drift or other interventions.
  3. Capture the follow-up and prompt-level evidence. Use the actual ABV labels and units. Save representative improved, unchanged, and worsened answers/citations where available; avoid cherry-picking only favorable prompts.
  4. Recheck relevant claims, URLs, and traffic. Add supporting data only for the same relevant scope and available periods. Missing integrations or response coverage gaps are limitations, not zero outcomes.
  5. Write an observation, not unsupported causation. Describe what changed and for which scope. When several child actions ran, report strategy-level movement without assigning the uplift to one child opportunity.

For a supported, eligible Workspace experiment

  1. Monitor the actual strategy lifecycle. The documented RCV flow uses Baseline collected → Optimization deployed → Measuring impact → Experiment complete and typically takes about two weeks. Use the displayed lifecycle and evaluation period for the selected type; do not promise RCV timing or metrics for all edge-deployed opportunities.
  2. Inspect the product's impact analysis. Record its exact baseline, post-optimization results, metric names/units, experiment dates, measurement prompts, and deployed-versus-measured URL scope. The engine may evaluate only a representative subset with sufficient data, not every deployed page.
  3. Keep the dedicated prompts separate. Experiment prompts are visible in the corresponding strategy, remain active only during measurement, do not count toward the Prompt quota, and retire afterward. Experiment results do not substitute for or change your ongoing Brand Presence cohort.
  4. Investigate missing analysis before concluding. Check measurement support for the selected type, current eligibility, deployment status, and processing with Adobe. For the documented RCV path, also verify the selected High Impact URLs. Pages may be optimized even when no impact analysis is generated; use manual evaluation when automatic measurement is unavailable and record that distinction.

Percentage points versus relative change

A move from 28% to 33% is +5 percentage points, not +5%. The relative increase is approximately 17.9%, if a relative calculation is appropriate for that metric. Prefer the product's displayed change and record its unit; do not apply percentage arithmetic to an unexplained score.

Defensible sample interpretation

For the same recorded Northstar cohort and configuration, follow-up Brand Visibility was 33% versus 28% at baseline, and Source Visibility was 17% versus 12%. Three related page opportunities were implemented. These are strategy-level observations; the comparison does not identify which action caused the movement. Any Workspace experiment results are reported separately with their own prompts, dates, and URL coverage.

Limits to disclose: AI-answer variability, insufficient or changing response coverage, different platforms or competitors, brand/alias changes, prompt edits, missing integrations, multiple interventions, and differences between deployed URLs and the experiment's measured subset. Do not generalize one cohort to all AI answers or translate visibility movement into revenue causation.

6B · Decide

Close the initiative with a decision-ready readout

  1. Confirm implementation records are accurate. Reconcile the parent strategy and child opportunities with the actual shipped work. Keep unresolved actions, failed URLs, or incomplete measurement visible. Use the statuses available in your UI rather than inventing a “complete” command.
  2. Write the strategy readout. Include objective and scope, Workspace reference, completed opportunity IDs and dates, core cohort/version, baseline/follow-up windows, Brand Presence results, separate Workspace outcomes if supported, coverage, limitations, and evidence references.
  3. Add business impact only when supportable. Use Adobe Analytics, CJA, or GA4 when configured and relevant. Explain event definitions, attribution, and periods. If conversion or revenue evidence is unavailable, say so; it is not mandatory to invent ROI to complete an AI-visibility readout.
  4. Review with the measurement owner and approver. Confirm numerical accuracy, metric units, comparable scope, and cautious attribution. Check that the conclusion follows the pre-agreed decision criteria.
  5. Record the next-cycle decision and ownership. Choose scale, iterate, hold, or stop. Add a named owner and due date, save the evidence pack, and update Workspace progress as supported. A hold with pending data is a reviewed outcome, not an “Experiment complete” status.

Decision 01

Scale

Evidence meets the agreed criteria and guardrails. Repeat the method or create a new strategy for a new scope; retain the current cohort for ongoing monitoring.

Decision 02

Iterate

Evidence suggests a useful direction, but the change, scope, or measurement design needs refinement. Define the next version without silently replacing the original baseline.

Decision 03

Hold

Evidence is insufficient or a dependency remains. Record the blocker, responsible owner, and next review date. Keep unfinished measurement gates open.

Decision 04

Stop

Evidence or feasibility does not justify continuing this approach. Record the learning, any rollback/correction, and the next priority.

Northstar readout · Fictional sample

Northstar completed three related opportunities on its scheduling page for the US-English shift-scheduling scope. For the same eight-prompt cohort, Brand Visibility increased from 28% to 33% and Source Visibility from 12% to 17% across the recorded periods. Response counts are reported as coverage context. No individual child action is credited with the movement, and no revenue impact is claimed. The team will continue monitoring this scope and separately evaluate whether another use case warrants a new strategy. Final decision, owner, and review date must be entered against the team's agreed criteria.

Keep as evidence

Measure: comparable follow-up export/screenshots, two-part scorecard, exact prompt evidence, coverage notes, experiment report if available, interference log, metric-definition changes, and reviewer-approved interpretation. Decide: final reviewed readout, decision and rationale, reviewers, next action/owner/date, saved scorecard and source evidence, and the actual Workspace progress/outcome record. For a QBR, summarize scope → work → results → limits → next decision.

Complete when

Follow-up evidence has been reviewed against the correct baseline—or a genuine measurement limitation is investigated and documented with a next review date—and the two measurement lanes remain separate. The reviewed readout states the evidence and limitations, with a recorded decision, named next-action owner, and review date.

The finish line

Confirm the activity is genuinely complete

The activity is complete when the six step criteria above are met and these four final checks are true. It is a self-check, not an automatic certification or an ABV status update.

Final check 1

The evidence pack is saved

The brief, prompt cohort, baseline, real child actions, deployment validation, follow-up or investigated limitation, and readout are retrievable.

Final check 2

Measurement and attribution are accurate

Metric names, units, dates, cohorts, filters, and coverage are explicit. Workspace experiment results are separate; no unsupported child-action or revenue causation is claimed.

Final check 3

The owner and reviewer have approved the conclusion

The decision follows the agreed criteria. Any hold, missing analysis, or unfinished work remains visible, with a clear follow-up.

Final check 4

The next action has an owner and date

Your team can repeat the method for a new scope without silently changing this strategy's original cohort or baseline.
