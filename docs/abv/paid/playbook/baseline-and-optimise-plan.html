GEO Baseline & Optimization Plan

GEO baseline and optimization plan for the Summit Cash Rewards Credit Card

A pre-implementation plan built on a locked 64-prompt ChatGPT baseline (192 answer runs), framed against four GEO goals for the card — win unbranded discovery, agent-readable pages, owned citations, and AI-referred engagement. The card performs best on branded and product-term questions, with a moderate but consistent gap on unbranded discovery — and three key pages show a related content-visibility gap. The sections below cover those goals, the baseline, the gaps behind it, and a set of prioritized opportunities, including a proposed Optimize at Edge deployment.

Cedarline Bank · Summit Cash Rewards Credit Card · Illustrative sample · United States / English · ChatGPT only

  • 53% overall mention rate (192 runs)
  • 29% owned-page citation rate
  • 24–34% content visibility on 3 key pages
  • up to 4.2× estimated content gain

05 Gaps

Two related gaps, one likely cause

A softer discovery gap in how ChatGPT answers, and a content-visibility gap in what agents can actually read on Cedarline’s own pages.

Gap 1 · Discovery G1

Unbranded discovery is the weakest group, not an empty one

The unbranded cash-back discovery-and-comparison group is the largest (72 runs) and the softest: the card is mentioned in 40% of runs and a Cedarline page is cited in 16 of 72 (22%) — roughly 13 points below the branded group’s citation rate.

Interpretation: there is room to make Cedarline’s public content a more competitive source for discovery — this pattern alone does not yet explain why citation lags mention here.

06 Working diagnosis

Both gaps point to the same likely constraint

Content that Cedarline has already published isn’t fully reaching the agent-readable view — and that is the more tractable of the two gaps to close first.

Evidence 1

ChatGPT pattern

ObservationCitation rate is 13–16 points softer on unbranded discovery than on branded or rates/fees questions
Evidence 2

ABV page diagnostics

ObservationContent visibility runs 24%, 29%, and 34% (the three priority pages) — all relevant and heavily requested

Confidence: high that the selected pages have a content-visibility gap in this illustration; medium that it fully explains the softer discovery-group numbers. Recovering content can make it accessible to agents, but does not guarantee ChatGPT will cite it.

Priority 1 in detail · Optimize at Edge deployment

Recommended change: deploy the Recover Content Visibility optimization through Optimize at Edge on the three selected credit-card pages. Optimize at Edge applies supported changes at the CDN layer for LLM user agents without requiring a CMS authoring change; it targets agentic traffic rather than changing the human or SEO-bot experience.

  1. Confirm the rate, fee, reward, eligibility, and disclosure content is current and approved on the public source pages.
  2. Preview the Recover Content Visibility changes for each URL.
  3. Deploy only the reviewed pages.
  4. Use the live agent-facing view to verify that the recovered content matches the approved source.
  5. Roll back if any content is stale, incomplete, or inconsistent.
Credit-card content safeguard. Edge delivery must not create or alter product terms. If an APR, fee, reward condition, or disclosure is missing or incorrect on the source page, correct it at the source before exposing it to agents. Keep customer-specific information and individualized credit decisions out of scope.
