Organize the team

Name an owner for every responsibility

One person can hold several roles, but every decision and deliverable needs a named owner. Keep the core team small and bring reviewers in when a change needs them.

  • Core team

    Executive sponsor

    Connects ABV work to business priorities, gives visible support, and clears cross-functional barriers.

  • Core team

    ABV program / GEO lead

    Runs the work day to day: keeps priorities and actions moving, facilitates reviews, and coordinates with Adobe.

  • Core team

    Business, brand, or product owner

    Sets the journey and business context, validates priorities, and makes or sponsors business decisions.

  • Core team

    GEO/SEO and content

    Shapes journeys and prompts, interprets insights, and recommends or creates content changes.

  • Core team

    Analytics / measurement

    Defines KPIs, maintains the baseline, and supports the review of results and business impact.

  • Platform

    ABV administrator

    Manages users and access, brand configuration, and integrations in ABV.

  • Technical

    Web / engineering

    Assesses and delivers approved source, CMS, or technical changes through established release processes.

  • Technical

    IT / CDN and security

    Owns log forwarding, crawler access, WAF and bot management, and edge routing when it is in scope.

  • As needed

    Approvers and reviewers

    Brand, legal, compliance, or technical sign-off when a change requires it under your processes.

Move on when

Every role has a named owner or is marked not needed, and each person knows what they own.

Agree ways of working

Agree how decisions get made

Operating approach

Organizations may work in centralized, federated, or decentralized ways. The right fit depends on factors such as the number of business units, local autonomy, available capabilities, and the need for shared oversight.

Centralized

A central ABV team coordinates priorities, standards, and decisions; delivery teams carry out approved work.

Federated

A central team sets shared direction and governance while business units or markets plan and execute locally.

Decentralized

Individual teams lead their own ABV work within shared enterprise principles and agreed guardrails.

Decision path

  1. RecommendGEO/SEO and contentPropose prioritized opportunities and changes.
  2. ApproveBusiness owner + reviewersSign off scope, change, and timing, with legal or compliance when required.
  3. ExecuteContent, web, or IT/CDNDeliver through the agreed publishing route.
  4. ReviewAnalytics + business ownerConfirm what shipped and assess the results.

Working rhythm

  1. Book a regular working session. A short weekly check-in keeps actions, approvals, and blockers moving during the first loop.
  2. Use existing forums for decisions. Route ABV decisions through current planning, approval, and release forums. Add a dedicated review only when they can’t provide the ownership you need.
  3. Keep one shared record. Track decisions, actions, owners, and outcomes in one place so follow-through doesn’t depend on meetings.
  4. Prepare the people involved. Have the sponsor explain why the work matters, tell impacted teams what changes and when, and give each role focused guidance.

Move on when

The operating approach and decision path are agreed, the working session is booked, and impacted teams know their part.

Define success

Agree what success looks like and who reviews it

  1. Start from the business objective. Name the outcome the first loop should move, and the product and journey it applies to, before choosing metrics.
  2. Pick a primary KPI and supporting measures. Choose what is relevant to that journey, such as Brand Visibility or Share of Voice, plus AI-referral engagement or conversions where analytics is connected.
  3. Agree the baseline period and decision criteria. Decide in advance what evidence means scale, iterate, hold, or stop, so the result isn’t judged after the fact.
  4. Set the review contract. Agree who reviews each measure, how often, and how the results feed the next decision.

Keep outcomes and ways of working separate. Business and visibility measures show impact; follow-through on decisions and actions shows whether the operating model works. Don’t assume one set of KPIs or thresholds fits every product, journey, or market.

Move on when

The objective, primary KPI, supporting measures, baseline period, decision criteria, and reviewers are agreed.

Configure the account

Set up the account, scope, and access

  1. Confirm capabilities with Adobe. Check which capabilities, integrations, and opportunity types are available in your account. Some are access-gated; for example, product-page enrichment requires Adobe Commerce.
  2. Give the right people access. The ABV administrator sets up users. Make sure at least one person can configure and, where applicable, deploy.
  3. Configure the brand and first scope. In Brands Management, add the brand, market, primary domain and supporting URLs, aliases, and 3–5 competitors. Start small: one brand, one market, and one or two priority journeys.
  4. Confirm crawler access. The technical owner checks robots.txt, WAF, bot-management, and CDN rules so the ABV crawler (Spacecat/1.0) and the AI agents you intend to allow can reach in-scope content.
  5. Agree the publishing route. Use Optimize at Edge for supported changes where edge is in scope; otherwise route work through CMS, engineering, or offsite owners.

Move on when

Users have the access they need, Brands Management reflects the agreed scope, and crawler access and the publishing route are confirmed.

Connect your data

Connect what your first loop needs

Connect only what the selected loop uses, and verify each connection before relying on its data. Start CDN work first: traffic evidence and edge deployment both depend on it.

Traffic evidence

Start first

CDN log forwarding

Shows which pages AI agents request and which visits come from AI answers. Traffic baselines and several recommendations depend on it.

Owner
IT / CDN
Unlocks
Agentic Traffic, Referral Traffic, URL Inspector agent hits, and Citation Attempts in Prompt Strategy.
Ready when
Fresh, in-scope logs are ingesting, and CDN targeting for the first loop is complete.

Edge deployment

If in scope

Optimize at Edge routing

Delivers supported changes to AI agents at the CDN, without changing the origin CMS or human and SEO delivery.

Owner
IT / CDN
Unlocks
Preview, deploy, view live, and roll back supported opportunity types.
Ready when
Routing and fetcher access are configured, AI-only delivery is verified, and rollback works.

Prompt discovery

Google Search Console

Grounds prompt selection in real search behavior, so the tracked cohort reflects what people actually ask.

Owner
SEO, with the ABV administrator
Unlocks
Search Console recommendations in Prompt Strategy, based on real queries for your domain.
Ready when
The approved property for the in-scope domain is connected.

Business outcomes

Analytics

Links AI visibility to the engagement and conversions of visitors arriving from AI answers.

Owner
Analytics
Unlocks
AI-referral engagement and conversions from Adobe Analytics, Customer Journey Analytics, or GA4.
Ready when
A supported provider and property are connected, and the measures to review are agreed.

Requested isn’t ready. A connection counts only when its owner has verified data is flowing for the in-scope domains. If edge deployment isn’t part of the first loop, skip routing and use owner-managed publishing.

Move on when

Each selected connection has an owner and is verified, and any skipped connection is a deliberate choice.

Confirm readiness

Confirm you’re ready for the first loop

People & decisions

  • Every role has a named owner
  • Decision path and approvers are agreed
  • Working session and review cadence are booked
  • Primary KPI, baseline period, and decision criteria are agreed

Account & access

  • Capabilities and integrations are confirmed with Adobe
  • Users can configure and, where needed, deploy
  • Brand, market, domains, aliases, and competitors are configured
  • Crawler access and the publishing route are confirmed

Connections

  • CDN logs are flowing for in-scope domains
  • Optimize at Edge routing and rollback are verified · if in scope
  • Search Console property is connected · if used
  • Analytics provider and property are connected · if used

Gaps don’t always block. If an item isn’t needed for the first loop’s scope, record it with an owner and date, and move on.

The account, owners, approvals, and selected connections are in place for the first loop. Next, learn the six-step method, then run it with the Run your first GEO loop guide.
