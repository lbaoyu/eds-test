On-site GEO for AI answers

Write pages an AI can find, trust and quote

On-site content is the lever your teams control directly. The job of every important page is to be easy for an AI system to access, understand and trust — and to lift a clean answer from, without inventing or mixing in the wrong detail.

What a page has to be

Discoverable by AI crawlers, understandable through structure, relevant to the prompts your audience asks, trustworthy enough to cite, and current. The patterns below are the concrete moves that get a page there — each with a before/after you can copy. The final group goes one step further: converting the visitor once an AI answer sends them your way.

What the research says

What actually moves AI visibility

Princeton’s GEO study (Aggarwal et al., KDD 2024) tested nine content edits across ~10,000 queries and nine domains. Edits that signal epistemic authority — statistics, quotations, citing credible sources — lifted visibility by roughly 30–40%, while classic keyword density barely moved citation probability.

Read the numbers honestly

Figures are maxima under favourable conditions, not averages. A 2025 follow-up (C-SEO Bench, Puerto et al.) found many “conversational-SEO” tricks don’t help — and some hurt — while plain source relevance keeps working. Optimise against observed citations, not tactics in isolation.

The strongest single signal

The single strongest content signal for being cited is original, proprietary data or statistics — numbers no one else has published. Specificity and evidence beat volume and adjectives at every turn.

Who does what

AI-ready content — role checklist

Pick the track that matches your role. Each is the top-three of what makes content safe for an AI to reuse.

Content / UX writers

  1. Make pages answer-first and question-shaped (headings, summaries).
  2. Structure content into single-intent sections with short lists.
  3. Add FAQs, examples and clear language AI can reuse safely.

Product managers

  1. Own canonical definitions and eligibility rules.
  2. Clarify differences between similar features and plans.
  3. Keep versioning and product rules aligned with docs and legal.

Product marketing / Pricing

  1. Make plans, pricing and value clear in tables and examples.
  2. Disambiguate similarly named plans.
  3. Provide concrete benefits, metrics and usage scenarios.

Legal / Privacy

  1. Provide TL;DRs and plain-language explanations of key policies.
  2. Clarify which laws and rights apply where.
  3. Align product content with legal constraints (region, age, data).

Support / Docs writers

  1. Turn common questions into FAQs and how-to articles.
  2. Express troubleshooting as step-by-step flows and error → resolution tables.
  3. Document edge cases and outcomes.

SEO / Web / IA

  1. Maintain canonical “What is X?” and hub-and-spoke structures.
  2. Keep terminology and URL intent consistent.
  3. Expose geo-specific content clearly and keep pages discoverable.

Localisation / Regional

  1. Deliver natural, idiomatic local-language versions of key journeys.
  2. Localise legal, pricing and eligibility rules per region.
  3. Adapt examples and terminology to local context.

Shape the answer

Pre-click7 patterns

How the page is organised so a model can find and lift a clean answer.

01

Answer-first page summaries

Pre-click
PrimaryContent / UXPMMSEOAlsoProduct

Open key pages with 2–4 sentences that directly answer the page’s main question — what it is, who it’s for, key benefits — instead of a vague intro.

Why AI prefers it
  • LLMs often read and summarise the top of the page first.
  • A concise, self-contained summary is easy to quote or paraphrase.
  • Reduces the chance the model picks an off-topic sentence as “the answer”.
Before

“In a world where collaboration is more important than ever, companies are searching for new ways to stay ahead. Our platform helps you unlock the power of teamwork and drive innovation across your organisation.”

After

What is Acme Tasks?

Acme Tasks is a project and task management tool for small and mid-sized teams. It lets you create tasks, assign owners, track deadlines, and see progress in one view. Most teams use it to coordinate launches, campaigns and internal projects without email chaos.

02

Question-aligned headings

Pre-click
PrimaryUXDocsSupportAlsoSEOPMM

Use headings and sub-headings that mirror real user questions rather than internal labels.

Why AI prefers it
  • LLMs match user queries against similar phrasing in headings.
  • Question-shaped headings make the relevant section easy to locate.
  • Reduces ambiguity when several sections could apply.
Before
  • H2: “Account Settings”
  • H3: “Plan Options”
  • H3: “Advanced Configuration”
After
  • H2: “How do I change my subscription plan?”
  • H3: “How to upgrade or downgrade”
  • H3: “How billing changes when you switch plans”
03

Single-intent sections

Pre-click
PrimaryUXDocsAlsoProductSupport

Keep each section (and ideally each page) focused on one intent — what it is, how to set up, pricing, troubleshooting — not a mix.

Why AI prefers it
  • LLMs map one question to one coherent answer span.
  • Mixed sections force the model to filter noise and can drop important conditions.
Before

H2: “Using Smart Reports” — a single paragraph combines what they are, how to enable them, pricing differences and common errors.

After
  • H2: “What are Smart Reports?”
  • H2: “How do I turn on Smart Reports?”
  • H2: “How much do Smart Reports cost?”
  • H2: “How do I fix Smart Reports export errors?”
04

Short paragraphs & bullet lists

Pre-click
PrimaryContentDocsAlsoSEO

Split long sentences and paragraphs into short, clean sentences and scannable lists.

Why AI prefers it
  • Smaller chunks make it easier to extract exact steps or facts.
  • Reduces the chance the model loses a condition inside a run-on sentence.
Before

“To add a new admin you need to open Settings and then go to Users where you can click Add User and assign the Admin role and you should also make sure that they confirm their email or they won’t be able to log in correctly.”

After

To add a new admin:

  1. Go to Settings › Users.
  2. Click Add user.
  3. Enter their email address.
  4. Assign the Admin role.
  5. Ask them to confirm their email before logging in.
05

Pre-click
PrimaryUXDocsDesignAlsoSupport

Use the exact UI wording — buttons, menus, screens — in the surrounding explanatory text.

Why AI prefers it
  • Lets assistants give precise navigation steps (“Click Create team”).
  • Avoids confusion when the UI uses one term and the docs use another.
Before

“Go to the area where you manage teams and create a new one.”

After

“Go to Settings › Teams and click Create team. Give your team a name and, optionally, add a description.”

  • Models primarily consume text; they don’t see your images directly.

After

Why AI prefers it
  • Models lift single sentences out of context; a self-contained one survives extraction intact.
  • Reduces the chance a lifted sentence loses its condition or subject.
Research-backedContent written for clean extraction is quoted more readily; well-structured pages are cited more often. — Citation-pattern analyses, 2026

After

Pre-click
PrimaryPMMProductDocsAlsoSalesSupport

Why AI prefers it
  • Clarifies the distinct meaning of each label.

Consistent terminology & controlled synonyms

Pre-click
PrimaryDocsPMMLocalisationAlsoProduct

Define one canonical name per concept and limit synonyms to a small, controlled set.

Why AI prefers it
  • Reduces confusion when several terms describe the same concept.
  • Helps models map a user’s wording (a synonym) back to one canonical entity.
Before

Pages use “workspace”, “team area”, “board space” and “collaboration hub” interchangeably.

After
  • Canonical term: workspace.
  • Acceptable variants: team workspace, workspace for your team.
  • Avoid new labels such as “board space” unless it is genuinely a different concept.

Explicit eligibility, scope & limits

Pre-click
PrimaryProductDocsLegalAlsoSupportPMM

State clearly who is eligible, where it applies, and the key limits — plans, roles, countries, thresholds.

Why AI prefers it
  • AI fields many “Am I eligible?” and “Does this work in country X?” questions.
  • Enumerated rules give concrete facts instead of implied assumptions.
  • Negative rules make it safe to answer “no” correctly.
Before

“Instant withdrawals are available in most regions for creators.”

After

Who can use Instant Withdrawals?

  • Available in: US, UK, Canada, Germany, France.
  • Not available in: other countries.
  • Account types: individual and business creator accounts.
  • Requirement: at least 3 completed payouts in the last 90 days.
  • If you don’t meet these conditions, Instant Withdrawals won’t appear in your account.

Edge cases & exceptions

Pre-click
PrimarySupportDocsProductAlsoLegalCX

Document the important “what if” scenarios: failed payments, over-limit, suspensions, deletion.

Why AI prefers it
  • Edge cases are where wrong answers carry the highest risk.
  • Explicit rules reduce hallucinated consequences.
Before

“If something goes wrong with your subscription, contact support.”

After

If your subscription payment fails

  • We email you and retry up to 3 times over 7 days.
  • Your account stays active during the 7-day grace period.

  • After 90 days, we may permanently delete your content.
32

Structured troubleshooting flows

Pre-click
PrimarySupportDocsAlsoProduct

Write troubleshooting as branching “if A, then B” flows instead of one long narrative.

Why AI prefers it
  • Models answer “What do I do if error 403 appears?” more easily from branch logic.
  • Reduces vague replies like “Try again later”.
Before

“If login fails, check your details and try again. If it still doesn’t work, there may be a problem with your account.”

After

If you get a 403 error when logging in

  1. Check that you’re using the correct email address.
  2. If your company uses SSO, try Sign in with SSO instead of email + password.
  3. If you still see 403, your account may be disabled — contact your admin to re-enable access.

“Nuestra plataforma ofrece tableros de proyectos para que los equipos planifiquen, asignen tareas y sigan el progreso en tiempo real.

Es ideal para equipos de marketing, operaciones y producto que coordinan campañas o lanzamientos.”

Convert the AI referral

Post-click4 patterns

The post-click half: once an AI answer sends someone to your page, serve a visitor who arrived mid-decision — confirm the claim, prove it, make the next step obvious.

35

Answer-consistent landing

Post-click
PrimaryContentPMMWebAlsoProduct

When an AI answer cites a page, the visitor arrives expecting the exact claim they just read. Open that page by confirming and expanding that claim — don’t route them to a generic homepage or a hero that restarts the pitch.

Why it works
  • A mismatch between the AI’s summary and the landing page breaks trust and bounces the visitor.
  • Confirming the claim immediately continues the conversation the assistant started.
Research-backedAI-referred visits went from ~23% less likely to convert (mid-2025) to ~16% more likely than other channels by late 2025 — the post-click experience is worth optimising. — Adobe Analytics
Before

An AI cites “Acme Docs supports SSO and audit logs”, but the link lands on the marketing homepage headlined “Transform how your team works.”

After

The linked page opens with “Acme Docs: SSO & audit logs”, confirms both in the first line, then links to setup and pricing.

36

Lead with verification, not re-introduction

Post-click
PrimaryContentUXPMMAlsoWeb

AI referrals arrive mid-decision, having already read a summary. Put the concrete proof first — specs, numbers, eligibility, price — with the next step above the fold; skip the “what is X” intro they’ve already had.

Why it works
  • The visitor is warm and specific, not new — a 101 introduction wastes the moment.
  • Proof plus a next step matches why they clicked: to verify and act.
Before

The landing page opens with three paragraphs of “In today’s fast-moving world…” before any specifics.

After

It opens with the spec table, the eligibility rule and a “Start free trial” button; the background story moves further down.

37

Anticipate the follow-up question

Post-click
PrimaryContentDocsPMMAlsoSupport

The AI answered the first question; the visitor clicked for the next one — “how much exactly?”, “does it work for my case?”, “how do I start?”. Put that follow-up answer on the landing page.

Why it works
  • AI-referred visitors have a predictable next question; answering it on arrival prevents a second search.
  • Reduces the bounce back to the assistant to ask again.
Before

A page cited for “supports enterprise SSO” says nothing about which plans include it or how to switch it on.

After

The same page adds “SSO is on Pro+ and Enterprise — here’s how to enable it” right where the visitor lands.

38

One unmistakable next action

Post-click
PrimaryWebPMMUXAlsoGrowth

A high-intent visitor who arrived ready to act needs one obvious primary action — start, buy, book, contact — not a wall of equally-weighted links.

Why it works
  • AI referrals convert best when the next step is singular and visible.
  • Competing calls-to-action dilute a decision the visitor has largely already made.
Before

The landing page offers “Learn more”, “Watch demo”, “Read docs”, “Contact sales” and “Pricing” as five equal buttons.

After

One primary “Start free trial” button, with secondary links de-emphasised beneath it.

The machine-facing plumbing — semantic markup, structured data, server-rendering, linking and media — usually shared with front-end and SEO.

Markup & rendering

Pre-click4 patterns

Semantic HTML, schema and server-rendering so crawlers can parse and retrieve the answer.

39

Semantic HTML & heading hierarchy

Pre-click
PrimaryFront-endUXDocsAlsoSEO

Use real semantic elements and a clean H1→H2→H3 outline so machines can parse the document’s structure, not just how it looks.

Why AI prefers it
  • Models rely on heading structure to split a page into answerable spans.
  • Real lists and tables mark up meaning explicitly; styled <div>s hide it.
Research-backedStructured, well-organised pages are disproportionately cited — heading and list structure is a parsing aid. — Citation-pattern analyses, 2026
Before

Everything is a styled <div>, so structure exists only visually:

<div class="title">Billing</div>
<div class="big">How refunds work</div>
<div class="txt">Refunds take 5–7 days…</div>
After

Real elements, one H1, a question H2 and a proper list:

<h1>Billing</h1>
<h2>How do refunds work?</h2>
<p>Refunds take 5–7 days to reach your card.</p>
<ul>
  <li>Card payments: 5–7 days</li>
  <li>Bank transfer: up to 10 days</li>
</ul>
40

Structured data (JSON-LD schema)

Pre-click
PrimaryFront-endSEOProductAlsoDocs

Add schema.org JSON-LD — Organization, Product, FAQPage, HowTo, Article, BreadcrumbList — that mirrors the visible content exactly.

Why AI prefers it
  • Gives machines a typed reading of entities, prices, FAQs and steps.
  • Only mark up what a user can actually see on the page — never assert unverifiable claims.
Research-backedMicrosoft confirmed schema markup helps its LLMs interpret content; keep it matched to what’s visible. — Bing / Canel, SMX 2025
Before

Prices appear only in body copy and images — nothing a machine can read as a typed Product with an Offer.

After

The visible price, mirrored in schema.org JSON-LD:

<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Product",
  "name": "Acme Pro",
  "offers": {
    "@type": "Offer",
    "price": "29.00",
    "priceCurrency": "USD",
    "availability": "InStock"
  }
}
</script>
41

Server-render the key content

Pre-click
PrimaryFront-endSEOAlsoProduct

Make sure the core answer — headings, summary, key copy, links — is in the raw HTML, not injected only after client-side JavaScript.

Why AI prefers it
  • Many AI crawlers don’t execute JavaScript; a bare app shell reads as an empty page.
  • Server-rendered or pre-rendered HTML makes the answer retrievable on first request.
Research-backedAdobe’s guidance warns JavaScript-only fragments can be invisible to LLM crawlers — server-render key content. — Adobe GMO playbook
Before

The answer only exists after JavaScript runs — the first response is an empty shell:

<div id="app"></div>
<script src="/app.js"></script>
After

The answer is in the initial HTML; JavaScript only enhances it:

<h1>How do refunds work?</h1>
<p>Refunds take 5–7 days to reach your card.</p>
<a href="/refund-policy">Read the refund policy</a>
<!-- JS enhances; the answer is already here -->
42

Table of contents & jump links

Pre-click
PrimaryDocsUXAlsoSEO

On long pages, add an “On this page” table of contents with anchor links to each section heading.

Why AI prefers it
  • Gives models an explicit map of the page and stable anchors to the exact answer span.
  • Lets an assistant deep-link a reader straight to the relevant section.
Before

A 3,000-word guide with no in-page navigation and no section anchors — a model has to scan the whole thing.

After

A short anchor list at the top, each linking to a real section heading:

<nav aria-label="On this page">
  <a href="#what">What it is</a>
  <a href="#how">How to set it up</a>
  <a href="#pricing">Pricing</a>
  <a href="#faqs">FAQs</a>
</nav>

Linking & media

Pre-click4 patterns

Link architecture, anchor text and transcripts that route models to the right content.

43

Hub-and-spoke internal linking

Pre-click
PrimarySEODocsProductAlsoUX

From high-level “hub” pages (“What is X?”), link clearly to deeper how-to, pricing and comparison pages.

Why AI prefers it
  • When a hub becomes the primary answer source, links show the model where to go for detail.
  • Encourages AI to use the right page for the right intent.
Research-backedCiting credible sources yourself signals thoroughness and raises your own citation likelihood. — Aggarwal et al., 2024
Before

“Workspaces help you group projects and teams.” (no clear links to more detail)

After

Workspaces help you group projects and teams.

  • Learn how to create a workspace
  • Learn how to add people to a workspace
  • See workspaces vs projects
44

On-page transcripts for audio & video

Pre-click
PrimaryDocsContentMediaAlsoAccessibility

Publish full transcripts and descriptive captions for embedded video and audio on the page itself.

Why AI prefers it
  • Models consume text; a transcript turns spoken content into retrievable, citable material.
  • Named features and steps in a transcript become answerable facts.
Research-backedBrand mentions in video titles and transcripts are among the strongest correlates of AI-answer visibility. — Ahrefs, 75,000 brands
Before

An embedded product-demo video with a one-line caption and nothing else on the page.

After

The video plus an on-page transcript naming each feature and step, and a short text summary of what the demo shows.

45

Descriptive, intent-revealing link text

Pre-click
PrimaryContentSEOUXAlsoDocs

Make link text describe the destination and intent — never “click here” or a bare URL.

Why AI prefers it
  • Link text tells a model what the linked page answers, strengthening hub-and-spoke signals.
  • Descriptive anchors help the model pick the right page for an intent.
Before

“For pricing, click here.”

After

“See the full pricing breakdown for team plans.”

46

Deep-link to the cited section

Post-click
PrimaryFront-endSEODocsAlsoWeb

Expose stable fragment anchors so a citation lands the visitor on the exact passage — not the top of a long page they then have to hunt through. The one post-click move in this group: set the anchors up front, and every AI referral lands on the answer.

Why it works
  • A referral that lands on the relevant answer continues the assistant’s thread; landing at the top forces a scroll-hunt.
  • Anchors also give engines a precise target to cite.
Before 
https://example.com/guide

→ lands at the top of a 3,000-word guide;
  the visitor scrolls to find the SSO section.
After 
https://example.com/guide#sso

→ lands directly on "How to enable SSO".
