Part 1Understand the experienceWhat it is and how it works · See it in action

The basics

What it is and how it works

Key points
  • Visitors can ask questions, compare options, and take a next step on your website.
  • Knowledge, skills, integrations, and guardrails shape the experience.
  • Answers use your configured sources—not general web knowledge.

01.1The concept

What is Adobe Brand Concierge?

Adobe Brand Concierge is a conversational experience on a brand’s website. Visitors ask questions in natural language, get relevant answers, and continue with follow-up questions instead of relying only on navigation or search.

Depending on its configuration, it can answer questions, help visitors discover or compare products, and offer a next step such as contacting a person.

What it is

A configured website experienceBuilt around a visitor task, using your approved content and connected services.

What not to assume

A replacement for every channelIt does not automatically replace website search, support, or sales teams.

What visitors experience

Ask their way

Type, or switch on hands-free voice mode where you enable it. Adobe also describes image input.

See richer answers

Product cards, side-by-side comparisons, images, and links—not just text.

Keep going

Suggested follow-up questions and a thumbs-up or thumbs-down rating on each answer.

Reach a person

Hand off to live chat or book a meeting, with the conversation history carried over.

The current scope serves anonymous visitors: answers come from the conversation and your knowledge sources, not a live lookup of a visitor’s profile or journey stage.

A clear answer, a useful next step

Five exchanges: a visitor compares two plans, asks about pricing, and requests a person.

Illustrative scenario · Fictional products

Choosing a project-management plan for eight people

Sample Q&A
Standard
S$19 / user / month.
Shared projects, task assignments, and dashboards.
Premium
S$29 / user / month.

Fictional product facts, not Adobe offerings. Monthly billing, before tax. In this scenario, sales chat is unavailable and meeting booking is enabled.

Visitor · Question 1

We have eight people and need to manage projects together. Which plan should we choose?

Brand ConciergeAnswer 1

Both Standard and Premium support shared projects, task assignments, and dashboards. Do you also need audit-log exports or priority support?

Why this works: Clarifies the requirement before recommending a plan.
Visitor · Question 2

No, we only need shared projects and task assignments. What would Standard cost for eight people?

Brand ConciergeAnswer 2

Standard fits those requirements. At S$19 per user per month, eight users cost S$152 per month, billed monthly before tax. It also includes dashboards.

Visitor · Question 3

What would we get with Premium, and how much more would it cost?

Brand ConciergeAnswer 3

Premium adds audit-log exports and priority support. It costs S$29 per user per month, so eight users cost S$232 per month before tax—S$80 more than Standard.

Why this works: Connects the additional features to the total cost and price difference.
Visitor · Question 4

Can you guarantee a 10% discount if we sign up today?

Brand ConciergeAnswer 4

I can’t confirm or promise a 10% discount. The plan information available to me doesn’t include that offer. Would you like to discuss pricing with our sales team?

Why this works: Acknowledges the missing offer instead of making an unsupported commitment.
Visitor · Question 5

Yes. Can I speak with someone now?

Brand ConciergeAnswer 5

No sales representative is available for live chat right now. Would you like to view available consultation times and book a 30-minute meeting instead?

Why this works: Offers the configured booking fallback. No meeting is confirmed until the visitor selects a valid slot and completes the booking.

Key points

03.2Start focused

Plan the experience around a clear use case

Before configuring an experience, define:

  • Visitor: Who is the intended audience?
  • Need: What are they trying to find, understand, or decide?
  • Current friction: Where does the existing website, search, or chat experience fall short?
  • Helpful content: What approved information would support the visitor?
  • Boundaries: What should the concierge handle, defer, decline, or route to a person?
  • Outcome: What would indicate that the visitor was better served?
Example use-case brief · Fictional B2B site

Help a small team choose its next step

Visitor
Business buyers comparing two service plans for a small team.
Need
Understand the differences and total cost, then choose a suitable plan or decide whether to speak with sales.
Current friction
Features are spread across several pages, making comparison difficult.
Helpful content
Approved plan details, pricing, comparisons, and FAQs.
Boundaries
Explain published plans and pricing. Do not promise unapproved discounts or contractual commitments; route questions requiring a person to sales.
Outcome
Complete a plan-selection task or book an appropriate consultation—not simply start a conversation.

Content operations

Prepare and maintain the content

Key points
  • Use approved, current sources that cover the visitor’s questions.
  • Name an owner for each source and decide when it needs updating.
  • After a content change, check processing and test the answer—not just the source.

The concierge answers only from its configured knowledge sources. If something is not in them, it is designed to say so instead of guessing. Before testing, check that each source is:

  • Approved for the intended use.
  • Accurate and current—prioritize pricing, policies, availability, and links; retest after changes.
  • Relevant to the visitor journey.
  • Clear enough to support the questions visitors are likely to ask.
  • Owned by someone who can maintain it.

Sources can be website links, an AEM Sites Content AI index, or a small product catalog. At least one is required. Processed content is a prerequisite, not a guarantee of answer quality—test that the concierge acknowledges missing information.

If the content changes

The concierge answers from its last processed copy of your content, not from the live site. Here is how three common changes would alter the Premium answer above.

The price on /pricing changes

Affects 2
What happens
Website Links keep the copy of /pricing from the last crawl. Until that source is processed again, Premium is still S$29 to the concierge.
What visitors see
The old price, stated confidently, plus an S$80 difference calculated from it. Nothing in the reply signals that it is out of date.
What to do
Set a refresh schedule (for example, weekly) that matches how often prices change, and reprocess after any price update. Check the last refresh time, then retest pricing questions. Pages on AEM Sites as a Cloud Service update automatically.

/pricing fails to process

Affects 123
What happens
The source shows Partial success: other pages processed, but /pricing did not. The plan features and prices on it are not available.
What visitors see
No price or feature comparison. The reply falls back to the “Not in your content” pattern: it says it doesn’t have that information and offers sales.
What to do
Open the source details and compare succeeded and failed pages. Select Fix Issues to download the failed URLs and their errors, correct or remove the URL, then request reprocessing.

The catalog row is missing

Affects 4
What happens
The pricing page still supplies features and prices (1–3), but nothing in the catalog matches “Premium plan.”
What visitors see
A correct text answer with no product card and no link to /plans/premium, so there is one less step toward signing up.
What to do
Add or update the catalog row when a plan launches, is renamed, or moves to a new URL. Keep its link pointing to a live page, then retest to confirm the card appears.

Brand and trust

Set boundaries and protect visitors

Key points
  • Define the brand voice, supported topics, and response when information is missing.
  • Approve the conversational AI disclosure and how visitor data and consent are handled.
  • Limit editing to named owners; record and retest changes before release.

05.1Voice and limits

Configure the experience and its boundaries

Decide how the experience communicates and what it does at a limit. Consider:

  • The desired brand voice and response style.
  • Topics it is intended to handle.
  • Topics it should defer or decline.
  • How it should respond when a question is unclear or the required information is missing.
  • When it should offer a human handoff or another next step.
Do

Acknowledge the limitExplain what is missing and offer a configured next step, such as contacting sales.

Avoid

Making unsupported commitmentsDo not promise custom prices, discounts, or legal terms that approved content does not support.

Involve brand, privacy, legal, and compliance reviewers where needed. Product configuration does not replace your own policy review (05.2).

Example instruction · Review before applying

Make the limit useful

“Explain plan differences using approved information. Ask a clarifying question when the visitor’s needs are unclear. Do not promise custom pricing or legal terms. If a requested detail is missing, say so and offer the configured consultation route.”

05.2Trust and control

Protect visitors and control changes

Three responsibilities sit with your organization, not the product:

  • Telling visitors how the conversation works.
  • Handling their data under your policies.
  • Controlling who can change what the concierge says.
Required before launch

Add a conversational AI disclosure

Adobe makes customers responsible for a short in-conversation disclosure. Adapt the wording to your brand, but keep all three points:

  1. 1It is generative AI. Responses are generated, not written by a person.
  2. 2Conversations may be reviewed. You and your service providers may access them to improve and personalize the experience.
  3. 3Continuing means agreeing. Using the conversation means agreeing to this processing.

Privacy and consent: who handles what

Documented by Adobe
  • GDPR and CCPA compliance, with processing on Adobe’s infrastructure
  • Encryption and audit logging for conversations
  • Data residency for personal data collected in a conversation
  • System-level safety guardrails plus content and profanity filters
Your organization decides
  • Whether your consent banner and preferences cover chat data and analytics
  • How consent strings and data-usage labels apply to events sent to Experience Platform
  • How visitors who opt out of personalization are handled
  • Who may read transcripts, and how they are handled when visitors share personal details
  • Legal and compliance review before go-live

Access and change control

Manage Brand Concierge is currently the only Brand Concierge permission—no view-only tier—so whoever holds it can change what visitors hear.

Do

Limit access and record changesGive named editors access only to the sandboxes they need. Record what changed, why, who approved it, and the previous value.

Avoid

Releasing untested changesRetest affected questions before publishing changes to instructions, guardrails, or knowledge sources.

The human connection

Plan human handoffs

Key points
  • Decide when to offer a person, who receives the request, and when they are available.
  • Provide a working alternative for after-hours requests or failed connections.
  • Test chat, booking, and fallback routes in preview and on the deployed site.

A handoff needs an operational plan as well as a configured connection. Decide:

  • What visitor needs should lead to a human option.
  • Which team or representative receives the request.
  • When that team is available.
  • What the visitor can do instead if nobody is available or the connection fails.
  • What information the receiving team needs to continue the conversation.

Part 3Launch and prove valueTesting · Path to launch · Measure and improve · Readiness

Quality before scale

Test realistic questions and edge cases

Key points
  • Test common questions, missing or conflicting information, limits, safeguards, and handoffs.
  • Your team starts evaluation runs and reviews the recorded results—not just the score.
  • Fix failures, add real visitor questions, and retest after meaningful changes.

Test how visitors actually behave—including unclear, unsupported, or sensitive questions. A useful evaluation set covers seven types:

Test typeExampleWhat to review
Common questionA visitor asks about a product or service.Is the response relevant and supported by approved information?
Ambiguous request"Which option is best for me?"Does the experience ask for useful clarification rather than making unsupported assumptions?
Missing informationA visitor asks for a detail not covered by the content.Does the experience acknowledge the gap appropriately?
Conflicting informationTwo sources appear to provide different details.Does the response avoid presenting uncertain information as fact, and does the team address the content issue?
Out-of-scope questionA visitor asks about an unrelated topic.Does the experience defer or redirect appropriately?
Sensitive or adversarial inputA visitor requests restricted information or tries to bypass the experience's boundaries.Does the response stay within the organization's approved safeguards?
Handoff scenarioA visitor asks to speak to a person.Does the route work, and is there a clear fallback if nobody is available?

Review results yourself rather than relying on an automated score alone, and add real visitor questions over time.

Recorded by the product · Reviewed by your team

Repeatable testing—not a manual results spreadsheet

Your team starts the run
Maintain approved questions and expected answers. Select Run Evaluation, choose the set, and select Run.
The product records the results
It asks each question, compares actual and expected answers, and records the overall score, flagged answers, and high / medium / low ratings with reasoning.
Your team makes the decision
Open past runs in Evaluation Results to track changes over time. Review flagged answers, fix the cause, and rerun before launch and after meaningful content or configuration changes.

Adobe documents user-initiated runs; do not assume automatic evaluation scheduling. A score supports review—it does not replace approval.

Functional
Check relevant answers and useful clarification. If weak, fix content coverage, instructions, or skill routing and retest.
Out of scope
Check appropriate deferral or redirection. If weak, clarify scope and instructions. Missing information about a supported product is a content gap—not automatically out of scope.
Safeguard
Check approved boundaries with brand, privacy, legal, or compliance owners. Resolve failures before broader deployment and rerun affected and related cases.

These are Adobe’s three evaluation categories; corrective actions are recommended practice.

From preview to production

Path to launch

Key points
  • Connect the reporting datastream, install script, and page-matching surface rules.
  • The concierge appears only where the script is installed and a surface rule matches.
  • Test on staging, then limit the initial audience and expand when checks pass.

What you set up

Where does activity go?

Datastream

Sends visitor activity—clicks, form submissions, booked meetings, and live chats—to Adobe for reporting. Use a separate datastream for each environment.

How does it load?

Install script

A component snippet on existing pages, or a ready-to-host full page. The website team installs it in page code or through a tag manager.

Where does it appear?

Surface rules

The concierge owner sets domain and path rules in Composer—any page, starts with, ends with, or equals—to include or leave out pages.

Creating a concierge, previewing it, or sharing a test link does not make it live on production pages.

Control where—and how many visitors—see it

Page scope · Product configuration
Surface rules select domains and paths. Start with a narrow page scope, then add pages when ready.
Traffic share · Recommended practice
A tag-manager rule or testing tool such as Adobe Target can split visitors. Configure this separately so the share can change without a website code release; surface rules alone are not a traffic-percentage control.

Staging → sign-off → small share → full rollout

  1. Staging

    Internal only

    Install the script on staging or internal pages and point surface rules there. Test end to end.

    Ready when: staging deployment and acceptance checks pass.
  2. Sign-off

    Record the go decision

    Every go-live checklist item is complete or has an owner. Brand and legal approve; the responsible owner explicitly accepts any open risk.

    Ready when: the go decision is recorded.
  3. ~5% of intended traffic

    Start small in production

    Install the script on production pages, add production paths to surface rules, and limit traffic using the agreed control.

    Ready to expand when: transcript review finds no blocking issues.
  4. Ramp → 100%

    Increase in steps

    Expand the share over about a week, checking Analytics and answer ratings at each increase.

    Complete when: full intended traffic is reached.
If something goes wrong · Recommended practice

Reduce exposure, fix, and retest

Lower the traffic share, remove affected pages from surface rules, or pause the tag. Fix the cause, rerun evaluations, then resume the gradual rollout.

Deployment, surface configuration, and readiness checks follow Adobe documentation. The Adobe FAQ describes a staged rollout from approximately 5% to 100% over about a week—not a mandatory schedule. Traffic-splitting and rollback methods are recommended practices, not Adobe requirements.

Prove it

Measure and improve

Key points
  • Choose one visitor outcome, track it, and compare with a website baseline or control.
  • Use reports and transcripts to explain results; chat activity alone does not prove value.
  • Turn findings into fixes, retest, and check outcomes and safety before expanding.

More conversations do not necessarily mean more value. Measure whether visitors complete the task the concierge is meant to help with.

09.3Find the evidence

From the headline to the conversation

Where to review conversation logs

  1. Open your concierge
  2. Analytics
  3. Select a conversation
  4. Read the full transcript

Set the date range for the conversations you want to review; optionally filter by conversation type. Inspect what the visitor asked, how the concierge replied, and any thumbs-up / thumbs-down ratings.

Turn real questions into repeatable tests

Select Add to Evaluation in a transcript to add the conversation to an evaluation set. Use real visitor questions to check whether later content or configuration changes improve the answer without breaking something that already worked.

Example: a visitor asks, “Can eight people use Premium, and what is the monthly total?” Keep this case and rerun it after a price update. Check that the answer uses the current price, calculates the total correctly, and states the billing terms. Include successful questions as well as failures.

A conversation ID is a reference—not a download

The ID uniquely identifies a conversation. If another system also stores that ID, your team can use it to connect the transcript with a related record. This requires supported integration or tracking; it is not automatic.

Example: if a consultation-booking record contains the same conversation ID, match it to the transcript to investigate whether the visitor reached a confirmed booking. The ID alone does not export the transcript or guarantee that booking data is linked.

Analytics and the CJA starter report are provisioned after deployment. Custom rates and business outcomes need verified tracking; they are not guaranteed starter tiles. Sentiment is not explicit feedback, recommendations are not purchases, and longer conversations are not necessarily better.

Two supporting checks—not business-performance reports

Evaluation Results · Answer correctness
After meaningful content or configuration changes, run the affected tests and compare results with earlier runs. See the evaluation workflow.
Knowledge-source details · Processing and freshness
The product shows status, succeeded / failed pages, and last refresh time. Your team reviews these after updates or refreshes. Fix Issues downloads failed URLs and errors—it does not repair them. Correct the source, upload the corrected URL list if applicable, and request reprocessing.

Processed does not mean current or correct. Website Links can use scheduled refresh; native AEM Sites sources update automatically. Still check important changes in the answer. Automatic stale-content alerts are not established by the documentation.

09.5Optimize continuously

Turn evidence into the next improvement

Continuous optimization · Start with a weekly review

Review → fix → retest → decide

Missing or stale answers→ improve content
Update the approved source, check processing or refresh, and retest the affected questions.
Confusing answers or routing→ refine behavior
Clarify skill intents, instructions, and boundaries. Check that the right skill handles the question without making unsupported promises.
Failed next steps→ repair handoffs
Check links, integrations, representative availability, and booking fallback. Test the whole visitor path—not just the reply.
New visitor needs→ strengthen testing
Use Add to Evaluation from transcripts. Include successful, unanswered, and difficult questions—not only negative ratings.

Close the loop: Review performance weekly, check sources after updates or refreshes, and rerun affected tests after meaningful changes. Keep an action log for issue, owner, fix, and closure; compare outcomes and safeguards before expanding. Pause expansion while serious answer, privacy, or handoff issues remain.
