Purpose & principles

Purpose and intended outcomes

This guide describes how teams can organize and sustain work with Adobe Brand Visibility (ABV). It focuses on the people, decisions, governance, and change practices needed to turn ABV insights into prioritized actions and evaluate their impact.

Two complementary disciplines

The operating model clarifies who is involved, how decisions are made, and how work moves across teams. Change management helps people understand, adopt, and sustain the agreed ways of working.

This is a flexible framework, not a prescribed organization design. Adapt it to the customer's structure, objectives, and existing governance.

Intended outcomes

An effective ABV operating model and change approach should help teams:

  • 01 · Business alignment

    Align ABV work to business priorities and relevant customer journeys.

  • 02 · Clear ownership

    Establish clear ownership, decision rights, and cross-functional collaboration.

  • 03 · Insight to action

    Move from insight to approved action, implementation, and review.

  • 04 · People adoption

    Build the sponsorship, communication, and capabilities needed for adoption.

  • 05 · Sustained operations

    Sustain the work through an agreed governance cadence and transition to business as usual.

Guiding principles

  • Start with the business objective.

    Define what the team wants to improve before selecting measures or actions.

  • Use evidence to guide decisions.

    Connect recommendations to ABV insights, customer priorities, and an agreed measurement approach.

  • Make ownership explicit.

    Every decision and action should have a clear owner.

  • Work across functions.

    Include the business, brand, content, GEO/SEO, web, analytics, and other relevant teams.

  • Use existing processes where possible.

    Align ABV reviews and resulting changes with established planning, approval, release, and reporting practices.

  • Adapt the model.

    Governance should provide enough clarity and control without adding unnecessary overhead.

Operating approach

Choose an operating approach

Organizations may use centralized, federated, or decentralized ways of working. The right approach depends on factors such as the number of business units, the level of local autonomy, available capabilities, and the need for shared oversight.

  • Centralized:

    A central team coordinates priorities, standards, and decisions; delivery teams carry out agreed work.

  • Federated:

    A central team sets shared direction and governance while business units or markets plan and execute locally.

  • Decentralized:

    Individual teams lead their own work within shared enterprise principles and agreed guardrails.

Design for your organization

The operating approach may evolve as ABV work matures. The essential requirement is clarity about who coordinates, who decides, and who executes.

Assign responsibilities based on the customer's organization. One person may cover more than one role.

Clarify the decision path

For each recurring decision, clarify who recommends, approves, executes, and reviews. For example, identify who can prioritize an opportunity, who approves a proposed change, and who confirms that the change was implemented and assessed.

ABV work cycle

Use a consistent ABV work cycle

Use a repeatable cycle to connect ABV insights to action:

01 · Frame the work

Focus, scope, objectives, and evidence

  1. Set the focus. Identify the product, business priority, and customer journey the team will address.
  2. Select the GEO loop. Agree on the scope of the work and the question the team wants to answer.
  3. Choose the objective and KPIs. Select relevant goals and measures from the GEO metrics funnel in the step-by-step guide. The appropriate measures will vary by product and customer journey. Use the sample reports for examples of how results can be presented.
  4. Establish the measurement approach. Agree on the starting point, the measures to review, and the owners responsible for interpreting results.
02 · Act and learn

Priorities, decisions, delivery, and review

  1. Prioritize opportunities. Review available evidence, assess relevance and feasibility, and identify the actions that merit consideration.
  2. Make and document decisions. Record the decision, rationale, approver, owner, and any dependencies.
  3. Implement approved changes. Route work through the customer's established content, approval, and release processes.
  4. Review and learn. Assess results against the agreed objective and measures. Capture lessons and decide whether to continue, adjust, or expand the work.
The loop is a decision discipline

The goal is not to change every cycle. It is to make decisions and next steps clear, traceable, and aligned to the agreed objective.

Governance & cadence

Establish governance and a working cadence

Use existing forums where they can support ABV decisions. Add a dedicated review only when current forums do not provide the required ownership or decision-making.

A governance review should cover:

  • The current objective, GEO loop, and relevant customer journey.
  • Key insights and proposed priorities.
  • Decisions required, including the decision owner.
  • Actions in progress, accountable owners, and dependencies.
  • Adoption or implementation barriers.
  • Measurement updates and the next review point.
Keep follow-through visible

Set the review frequency according to the pace of the work and the customer's planning cycle. Keep a shared record of decisions, actions, owners, and outcomes so that teams can follow through between reviews.

Change & adoption

Plan for change and adoption

Change management should begin when the work is planned—not after changes are ready to launch.

  • Secure sponsorship.

    Confirm a leader who can explain why the work matters and help resolve barriers.

  • Identify impacted teams.

    Understand which roles or workflows will change and involve relevant stakeholders early.

  • Communicate clearly.

    Explain the purpose, expected changes, timing, responsibilities, and where people can ask questions.

  • Enable people for their roles.

    Provide focused guidance, examples, or working sessions that help each group perform its part of the process.

  • Check readiness.

    Confirm that owners, approvals, capacity, and supporting processes are in place before scaling changes.

  • Gather feedback.

    Provide a way for teams to raise issues and share what is or is not working.

  • Reinforce adoption.

    Use review forums and leadership communication to recognize progress, address gaps, and sustain the agreed practices.

Measurement

Measure progress

Keep business outcomes and operating effectiveness distinct.

Outcomes

ABV and business measures:

For each GEO loop, select the relevant objective and KPIs from the GEO metrics funnel in the step-by-step guide. The measures should reflect the product and customer journey in scope.

Ways of working

Operating and adoption measures:

Where useful, review whether teams are following through on decisions, completing agreed actions, engaging the necessary stakeholders, and using the agreed process. Select measures that help the team improve; do not add metrics without a clear purpose.

Agree the review contract

Agree in advance who will review each measure, how often it will be discussed, and how the results will influence the next decision. Do not assume that one set of KPIs or thresholds applies to every product, journey, or customer.

Pilot & handover

Pilot, learn, and transition to ongoing operations

Begin with a focused scope that is meaningful to the business and manageable for the participating teams. Before expanding the work, review:

  • Whether roles and decision rights were clear.
  • Whether the right stakeholders participated.
  • Whether actions moved through the agreed process.
  • Whether the measures supported useful decisions.
  • What should be adjusted before the next GEO loop or broader rollout.
Transition to ongoing operations

When the work becomes part of ongoing operations, confirm the accountable business owner, recurring governance forum, action-tracking approach, measurement responsibilities, and onboarding approach for new participants. Document lessons and update the working approach as business priorities or team structures change.
