Overview

What Adobe Brand Visibility measures

Adobe's current documentation refers to LLM Optimizer as Adobe Brand Visibility. The capabilities and workflow are the same.

The core visibility signal combines mentions, citations, sentiment, and position. The wider operating model also includes competitor benchmarking, agentic traffic, referral traffic, and opportunity tracking.

A key nuance: the tool measures a configured prompt set, so score movement should be read as a trend across comparable prompts — not as a real-time or universal industry ranking.

Metrics & prompts

Key metrics and how they map to prompts

There is no universal "good" visibility score. The most useful benchmarks are your own baseline, competitor performance, and business outcomes for the same prompt set.

MetricWhat it tells youHow to apply it to prompts
Visibility ScoreOverall presence and prominence in AI-generated answersTrack by category, topic, platform, region, and branded / non-branded prompt
MentionsHow often the brand appearsIdentify prompts where the brand is absent or inconsistently represented
CitationsHow often the brand's own content is used as a sourcePrioritize prompts where competitors are cited but your site is not
SentimentWhether the brand is represented positively, neutrally, or negativelyReview negative or inaccurate responses and the sources influencing them
PositionHow prominently the brand appears in an answerAim to improve prominence on high-value prompts, not just inclusion
Competitor visibilityRelative share of voice against selected brandsCompare competitors on the same topics and prompts
Agentic trafficHow AI agents crawl and access your contentIdentify pages that are difficult for AI systems to discover or read
Referral traffic & outcomesWhether AI visibility produces visits, engagement, conversions, or revenueConnect prompt and citation improvements to downstream business value

Use a stable core prompt set for trend reporting and a separate exploratory set for new topics. Each prompt should be tagged to a business intent, topic, category, target URL, region, platform, owner, and KPI.

Operating model

How leading teams use the tool

The most effective pattern is a continuous cycle:

Measure Prioritize Optimize Validate

A continuous loop — repeat as you expand coverage to new topics and regions.

  1. Start with high-value customer topics, products, or journeys.
  2. Build prompts from real search demand, site search, FAQs, customer questions, and Adobe's Industry Prompt Library.
  3. Benchmark against competitors and identify prompts where competitors are cited but the brand is missing.
  4. Use the Opportunities and URL Inspector views to find content, structure, schema, crawlability, or readability issues.
  5. Deploy a focused set of improvements.
  6. Re-run the same prompts and validate the change using visibility, citations, agentic traffic, referral traffic, and business metrics.

What works best is starting narrow — proving improvement on a priority topic or product area, then expanding to additional regions, categories, or business units. Teams get more value when SEO, content, PR, analytics, and web teams share ownership rather than treating LLM visibility as a standalone SEO metric.

Monitoring cadence

Recommended monitoring cadence

Weekly

Review material movers across priority prompts, topics, competitors, sentiment, citations, and referral traffic.

Monthly

A deeper review using a four-week trend: opportunities completed, visibility changes, traffic impact, and business outcomes.

After an optimization

Use the impact measurement process and allow ~2 weeks for an initial read. Use a longer multi-week trend before broad investment or strategy decisions.

Quarterly

Refresh the prompt library, competitors, categories, regions, and business priorities. Retire prompts that are no longer relevant.

Avoid reacting to a single answer or one-day movement. LLM outputs vary naturally, so base decisions on repeated results across a prompt or topic cluster.

Alerting

If alerting is available in your tenant, configure alerts for:

  • A sustained drop in visibility or owned citations on priority topics.
  • Negative sentiment or inaccurate brand claims.
  • A high-value URL losing agentic access or citations.
  • A competitor overtaking the brand on strategic prompts.
  • Significant changes in AI referral traffic or conversion performance.

Don't set alert thresholds from a generic industry benchmark. Set them against your own baseline and require confirmation across repeated measurements.

Reading score changes

How to interpret score changes

ChangeLikely interpretationRecommended action
Visibility, citations, and referral traffic all improveStrong evidence of meaningful progressValidate across related prompts, then scale the optimization
Visibility improves but citations remain flatThe brand is being mentioned, but owned content is not becoming the sourceReview content authority, page accessibility, structure, and competitor citations
Visibility drops across all platformsPossible site, content, crawlability, configuration, or broad model changeCheck URL Inspector, agentic traffic, recent releases, CDN / robots settings, and prompt configuration
Visibility drops on only one platform or regionPlatform, model, language, or local-content variationSegment the issue before changing the global strategy
Mentions increase but sentiment worsensGreater visibility may be exposing an accuracy or reputation problemReview the underlying AI responses and third-party sources; involve PR or communications
Agentic traffic increases but visibility is flatAI systems may be accessing more content before the score catches upReview page-level access and citations, then monitor the next measurement cycle
One prompt improves but the topic does notThe result may be phrase-specific or noisyValidate across the broader topic cluster before declaring success

The key principle: optimize for accurate, prominent, owned citations and measurable business impact — not simply a higher score.
