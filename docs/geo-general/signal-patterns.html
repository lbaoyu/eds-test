What this is

An exhaustive library of the signals you can read

A reference catalogue of 97 patterns you can detect in Adobe Brand Visibility (formerly LLM Optimizer) — per dashboard (Brand Presence, the URL Inspector, Agentic Traffic, Referral Traffic) and across dashboards, where the real story lives.

Use it as a reference

When you’re diagnosing a customer journey, find the pattern that matches what you’re seeing, read what it usually means, and reach for the typical actions.

Use it as a workshop playbook

Pick a pattern → validate it in the product → agree the actions. The catalogue gives the whole team a shared vocabulary for AI visibility.

Area 1 of 6

Brand Presence

22 patterns

How AI systems mention, cite and rank your brand across topics — visibility, share of voice, sentiment and narrative.

Coverage & Share of Voice

6 patterns

Where demand is high but you’re absent — and where you already own the AI shelf.

01

High popularity, low visibility

How to spot it

Topic has high Popularity, low Visibility Score (often across regions)

What it usually means

High user demand; you rarely show up in AI answers

Typical actions

Prioritise content and prompts for these topics; improve discoverability and authority.

02

High visibility, high mentions, high owned citations

How to spot it

Visibility, Mentions, and Owned Citations all high

What it usually means

You are a strong recommended option and trusted source

Typical actions

Maintain freshness; keep narrative and facts aligned; use as benchmark for weaker topics.

03

High brand mentions, low owned citations

How to spot it

Mentions high, Owned Citations low; 3rd-party sites dominate citations

What it usually means

AI talks about you but relies on other sites for evidence

Typical actions

Create/strengthen your own authoritative pages; improve schema and structure so your URLs are chosen as sources.

04

High share of voice but low prompt position

How to spot it

Good Share of Voice, but Prompt Position mostly outside top-3

What it usually means

You show up often but too low to influence decisions

Typical actions

Make answers easier to quote: concise summaries, clearer value prop, better headings and structure.

05

Low share of voice vs specific competitor

How to spot it

Across a topic, competitor has higher Share of Voice and citations

What it usually means

Competitor is winning AI "shelf space" in that journey

Typical actions

Analyse cited competitor pages; close content gaps; improve your own coverage and external sources.

06

Visibility concentrated in a few topics

How to spot it

High Visibility Score in few topics, very low in others

What it usually means

You're over-indexed on a narrow slice of your business

Typical actions

Expand prompts & content to adjacent journeys; use strong topics as templates.

Intent, Region & Stability

6 patterns

Branded vs generic prompts, regional skew, and visibility that shifts over time.

07

Strong on branded, weak on non-branded

How to spot it

Good visibility when questions use your brand; weak on generic prompts

What it usually means

You're visible when users already know you, invisible for discovery

Typical actions

Create content and prompts for generic "category" / competitor prompts; broaden Topic design.

08

Strong on non-branded, weak on branded

How to spot it

Strong presence on category prompts; weaker on brand-name prompts

What it usually means

Brand naming/messaging is fragmented or under-represented

Typical actions

Standardise brand naming/aliases; ensure brand pages are clear canonical sources.

09

Visibility skewed to one region

How to spot it

High Visibility in some regions, low in others

What it usually means

AI recognises you locally but not globally

Typical actions

Localise key content & prompts; ensure regional domains and language variants are well structured.

10

Rapid drop or spike in visibility over time

How to spot it

Visibility Score time series shows sudden change for a topic/region

What it usually means

Likely model update, big content change, or competitor move

Typical actions

Check release logs & content deployments; re-evaluate key prompts; adjust narrative/content if needed.

11

Topics with high "no show" rate

How to spot it

Many prompts in a Topic where you're neither mentioned nor cited

What it usually means

Clear coverage gap for that journey

Typical actions

Treat as backlog for net-new content & prompts; prioritise by Popularity.

12

Topic structure noisy or overlapping

New
How to spot it

Topics are very broad or overlapping; patterns hard to interpret

What it usually means

Visibility/SoV signals are diluted by poor Topic design

Typical actions

Re-organise Topics by user journey stages/intent; keep prompts under each topic coherent.

Sentiment & Narrative

5 patterns

How AI frames you — tone, differentiation, and who shapes the story.

13

Positive mentions, negative or neutral sentiment

How to spot it

Mentions present; Sentiment neutral/negative

What it usually means

AI knows you but frames you weakly or negatively

Typical actions

Address root causes: product FAQ, policy clarity, reviews; update on-site content and key off-site sources.

14

Neutral sentiment where differentiation matters

How to spot it

High visibility, mostly neutral tone across prompts

What it usually means

You're framed as "just another option"

Typical actions

Strengthen value props, comparisons, and proof points; add content driving clear preference.

15

UGC / third-party sources dominate citations

How to spot it

Low Owned Citations, multiple UGC/3rd-party domains heavily cited

What it usually means

Conversation about you happens off your domains

Typical actions

Improve your own source pages; maintain key partner/UGC pages so they're accurate and aligned.

16

Sentiment differs sharply by region

New
How to spot it

Positive in one market, negative/neutral in another

What it usually means

Local differences in product, service, or external coverage

Typical actions

Investigate local causes; adjust local content, support pages, and key external sites.

17

Volatile sentiment over time

New
How to spot it

Sentiment oscillates strongly for a topic

What it usually means

Ongoing product/experience noise or unstable narrative

Typical actions

Stabilise core messaging; ensure recent issues are acknowledged and resolved in official content.

Advanced signals

5 patterns

Deeper narrative, alias, head-to-head and staleness signals.

18

Over-indexed on support/FAQ content

How to spot it

Most citations for a topic point to help/FAQ/support docs instead of product/solution pages

What it usually means

AI frames you mainly through troubleshooting and issues

Typical actions

Create more product/solution explainer content; ensure help docs link to higher-level overviews; rebalance which URLs are canonical for key journeys.

19

Brand "alias" confusion

How to spot it

Same brand is referenced with multiple names/aliases across prompts/platforms

What it usually means

AI doesn't understand your brand naming consistently

Typical actions

Standardise naming in site copy and metadata; ensure redirects & canonicals consolidate old names; adjust prompts to use the preferred brand label.

20

Head-to-head prompts where you always lose rank

How to spot it

For prompts that mention you and a competitor, your visibility is consistently lower or you appear later in the answer

What it usually means

In direct comparisons, AI treats competitor as the default/better choice

Typical actions

Analyse competitor-cited pages; add clearer comparison content and differentiated proof points; improve off-site sources that shape those comparisons.

21

Platform-specific narrative drift

How to spot it

Same Topic across platforms (filter by Platform) has noticeably different visibility/sentiment

What it usually means

Different AI platforms are telling different stories about you

Typical actions

Identify which external sources differ by platform; align content and off-site presence with each platform's ecosystem where it matters most.

22

Legacy product or pricing still present in answers

How to spot it

Answers mention discontinued products, old plans, outdated pricing (visible in example answers or cited URLs)

What it usually means

AI is relying on stale content and hasn't fully updated its mental model

Typical actions

Update or deprecate old docs; add clear "superseded" messaging; ensure new canonical pages for current offers are strong and easy to cite.

Area 2 of 6

URL Inspector

16 patterns

The page-level view — how individual URLs are cited, fetched and converted.

Hero vs Supporting Pages

5 patterns

Which URLs power answers, and which merely get tested.

23

Hero URL (high Times Cited + many Prompts)

How to spot it

URL has high Times Cited and Prompts Cited In

What it usually means

Primary AI answer source for multiple prompts

Typical actions

Protect and refine this page; use its structure as a template for other journeys.

24

Zero-click answer page

How to spot it

High Agentic hits, low AI-assistant referrals

What it usually means

Page powers answers without driving clicks

Typical actions

Ensure content accuracy & clarity; add unique value that could justify a visit if desired.

25

High referrals but modest Times Cited

How to spot it

Moderate citations, strong AI-origin traffic

What it usually means

When it appears, this URL is an effective landing page

Typical actions

Treat as a key AI landing page; optimise for conversion and trust.

26

Many Citation Attempts, few Times Cited

How to spot it

Citation Attempts high, Times Cited low

What it usually means

AI checks this page often but prefers others

Typical actions

Improve content quality, clarity, and alignment with topical intent; fix readability/technical issues.

27

Multiple similar URLs (canonical confusion)

How to spot it

Several URLs share similar metrics for same journey

What it usually means

AI and humans see duplicates or unclear canonical

Typical actions

Consolidate or canonicalise; clean internal linking and navigation.

Technical & Regional

5 patterns

Readability, legacy URLs and region-specific citation.

28

Low readability + low citations

How to spot it

Readability score poor, low Times Cited

What it usually means

AI has trouble parsing/using the page

Typical actions

Improve HTML structure, headings, and text clarity; reduce heavy client-side rendering.

29

Legacy URL still cited more than new URL

How to spot it

Old URL has higher Times Cited vs new canonical

What it usually means

AI relies on outdated or legacy content

Typical actions

Ensure redirects are clean; update sitemaps; unify internal/external links to new URL.

30

High citations only in a specific region

How to spot it

URL is heavily cited in one market only

What it usually means

Region-specific relevance or localisation

Typical actions

Replicate content/structure for other regions where the journey matters.

31

High citations to low-priority URLs

How to spot it

Many citations to non-strategic pages

What it usually means

AI optimises for content that isn't business-critical

Typical actions

Adjust IA; create better "answer" pages for those intents and guide AI via links and structure.

32

URLs with agentic hits but no matching prompts

How to spot it

URL has agentic hits; not present in current Topics/Prompts

What it usually means

AI found useful content beyond your tracked journeys

Typical actions

Add new Prompts/Topics that reflect the user intents this URL serves.

Advanced Page-Level

6 patterns

Repurposed pages, hidden section value and CTA overload.

33

Used for "unexpected" prompts

How to spot it

Prompts Cited In show intents that differ from the page's design (e.g., product page used for policy questions)

What it usually means

AI is repurposing a page for journeys it wasn't designed for

Typical actions

Decide whether to split content into dedicated pages, add clearer sections, or discourage that use via better alternatives.

34

Multi-topic utility URL

How to spot it

URL is cited in multiple Topics with different intents but still performs well (citations and referrals)

What it usually means

A strong hub or explainer that works across journeys

Typical actions

Keep as a high-quality hub; ensure content is clearly structured with sections for each intent.

35

Readability vs conversion trade-off

How to spot it

URL with very structured text and high readability has good citations but weak conversions, or vice versa

What it usually means

Content optimised for AI and content optimised for humans diverge

Typical actions

Test content variations: keep machine-readable structure but strengthen human-oriented messaging and design.

36

"Sleeper" URLs discovered by AI first

How to spot it

URL gains citations and agentic hits before you actively promote or link it in navigation

What it usually means

AI has surfaced a useful page earlier than your own funnel

Typical actions

Evaluate and, if appropriate, promote this URL in your main IA or campaigns.

37

Section-level value hidden in a long page

New
How to spot it

Long page has strong citations but many users scroll only part-way before exiting

What it usually means

Only part of the content matches the AI-driven intent

Typical actions

Consider splitting into multiple focused pages or adding clear jump links and in-page navigation to key sections.

38

URL over-loaded with multiple CTAs

New
How to spot it

URL has good citations and traffic but poor conversion on any one goal

What it usually means

Page tries to serve too many business goals for an AI-driven visit

Typical actions

Simplify or segment the page: prioritise one main CTA for AI visitors; move secondary goals to follow-up pages.

Area 3 of 6

Agentic Traffic

23 patterns

How AI crawlers and agents reach your site — coverage, errors, performance and the URLs moving up or down.

Coverage & Errors

6 patterns

Whether agents can reach you at all — blocks, 4xx/5xx and slow pages.

39

Near-zero agentic traffic

How to spot it

Very few agent hits across the board

What it usually means

AI agents are not discovering or crawling your site

Typical actions

Check robots.txt / llms.txt, CDN/firewall blocks, basic indexability; ensure production domains are crawlable.

40

Agents hit robots.txt but not pages

How to spot it

High /robots.txt hits, almost no content requests

What it usually means

Rules likely block or confuse agents

Typical actions

Simplify robots rules; ensure relevant AI agents are allowed on key paths.

41

High 4xx rates (404, 410)

How to spot it

Large share of 4xx in status distribution; specific paths spike

What it usually means

Agents request outdated or broken URLs

Typical actions

Fix internal/external links; add redirects; clean up legacy paths.

42

High 5xx error rates

How to spot it

Many 5xx responses for agent traffic

What it usually means

Infrastructure / app issues impacting bots

Typical actions

Investigate availability, throttling, DDoS/bot rules; improve resilience.

43

Very high TTFB on some URLs

How to spot it

Average TTFB far above baseline for a subset of pages

What it usually means

Pages are slow for agents; crawl depth limited

Typical actions

Optimise server performance, caching, and heavy queries; test with headless clients.

44

Agents hitting non-HTML endpoints heavily

How to spot it

Many hits on JSON/API endpoints instead of HTML

What it usually means

Crawl budget wasted on low-value responses

Typical actions

Block or de-prioritise non-useful endpoints; promote clean HTML pages for answers.

Coverage vs Content

6 patterns

What agents find versus what matters, and where crawl budget goes.

45

Top SEO/paid pages missing from agentic traffic

How to spot it

High-value pages (known from analytics) have few/no agent hits

What it usually means

AI agents don't discover or understand your most important pages

Typical actions

Check internal links, sitemaps, canonical tags, rendering; ensure bots see full content.

46

Agentic traffic concentrated on a few URLs

How to spot it

Small subset of URLs gets most agent hits

What it usually means

Emerging AI hero pages

Typical actions

Invest in content/UX on these URLs; consider creating similar "answer-shaped" pages.

47

Strong agentic traffic on staging/non-prod

How to spot it

Agent hits on stage/UAT domains

What it usually means

AI may read staging content; risk of leaks/confusion

Typical actions

Block non-prod envs; ensure production is clearly preferred.

48

One AI platform generates most agent hits

How to spot it

Agent distribution shows one provider ≫ others

What it usually means

That platform's index heavily influences your AI presence

Typical actions

Validate you meet that platform's technical guidance; track changes from that platform closely.

49

Large differences across regions

How to spot it

High agentic activity in some GEOs, low in others

What it usually means

Regional tech, domain, or localisation differences matter

Typical actions

Check local domains, hreflang, CDNs; align technical setup with top regions.

50

High agentic hits, low citations in Brand Presence

How to spot it

URL shows many agent hits but low Times Cited

What it usually means

AI checks content but rarely selects it as a source

Typical actions

Improve clarity, factualness, and structure; compare with competing sources.

Time & Movers

5 patterns

Sudden drops and spikes, and the URLs gaining or losing AI attention.

51

Sudden drop in agentic traffic

How to spot it

Trend shows steep decline for key URLs

What it usually means

Something changed: robots, routing, infra, or model/platform

Typical actions

Check deployment logs, robots/redirects, platform announcements; restore access conditions.

52

Sudden spike in agentic traffic

How to spot it

Trend shows sharp increase

What it usually means

Pages became more relevant or newly discovered

Typical actions

Validate the content; ensure performance and structure are solid; consider linking to more supporting pages.

53

Movers: big positive jumps

How to spot it

"Movers" list shows URLs with rapid growth in agent hits

What it usually means

New or updated pages are gaining AI attention

Typical actions

Double down on these pages; check if they start appearing in Brand Presence & Referral Traffic.

54

Movers: big negative drops

How to spot it

"Movers" show key URLs losing agentic hits

What it usually means

Recent change or de-prioritisation by agents

Typical actions

Compare content/technical changes; decide whether to revert, fix, or accept the shift.

55

Agentic hits but no tracked prompts

New
How to spot it

URL seen in Agentic Traffic, not associated with any Brand Presence prompts

What it usually means

AI found relevant content outside your current tracked journeys

Typical actions

Use these URLs as candidates to expand Topics/Prompts.

Advanced Crawl & Behaviour

6 patterns

Crawl waste, retries, gated areas and depth problems.

56

Excessive hits on parameterised URLs

How to spot it

Same base URL appears many times with different query params / tracking codes

What it usually means

Crawl budget is wasted on variants; canonical unclear

Typical actions

Normalise or canonicalise param URLs; block non-essential params; ensure a clean canonical URL is easy to find.

57

Frequent retries on the same URLs

How to spot it

Same URLs show repeated attempts in a short time window, often mixed status codes

What it usually means

Agents struggle with intermittent errors, timeouts, or throttling

Typical actions

Stabilise infra and rate-limiting; ensure bots see consistent 2xx responses; test from typical agent IP ranges.

58

Agents hitting authenticated / paywalled areas repeatedly

How to spot it

Significant agent hits to /login, gated paths, or 401/403 responses

What it usually means

Bots are trying to access content they can't reach; signals wasted

Typical actions

Explicitly disallow secured/gated areas in robots/llms rules; provide public summaries of what's behind the gate if needed.

59

Heavy agent traffic on low-value boilerplate pages

How to spot it

Many hits to generic template pages (e.g., generic legal or structural pages) with low relevance to user questions

What it usually means

Bots are spending time on pages that don't add much answer value

Typical actions

Improve internal linking so topical pages are emphasised; demote boilerplate in navigation; use structured data to highlight key content.

60

Crawl depth drops on certain paths

How to spot it

Early URLs in a path are hit; deeper path levels rarely seen

What it usually means

IA or performance discourages deep crawl; important nested content under-exposed

Typical actions

Flatten IA where possible; add internal links from high-value pages to deeper content; improve performance on deeper paths.

61

Strong agentic traffic but inconsistent over time

How to spot it

Same URLs oscillate between high and low hits without clear content changes

What it usually means

Platform-side experimentation or unstable technical conditions

Typical actions

Correlate with platform announcements and infra changes; monitor for stability; ensure you're not introducing intermittent blocks.

How to spot it

One platform supplies most LLM-classified visits

What it usually means

You have a primary AI distribution channel (and dependency)

Prioritise this platform in partnership & roadmap; optimise its key landing pages.

63

High LLM pageviews + high bounce

How to spot it

Top landing URLs: many LLM pageviews, high bounce

What it usually means

Landing experience doesn't match AI answer or user intent

Typical actions

Align copy/headings with question; simplify page layout and next steps.

64

Low volume + high engagement/conversion

How to spot it

Small LLM traffic but high depth or conversion

What it usually means

Niche but high-value AI source

Typical actions

Protect and refine these experiences; test ways to responsibly grow this traffic.

65

LLM traffic underperforms other channels

How to spot it

Lower conversion or engagement than organic/paid

What it usually means

AI brings earlier-funnel or mismatched visitors

Typical actions

Clarify who the page is for; add context and guidance; consider segment-specific variants.

66

LLM traffic outperforms other channels

How to spot it

Higher conversion or engagement than other channels

What it usually means

AI is sending well-qualified, high-intent visitors

Typical actions

Study these journeys; reuse patterns (content, UX, offer) across other channels.

67

Top landing URLs differ from SEO/paid landings

New
How to spot it

LLM landings are different URLs from those used in SEO/paid

What it usually means

AI has discovered alternative "best answer" pages

Typical actions

Evaluate those pages; promote them or align SEO/paid strategies accordingly.

Very low consent rate on LLM visitors

How to spot it

Consent rate much lower for LLM than other channels

What it usually means

Measurement gap; consent UX or tagging issues

Typical actions

Simplify consent UX; fix consent and analytics tagging for AI-origin traffic.

69

Strong skew to mobile or desktop

How to spot it

LLM visitors mostly on one device type

What it usually means

AI traffic has a distinct device profile

Typical actions

Optimise UX/performance for the dominant device; test device-specific layouts.

70

LLM traffic concentrated in a few regions

How to spot it

GEO breakdown shows heavy concentration

What it usually means

Some markets are early adopters or better covered by AI

Typical actions

Localise and invest in strong regions; apply learnings to lagging ones.

71

Almost no LLM-classified traffic

How to spot it

Referral dashboard nearly empty

What it usually means

Limited AI presence or missing classification

Typical actions

Check Brand Presence & Agentic for visibility; verify referrer patterns and classification rules.

72

Sudden spike/drop in LLM traffic

How to spot it

Time series shows big change

What it usually means

Model updates, campaigns, PR, or content/infrastructure change

Typical actions

Correlate with internal campaigns and external events; adjust content and expectations accordingly.

Advanced Behaviour & Journey

6 patterns

AI-only journeys, cross-device paths and recovery flows.

73

AI-only journeys

New
How to spot it

Pages that get meaningful LLM traffic but little/no traffic from other channels

What it usually means

AI assistants "discovered" journeys that don't exist in traditional funnels

Typical actions

Study these journeys; decide whether to formalise them (navigation, campaigns); use learnings to enrich SEO/paid strategies.

74

"Explore then bounce" pattern

How to spot it

LLM sessions with >1 pageview but short duration and no key events

What it usually means

Visitors click around but don't find what they need, then leave

Typical actions

Improve signposting and in-page navigation; clarify next steps; add clearer content hierarchy for that intent.

75

Time-of-day / day-of-week AI spikes

How to spot it

LLM traffic spikes at specific times (e.g., weekday daytime) vs other channels

What it usually means

AI traffic is aligned to certain usage contexts (work vs personal)

Typical actions

Tailor content and offers to likely context; use as input for support/coverage planning.

76

OS / browser skew for AI traffic

How to spot it

LLM visitors strongly skew to specific OS (e.g., iOS) or browser

What it usually means

AI integrations (apps, plugins) bias toward certain platforms

Typical actions

Ensure those platforms have best-in-class performance and UX; consider testing features where that audience is strongest.

77

Cross-device journeys from AI

New
How to spot it

Same user profiles (if visible) show LLM referral on desktop, later return via mobile, or vice versa

What it usually means

AI answer triggers multi-session journeys across devices

Typical actions

Ensure continuity: consistent content and design across devices; preserve key CTAs and navigation.

78

AI-boosted recovery journeys

New
How to spot it

LLM visits land on support/FAQ pages soon after failed conversions or errors from other sessions

What it usually means

Users use AI to recover from earlier friction or confusion

Typical actions

Improve diagnostics support; add clearer troubleshooting flows and self-service tools; address root causes in the main journey.

Visibility → Bots → Humans

Signals that only make sense when you read the whole funnel together.

High AI interest, weak on-site experience

How to spot it

Brand Presence: high visibility; Agentic: many hits; Referral: high bounce/low depth

What it usually means

AI surfaces you, but landing experience doesn't deliver

80

Strong content, poor AI reach

How to spot it

Agentic: low Success Rate / many 4xx/5xx on key URLs; Brand Presence: low visibility; Referral: low LLM traffic

What it usually means

Good content exists but AI can't reliably fetch/use it

81

High bot usage, almost no visits (zero-click)

How to spot it

Agentic: many hits; Brand Presence: citations; Referral: near-zero LLM visits

What it usually means

Content is trusted and reused inside answers; users don't need to click

Typical actions

Focus on content accuracy and positioning; add on-site value where visits are desirable.

82

One AI assistant dominates referrals and agentic hits

How to spot it

Agentic & Referral both dominated by one platform

What it usually means

One assistant is your primary AI distribution channel (and dependency)

Typical actions

Prioritise this platform in strategy; optimise its journeys; monitor reliance and explore secondary platforms.

Journeys, Gaps & Opportunities

5 patterns

Hero pages, described-vs-actual mismatches, and emerging opportunities.

83

Hero URLs across all dashboards

How to spot it

URL Inspector: hero URL; Agentic: strong hits; Referral: strong conversions

What it usually means

High-impact AI hero pages that drive real outcomes

Typical actions

Prioritise for experimentation; use as templates for other journeys.

84

Brand Presence vs actual landing URLs mismatch

How to spot it

Brand Presence: suggests certain URLs/journeys; Referral: traffic lands on different URLs

What it usually means

Misalignment between what AI describes and where users arrive
