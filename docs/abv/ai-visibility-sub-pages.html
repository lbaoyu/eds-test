AI Visibility: three views, one operating workflow

AI Visibility is three views run as one sequence, with one job: build a short, owned set of prompts worth promoting into the Prompt Library to track and measure. Baseline the market, investigate the conversation, validate the gap, then track the prompts that matter.

The core idea

Three views, one workflow

AI Visibility is not a single score. It is a three-view workflow for establishing a baseline, discovering opportunities, and understanding competitive gaps — so the right prompts can be promoted into the Prompt Library to track and measure. Each view answers a distinct question and produces a distinct output.

ViewBusiness questionPrimary output
Visibility OverviewWhere does the brand stand?A scoped AI visibility baseline
Prompt ResearchWhat conversations shape visibility?A prioritized opportunity list
Market ComparisonWhere do competitors lead?A competitive gap map

AI Visibility is powered by Semrush Enterprise AIO and draws on a database of more than 289 million AI prompts, so a baseline is available immediately — no custom prompt set to configure or execute first. Coverage currently spans ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode, and Gemini. The three views are built for discovery; the most relevant findings are then promoted into the tracked set.

Where this fits

AI Visibility discovers opportunities across the broad AI-search landscape using Semrush market data. Brand Presence is the separate, continuous measurement of an organization’s own configured prompts. The bridge between them is Track — promoting a promising topic or prompt out of AI Visibility into the Prompt Library, where Brand Presence then measures it over time.

Discipline

Start every loop here

Scope the loop

The workflow runs in loops, and every loop opens with one decision: its focus. A focus is a single, concrete thing the loop is about — a product, a customer journey, or a campaign — in a defined market. That one choice drives the whole discovery pass and, in turn, the prompts the loop promotes to track.

Choose the focus for this loop

FocusWhat it meansExample
Product or lineA specific product, solution, or SKU family the loop is about.Enterprise PDF & e-signature
Customer journeyOne or more journey stages the loop targets, for a defined audience.Consideration → conversion, SMB buyers
Campaign or initiativeA time-boxed launch or push the loop supports.Tax-season campaign, US
Market(s)Always part of the focus — each market is scoped and measured separately.United States

Keep it to one coherent focus per loop. A loop that tries to cover every product, journey, and market at once produces a prompt set no one can act on or measure.

How the focus drives discovery

ViewWhat the focus sets
Visibility OverviewThe domain, market, and period the baseline is read for.
Prompt ResearchThe topics entered — drawn straight from the focus and its journey stages.
Market ComparisonThe brand under study and the real competitors for that focus.

Refine within the focus

Inside the chosen focus, two habits keep the resulting set balanced and interpretable: cover the journey stages that matter for the focus (discovery through retention), and hold roughly 70–80% unbranded to 20–30% branded so the set reflects real demand, not just brand queries.

Loop scoping worksheet Focus Product / journey(s) / campaign Market(s) Scoped & measured separately Objective What this loop must prove Journey Stages in scope for the focus Competitors Up to 4 real alternatives Mix ~70–80% unbranded · 20–30% branded Output The prompts this loop promotes to track
Scoping rule

One loop, one focus. Scope it narrow, discover it deep, promote only the prompts that map to the focus — then start the next loop on the next focus.

View 1 · the control tower

Visibility Overview

A high-level read on how brands appear across AI-powered search, and the launch point for deeper investigation. The headline number is the start, not the answer.

Filters: Domain · Market · Platform · Reporting period. Domain defaults to the primary brand; switch it to analyze any domain, including a competitor’s.

Metric

AI Visibility

Presence in AI answers — how often the brand is mentioned versus the median mentions of competitors. Shown as a percentage.

Metric

Mentions

Total times the brand was mentioned across the AI engines in view.

Metric

Monthly Audience

Estimated monthly reach in AI contexts — directional, not measured traffic.

Metric

Cited Pages

Pages from the brand’s own site cited in AI answers.

How to read it

  1. Confirm the domain is right. The brand’s primary, region-correct domain — not a parent brand confused with a product brand.
  2. Start broad, then isolate. Read all engines and the priority market first; drop to a single engine or market only to explain a difference.
  3. Trust the trend, not the point. Use the reporting period to tell stable from improving, declining, or a one-off movement.
  4. Read the four cards together. Cross-reference them — see the diagnostic patterns below.
  5. Work the breakdowns and tables. Two charts and six tabs sit below the cards.

Two breakdowns, six tables

ElementWhat it shows
By model (chart)Mentions, Visibility, or Audience across each engine — e.g. strong in ChatGPT, weak in Google AI Overviews.
By market (chart)Mentions, Audience, or Cited Pages across top markets; smaller markets grouped as “Other.”
Performing prompts & topicsTopics where the brand is already mentioned; expand to the prompts behind each.

These are diagnostic hypotheses, not conclusions. The next step is always to inspect the supporting topics, prompts, and sources — via Details.

Act on a finding

Two actions sit on the topic tables. Details opens the full AI answer behind a prompt — the response text, whether the brand was mentioned, which brands appeared, whether the site was cited, and the source URLs. Track promotes a topic or prompt into the tracked set, so a discovery becomes something Brand Presence measures over time.

Capture a baseline brief

Do

  • Use a specific market when the decision is regional
  • Start with all engines, then isolate the weak one
  • Read trends before drawing conclusions
  • Inspect cited pages and cited sources
  • Record the selected filters with every screenshot

Avoid

  • Reading Monthly Audience as actual traffic
  • Treating AI Visibility as a page-level technical score
  • Assuming high mentions means high authority
  • Comparing markets without stating the scope
  • Equating AI Visibility with Brand Presence — they measure different things

In practice

Visibility Overview establishes the baseline — presence, mentions, estimated reach, and owned pages cited. It is a starting point: the next questions are which topics and prompts create that result, and whether it holds across engines and markets.

View 2 · the demand map

Prompt Research

Topic-driven, not domain-driven. Enter a topic and Prompt Research surfaces the related topics, the questions people ask AI systems, and the brands and domains already appearing in those answers. It turns “visibility is low” into “the brand is missing these specific conversations — and here is why they matter.”

Filters: Topic · Market · Platform. No domain or date filter — a topic in a market, not one site over time; the page stays empty until a topic is entered.

A Related-topics intent breakdown then splits demand across five intents — Informational, Commercial, Transactional, Task, and Navigational — so it’s clear whether people are learning, comparing, or ready to act.

Frame a strong topic

Start from a category, use case, or customer problem — not a bare brand name. A useful topic carries enough context to represent a real situation:

Goal + Persona + Constraints + Comparison set e.g. “Best enterprise PDF solution for a legal ops team that needs secure collaboration, auditability, and integration with existing document workflows”
  • Weak: “Adobe”
  • Weak: “Software”
  • Better: “Enterprise PDF editing”
  • Better: “PDF workflow automation”
  • Better: “Acrobat alternatives for enterprise”
Portfolio rule

When promoting finds into the tracked set, aim for roughly 70–80% unbranded and 20–30% branded prompts, with a mix of intents and localized prompts for relevant markets. Track prompts worth acting on — a prompt with no content, no owner, and no business reason is noise, not insight.

Act on a finding

As in Visibility Overview, Details opens the AI responses and citations behind a prompt, and Track adds a topic (all its prompts) or a single prompt to the tracked set — turning a research find into something measured over time.

View 3 · the gap map

Market Comparison

Places the brand beside a set of chosen competitors and shows exactly where those brands earn mentions and citations the brand doesn’t. The brand is set automatically from the Domain filter; up to four other brands are added automatically, for five in total, and the brand always stays in the comparison.

Filters: Brand set (up to 5) · Market · Platform. No date filter — always the most recent data available.

Find the gaps

A gap analysis sits below, viewable by Topics or by Source domains and filtered by gap type:

Gap typeWhat it shows
AllEverywhere at least one of the other brands appears.
MissingOther brands appear, the brand does not — the clearest opportunities.

The Topics view lists each topic with its Volume, the brand’s Visibility and Mentions, the topic Difficulty, Total competitor mentions, and a Mentions-by-brand breakdown (the brand highlighted). Expand a topic to see the prompts behind it — engine, how many brands were mentioned, and cited sources. The Source domains view lists cited third-party sites, with Pages cited, the Responses that cited them, the brand’s mentions on those sites, and estimated Organic traffic.

Choosing the brands to compare

Auto-added competitors are a starting point, not the answer. Swap them for brands that appear in the actual buying or research conversation — not four household names.

Business questionCompetitor selection
Category leadershipThe top category alternatives

Regional competitionLocal and regional alternatives
Premium positioningPremium or specialist competitors
Disruption riskNew entrants and nontraditional substitutes
Source strategyBrands earning citations from the same sources

In practice

Market Comparison puts the baseline into competitive context. Who is ahead matters less than where the advantage comes from — specific topics, prompts, or source domains. Use Details to confirm the mechanism, then Track the validated gaps into the tracked set.

In practice

Three operating scenarios

Each is one loop with a different focus, running the same sequence — Overview to Research to Comparison.

Scenario 01

Product marketing discovers a comparison gap

Situation. A software company believes it is visible for its category but wants to know whether it appears when buyers ask for recommendations.

Workflow

  • Overview: priority market, engine breakdown, Topic Opportunities.
  • Research: category and use-case topics; commercial and comparison prompts; which competitors appear; cited sources.
  • Comparison: add the competitors that recur; filter to Missing; open Details on the most commercial prompts.

Outcome

A prioritized set of commercial and comparison prompts — each with an owner and a baseline — promoted into the tracked set so movement can be measured over time.

Scenario 02

A consumer brand finds a market-specific weakness

Situation. Strong worldwide visibility, weak performance in one priority country.

Workflow

  • Overview: compare the country against worldwide — is the weakness in mentions, audience, or cited pages?
  • Research: the category in the local market; local-language and local-intent prompts; regional brands and publishers.
  • Comparison: regional competitors, not just global leaders; Missing source domains; local publishers, retailers, community.

Outcome

A market-specific, localized prompt set added to tracking: regional competitors, local-intent topics, and the priority-market prompts worth measuring separately.

Scenario 03

Mentioned, but not supported by its own content

Situation. Meaningful mentions but relatively few cited pages.

Workflow

  • Overview: review Cited Pages and Cited Sources — which important pages are absent?
  • Research: prompts where the brand is mentioned but another source is the evidence.
  • Comparison: whether competitors have stronger owned or earned-source coverage.

Outcome

The prompts where the brand is mentioned but not cited, promoted into the tracked set so citation movement can be measured over time. Read a citation as an authority signal, not a conversion.

Keep it moving

A recommended operating rhythm

AI Visibility works best as an ongoing practice, not a one-off audit — a cadence of loops, each refreshing the tracked set.

Weekly

Monitor & triage

New Missing topics, competitor movements, source opportunities, engine and market changes. Output: top new findings, notable changes, owners, open questions.

Monthly

Refresh the backlog

Are the tracked topics still relevant? New customer questions? Right competitor set? New opportunities worth adding to the set? Prompts over-concentrated in one intent or market?

Quarterly

Refresh the strategy

Market coverage, branded/unbranded balance, intent and topic spread, competitor selection, localization, and alignment between tracked prompts and business priorities.

Field craft

Tips & guardrails

The habits that separate a clean read from a misleading one.

All engines for discovery, one for diagnosis

Start broad so no signal is missed; isolate an engine only to explain a difference.

Specific market for decisions

Worldwide orients; a country or region is where content, PR, and localization budget go.

Start with Missing

Missing surfaces obvious opportunities; Shared shows where to lift prominence; Unique shows strengths to defend.

Relevance before volume

Validate customer fit, product fit, commercial intent, and whether the topic is one worth measuring over time.

Use Details to challenge assumptions

Was the competitor actually recommended? Was the brand omitted or just later? Was the source authoritative? Don’t act from a summary row alone.

Export & annotate

Add relevance, owner, action, priority, confidence, and status — turn a table into a working document.

Track deliberately

Only promote a topic or prompt into the tracked set once it’s validated and someone can act on it.

Don’t promise exact reruns

AI answers vary. The unit of analysis is the pattern across prompts, engines, and time — directional and aggregate, not a deterministic replay.

Preserve scope with every artifact

Domain, market, engine, period, brand set, and capture date travel with every screenshot and export.

Decide what to track

Prioritize what to track

Discovery produces more candidates than any team can act on. Rank them before promoting anything into the tracked set.
