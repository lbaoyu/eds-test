01

From editorial sketch to premium productized visual argument

The original framework over-indexed on editorial thought leadership and under-specified execution aesthetics. v1 said one image = one argument. v2 keeps that and raises the bar:

One image = one argument, presented through a premium system.

A reader should feel “this is a strategic insight expressed with product-grade design” — not “this is a smart sketch,” and not “this is a product datasheet.” The design confidence borrows from Adobe, Linear, Stripe and Notion. The thinking stays yours.

02

Visual territory

Two ideas define the space. The first carries over from v1; the second is what the latest image added.

Editorial systems thinking

Adobe design confidence × systems architecture × visual storytelling × your explanatory thinking. The house voice.

Productized thinking

Ideas should feel operational. Show modules, workflows, orchestration layers, decision systems, feedback loops and capability cards — not concepts floating in space. Readers trust systems more than abstractions.

05

Color hierarchy — 70 / 20 / 10

Discipline is the whole point. Most of every image is neutral; product colors carry categories; red is rationed to a tenth of the frame.

Off-white Grey Black Red
70% neutral — black, grey, off-white 20% product — purple, blue, green, amber for cards & categories 10% red — contradiction, emphasis, annotation, key transition, the missing layer
Red creates tension, not decoration. If it’s doing anything other than marking a contradiction, an emphasis, an annotation, a key transition, or a missing layer — it’s overspent.
06

Card system

Information is chunked into an enterprise-product card language: large containers for major concepts, small capability cards for supporting ones. Soft, minimal shadows. Large corner radius (16–24px). Consistent, generous spacing. Think Adobe, Linear, Stripe, Notion — not PowerPoint.

07

Information hierarchy 2.0

Every image carries three layers. Layer 1 is the argument — the largest thing on the page, readable from mobile-feed distance. Layer 2 is the explanation — usually three to seven nodes. Layer 3 is evidence — small cards, icons, annotations that reward a closer look but aren’t needed to get the point.

Never put the important insight in small text. Blur the image to 20%: if the argument still lands, the hierarchy works. If not, it failed.

Annotation & signature

10

The quality bar

Before publishing, run all five. If any fails, the image isn’t on-brand yet.

Test 01Insight

Is there a surprising idea?

Test 02Mobile

Can you understand it on a phone?

Test 03Product

Does it look like a premium SaaS product?

Test 04Curiosity

Do you want the explanation?

Test 05Ownership

Could someone recognize it as yours without the name?

After 20–30 posts, a reader should register the warm off-white ground, the large editorial statement, the product-grade card system, the selective red, the systems and orchestration, the small subtle signature, and the one missing connection revealed — and think: this isn’t another AI diagram. It’s Leo showing me the layer everyone else missed.
