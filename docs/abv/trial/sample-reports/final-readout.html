06 Read this carefully

What this readout does and doesn’t tell you

Confound

Three priorities, one deployment window

Priorities 2 and 3 shipped together, so the +5pt aggregate citation-rate lift is their combined effect. There is no isolated evidence for how much of that lift came from the new FAQ content versus the schema markup alone.

Scope mismatch

IME watches 22 prompts, the comparison watches 64

The Impact Measurement Engine’s citation-rate delta is scoped to the prompts monitored within the RCV strategy, not the full locked set. Use it as a faster, narrower confirmation signal — not a replacement for the full re-run.

Time window

Six weeks may be early for discovery content

New FAQ and comparison content can take longer than six weeks to be fully re-crawled and cited by AI systems. The 22%→29% unbranded-discovery movement may still be understating the eventual effect.

Integrity held

The locked set stayed locked

The 3 new Citation Attempt suggestions surfaced by the library were correctly queued rather than added mid-comparison — which also means the rates-and-fees under-coverage flagged in the Prompt Audit is still open, not yet addressed by this readout.

07 Learnings

What this round confirmed about the approach

  • Content-visibility recovery worked essentially as diagnosed. The content already existed; making it agent-readable through Optimize at Edge produced a fast, large lift with no CMS authoring change — the cleanest result of the three priorities.
  • Publishing and schema work moved the discovery numbers fast. Unbranded discovery citation rate improved 22%→29% in just six weeks — real progress toward the 35% target, with a deeper content pass likely to close the rest.
  • The Impact Measurement Engine is a faster confirmation path, but only for supported opportunity types. Non-RCV work still needs the manual prompt re-run as its only evidence.
  • Keeping the prompt set locked, and queuing new candidates separately, kept this comparison clean. The discipline the Prompt Audit recommended paid off directly in this readout.

08 Recommendation & next steps

Scale the operating model; keep refining Priority 2 inside it

Across the four-goal scorecard: G2 met its target, G1 and G3 are trending toward theirs, and G4 has a working baseline for the first time. That combination — one goal fully proven, none regressed — is enough evidence to scale the pattern, not just repeat it once more.

Decision · Section 08

Scale the locked-baseline → measured-change pattern to more pages

This isn’t a single-metric call. G2 (content visibility) hit 100% on all 3 pages, confirmed by the Impact Measurement Engine — full proof of the cycle. G1 and G3 (discovery and citation) both improved without fully closing, and G4 (engagement) went from no metric to a working baseline. Nothing regressed. That’s enough to extend the same pattern to the next tier of high-traffic pages, while Priority 2’s discovery-content work continues in parallel, inside the scaled program, rather than gating it.

What scaling means in practice

  1. Extend Optimize at Edge / Recover Content Visibility to the next tier of high-traffic pages beyond the current 3, using the Impact Measurement Engine to confirm sustained lift as each new page goes live.
  2. Track the 3 queued Citation Attempt prompts for the rates-and-fees page into a new v2 cohort — do not fold them into the locked 64-prompt comparison.
  3. Run a second pass on Priority 2 — deepen comparison-style content on the unbranded cash-back cluster specifically, to carry the trending 29% citation rate the rest of the way to the 35% target.
  4. Re-run the full 64-prompt evaluation in 4–6 weeks to see whether the discovery-content lift keeps compounding as pages are further crawled and cited.
  5. Set up a recurring IME strategy review (e.g., monthly) as more Recover Content Visibility opportunities are deployed, to catch regressions early while scaling.
  6. Let G4’s 612 sessions stand as the baseline, not a target — report against it at the next readout instead of comparing it to a number that didn’t exist before this window.
