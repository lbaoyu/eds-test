About this guide

A clear view of your AEM operating model

Whether you are reviewing your current AEM environment or planning a move to AEM as a Cloud Service, this guide explains the differences that affect your organization: platform maintenance, application delivery, scaling, and day-to-day responsibilities.

Designed for business and technology stakeholders evaluating AEM Sites and Assets. Use it alongside a review of your application, business requirements, and applicable Adobe service agreement.

The core distinction

Cloud-native service vs. a managed AEM deployment

Adobe operates the platform in both models. The distinction is how AEM is architected, updated, scaled, and extended—and how your teams plan and deliver application changes.

AEMaaCS

AEM as a Cloud Service

A continuously updated, cloud-native AEM service.

Adobe runs a dynamic, containerized platform with separated code and content, service-managed scaling, and automated AEM version updates. Your team builds within cloud-service engineering constraints.[1][2]

AMS

AEM on Adobe Managed Services

A versioned AEM deployment operated by Adobe.

Adobe manages the agreed hosting and operational service for AEM 6.5 / 6.5 LTS. The topology, maintenance approach, and optional capabilities are governed by the supported solution design and service agreement.[3][4][9]

What matters for your organization: AEMaaCS shifts platform maintenance toward a continuously updated service. Your team and implementation partner continue to manage your application, integrations, testing, and governance.

Scope: Primarily AEM Sites using the AEM Publish tier and AEM Assets. AMS means Adobe Managed Services, not self-managed AEM on a hyperscaler. AEM 6.5 and 6.5 LTS are distinct release lines; validate the actual version. Edge Delivery Services is a separate delivery choice, not a mandatory consequence of moving to AEMaaCS. Forms and solution-specific capabilities require their own fit-gap review.[1][8]

Key concepts

Understanding the terms behind the differences

These concepts explain how each model affects your application and operations. They provide a common foundation for discussions with your architecture, delivery, and service teams.

1. Operating model
How platform changes, capacity, releases, and incidents are handled. Both models are Adobe-operated, but AEMaaCS standardizes more of the runtime and platform lifecycle; AMS operates a versioned deployment under an agreed service model.[1][3][4]
2. Immutable code, mutable content
Immutable means application areas such as /apps and /libs cannot be edited at runtime in AEMaaCS. Mutable content, such as authored pages, can change through supported content operations. Publishing a page and deploying a component are different kinds of change.[5][6]
3. Elasticity and autoscaling
Elasticity adjusts runtime capacity as demand changes. AEMaaCS uses dynamic containerized services. AMS also offers optional production Dispatcher/publish autoscaling, but its scope and operating process differ. Scaling does not fix slow queries, ineffective caching, or downstream bottlenecks.[1][3]
4. CI/CD and Cloud Manager
Continuous integration / continuous delivery automates building, checking, and delivering application changes. Cloud Manager supports both models. In AEMaaCS, it also provides the governed application deployment path for regular cloud environments; Rapid Development Environments (RDEs) support a separate rapid-iteration workflow.[1][3]
5. Rolling and blue/green deployment
A rolling deployment gradually replaces running instances, so old and new code can coexist temporarily. Blue/green prepares another serving environment before switching traffic. AEMaaCS uses rolling updates; AMS offers optional blue/green for production Dispatcher/publish pairs. Application compatibility still matters.[2][3]
6. Availability, RPO, and RTO
An SLA defines a service commitment. A recovery point objective (RPO) describes the acceptable data-loss window; a recovery time objective (RTO) describes the target time to restore service. They answer different questions. Confirm the applicable terms and prove that the end-to-end application recovery plan meets the business requirement.

Keep in mind: Your hosting arrangement, platform update process, and application release process are related but distinct. Likewise, scaling adds capacity, while application design and integrations determine how effectively that capacity supports your experiences.

Side-by-side comparison

What changes for your teams

The following comparison highlights platform differences and their practical implications for your organization. Confirm available capabilities and entitlements for your specific Adobe program.

Architecture

AEM as a Cloud Service

Containerized Author and Publish services with dynamic pod counts; a shared repository across Author pods.[1]

Adobe Managed Services

Versioned AEM 6.5 / 6.5 LTS environments in an Adobe-operated, agreed deployment topology.[4][9]

What this meansDo not carry server-local state or fixed-instance assumptions into AEMaaCS.

AEM updates

AEM as a Cloud Service

Adobe automatically applies AEM version updates, using environment checks and test gates. Application releases are separate.[2][7]

Adobe Managed Services

Service packs and version upgrades follow the applicable AEM release line and the agreed AMS maintenance/change process.[4][9]

What this meansReplace periodic platform upgrade projects with continuous compatibility and regression readiness.

Scaling

AEM as a Cloud Service

Dynamic, service-managed scaling of the containerized Author and Publish architecture according to activity and traffic.[1]

Adobe Managed Services

Capacity planning remains topology-specific. Cloud Manager offers optional autoscaling for production Dispatcher/publish pairs; confirm enablement and conditions.[3]

What this meansBoth models offer scaling capabilities, with different scope and behavior. Plan for your workload and validate application performance.

CI/CD

AEM as a Cloud Service

Cloud Manager is the governed deployment path for application code and configuration in regular cloud environments. RDE has a separate rapid-iteration workflow.[1][5]

Adobe Managed Services

Cloud Manager provides CI/CD, code inspection, security validation, and performance testing for AMS.[3]

What this meansCloud Manager supports both models. Review how your delivery process aligns with the deployment requirements of your chosen platform.

Code & configuration

AEM as a Cloud Service

/apps and /libs are immutable at runtime. OSGi configuration is deployed as code; mutable content is packaged separately.[5][6]

Adobe Managed Services

Uses the conventional AEM repository/package model, within AMS access and change controls. Production changes remain subject to AMS access and change controls.[4][6]

What this meansRemove runtime code writes and manual configuration drift; version and review changes.

Deployment continuity

AEM as a Cloud Service

Rolling updates allow old and new versions to coexist. Documentation describes updates as usually zero downtime, not an unconditional guarantee.[2]

Adobe Managed Services

Continuity depends on topology and procedure. Cloud Manager offers optional blue/green for production Dispatcher/publish pairs, with setup and limitations.[3]

What this meansDesign custom code and content changes to tolerate mixed versions; validate author-side behavior too.

Publishing content

AEM as a Cloud Service

A redesigned replication service uses publish/subscribe queues and Sling Content Distribution. Reverse replication is not supported.[1][5]

Adobe Managed Services

Uses the AEM 6.x replication framework and agents appropriate to the deployed solution.[1][5]

What this meansAssess custom replication agents and any flow that depends on writing data back from Publish.

Local disk & jobs

AEM as a Cloud Service

Containers can be replaced. Local disk is not durable application storage; long-running scheduler/cron assumptions need redesign.[5]

Adobe Managed Services

Traditional instance-oriented patterns may be supported, subject to architecture, resilience, and AMS operating controls.[4][5]

What this meansUse durable external storage and restart-safe, distributed or offloaded processing where needed.

Developer access

AEM as a Cloud Service

No production CRXDE Lite or direct Web Console configuration changes. Use supported developer tooling, logs, and code deployments; dev access has different rules.[5]

Adobe Managed Services

Access and troubleshooting workflows follow the AMS service model and the permissions agreed for your environment.[4]

What this meansUpdate support runbooks so diagnostics do not depend on direct production mutation.

Run modes

AEM as a Cloud Service

Only supported predefined run modes; custom run modes are not allowed.[5][6]

Adobe Managed Services

AEM 6.x supports the traditional run-mode model; deployed configuration remains subject to AMS controls.[5]

What this meansRework environment-specific configuration instead of copying a custom run-mode taxonomy.

Asset processing

AEM as a Cloud Service

Direct binary transfer and asset microservices replace the traditional core asset-processing model; adapt custom processing and post-processing workflows.[1][5]

Adobe Managed Services

Uses the asset-processing approach supported by the deployed AEM version, commonly including DAM Update Asset workflow customizations.[5]

What this meansAudit rendition steps, launchers, integrations, and binary upload code—not only content volume.

CDN & caching

AEM as a Cloud Service

Includes an Adobe-managed CDN. Dispatcher configuration is code-managed; a customer-managed CDN is a separate supported configuration to validate.[1][5]

Adobe Managed Services

CDN, Dispatcher, and network arrangements depend on the AMS solution design and contracted services.[4]

What this meansReview cache keys, headers, invalidation, domains, certificates, and WAF/network dependencies.

Identity

AEM as a Cloud Service

Adobe IMS is used for Author access. Publish visitor authentication is a separate concern; supported integrations can include SAML.[5]

Adobe Managed Services

Authentication depends on the existing AEM implementation and AMS configuration; assess author SSO and visitor identity independently.[5]

What this meansReview author single sign-on and site-visitor authentication separately when planning your migration.

Operations & resilience

AEM as a Cloud Service

Adobe operates the cloud platform and its update mechanisms; application health checks and customer tests are integral to safe change.[1][2]

Adobe Managed Services

Adobe supplies managed operations under the agreed AMS scope, including documented backup and disaster-recovery service options.[4]

What this meansFor both, agree incident ownership, recovery procedures, application validation, and escalation paths.

SLA, recovery & regions

Both models · Validate for the program

Validate the exact service agreement and program. Availability commitments, exclusions, backup retention, restore options, RPO/RTO, region availability, data residency, and network connectivity are not universal values inferred from the product name.

What this meansUse a workload-specific checklist. A backup is not proof that a business recovery target is met.

Commercial & TCO

AEM as a Cloud Service

Assess current entitlements and usage measures, migration effort, integration changes, and ongoing application operations.[1]

Adobe Managed Services

Assess the current AMS agreement, capacity/add-ons, maintenance effort, and release-line lifecycle obligations.[3][4][9]

What this meansCompare total cost over the same planning period and workload assumptions, including migration, maintenance, and application operations.

↓ AEM 6.x replication framework ↓

Engineering consequence: Treat AEMaaCS instances as replaceable compute, not durable servers. Eliminate node-local business state and assumptions that a specific process or filesystem will remain available.

Shared responsibilities

How Adobe, your team, and your partner work together

Adobe operates the platform, while your team and implementation partner manage the application and business processes built on it. Use the overview below to align responsibilities; your service agreement defines the applicable scope.

Adobe platform / service team

Operate the agreed platform

  • AEMaaCS: cloud runtime, platform version updates, and service-managed scaling.
  • AMS: agreed infrastructure and operations, with maintenance and capacity changes coordinated through the service model.
  • Platform incident response and service recovery within the applicable agreement.
Customer / implementation partner

Own application fitness

  • Custom code, content models, integrations, and business workflows.
  • Functional and UI regression tests; performance and cache design.
  • Access governance, content quality, secure implementation, and business acceptance.
Jointly planned

  • Critical journeys, workload assumptions, and measurable acceptance criteria.
  • Incident triage across platform, application, and third-party dependencies.
  • Recovery exercises, escalation paths, and migration or cutover responsibilities.

Common questions

These answers clarify what remains important when you adopt a managed platform.

What testing does our team need to maintain?

Your team should maintain automated functional and UI tests for business-critical journeys. Adobe’s version-update process uses customer tests as quality gates.[2]

Does AMS also support automated delivery and scaling?

Yes. Cloud Manager for AMS supports CI/CD and offers optional Dispatcher/publish autoscaling and blue/green deployments. Confirm availability and setup for your program.[3]

How should we plan for deployment continuity?

AEMaaCS version-update documentation describes updates as usually incurring zero downtime. Custom code compatibility, content changes, integrations, and the tier involved still matter. Preview can have downtime during deployments.[1][2]

Is moving to AEMaaCS a hosting-only change?

The transition changes more than hosting. Application code, content distribution, asset processing, identity, and instance lifecycle need to be assessed as part of migration planning.[5][6]

Migration planning

Plan around your application and business priorities

AEMaaCS alignment

Stronger fit when…

You want a continuously maintained platform, service-managed elasticity, and standardized delivery—and can adapt the application to cloud-native constraints. The team is prepared to maintain automated tests and treat code, configuration, and infrastructure assumptions accordingly.

AMS continuity

May remain appropriate when…

An existing supported deployment depends on patterns that need meaningful redesign, or the organization needs a phased transition. Keep lifecycle, security maintenance, service entitlements, and modernization dependencies explicit. This is not a recommendation to retain unsupported features or defer maintenance.

The appropriate approach depends on your application, operating requirements, and transition priorities. Industry or traffic volume alone does not determine the model; review your specific requirements with Adobe.

01

Assess the estate

Run Best Practices Analyzer on the existing environment, review results in Cloud Acceleration Manager, and inventory integrations and customizations.[5]

02

Resolve fit gaps

Separate mutable content and immutable code. Rework replication, asset workflows, run modes, local file usage, jobs, identity, and indexes.[5][6]

03

Prove the application

Establish Cloud Manager delivery and automated tests. Validate critical journeys, integrations, publishing, cache behavior, and representative workloads.[2][7]

04

Rehearse the move

Validate the content migration approach, identity and domain changes, reconciliation, freeze or delta strategy, cutover gates, and contingency plan.[10]

Before confirming your migration plan: Review application compatibility, solution entitlements, data residency, network dependencies, recovery targets, and version support. Compare costs using the same workload and planning period. Moving from AMS to AEMaaCS is a platform transition, not an in-place upgrade.

Your requirements

Connect the platform choice to your business needs

Use these common planning situations to identify what your teams should review with Adobe and your implementation partner. The right approach depends on your application and service requirements.

Planning priority 01 · Traffic readiness

Preparing for a major campaign

When you expect a traffic increase, assess the full experience—from cached pages to dynamic requests and third-party integrations.

What to plan

AEMaaCS can adjust runtime capacity, but caching, query performance, and downstream services still affect throughput. If you use AMS, confirm whether optional publish-tier autoscaling is enabled for your environment.

Review with your team: expected traffic patterns, uncached journeys, representative load tests, and the capacity of connected services.

Planning outcome: A campaign-readiness plan that addresses the application and its dependencies as well as platform capacity.[1][3]

Planning priority 02 · Application design

Adapting scheduled processing and file storage

If your current application runs long exports or stores intermediate results on an AEM instance, include those processes in your migration assessment.

What to plan

AEMaaCS containers can be replaced. Local disk is not durable business storage, and a long-running job cannot rely on one instance remaining available.

Review with your team: durable storage, opportunities to offload processing, restart-safe work units, and handling of retries or partial completion.

Planning outcome: A processing design that preserves your business workflow without depending on a specific server.[5]

Planning priority 03 · Release confidence

Maintaining confidence as the platform updates

Automatic AEM updates change how platform maintenance is delivered. Your custom application and integrations still need reliable regression coverage.

What to plan

AEMaaCS version updates use quality gates, including customer-written tests. Your team and implementation partner should maintain coverage for the journeys that matter to your business.

Review with your team: authoring, publishing, visitor and integration tests; ownership of failures; and how test coverage evolves with application changes.

Planning outcome: A continuous testing approach that supports platform updates and your own application releases.[2]

Planning priority 04 · Business continuity

Defining and validating recovery requirements

If your business requires recovery within a specific timeframe, define the application and dependency scope before comparing service arrangements.

What to plan

Availability, acceptable data loss, and restoration time are separate requirements. Backup availability alone does not establish an end-to-end recovery commitment.

Review with Adobe and your team: required RPO and RTO, relevant failure scenarios, applicable service terms, restore procedures, and recovery exercise results.

Planning outcome: A documented recovery approach with clear responsibilities and evidence against your business objectives.

Additional questions about the transition

Clarify these architectural points when you define the scope of your project.

Is Cloud Manager available in both models?

Yes. Cloud Manager supports both AEMaaCS and AMS. The underlying runtime, deployment behavior, and platform update model differ.[1][3]

Can our authors continue to edit pages?

Yes. Authors can edit content through supported authoring operations. Runtime immutability applies to application areas such as /apps and /libs; component deployments are separate from page editing.[5][6]

How is content stored across Author and Publish?

Author pods share an Author repository. Each publisher has its own repository of published content, populated through the content distribution architecture.[1]

Does moving to AEMaaCS require Edge Delivery Services?

No. AEM Publish and Edge Delivery Services are distinct delivery choices. Evaluate the delivery architecture separately from the platform migration; moving to AEMaaCS does not itself mandate an Edge Delivery rebuild.[1]

Planning with Adobe

Bring your requirements into the discussion

Share your current AEM version and topology, key customizations and integrations, traffic patterns, and business-critical journeys. Include your security and data requirements, recovery objectives, and preferred migration timeframe.

This gives your Adobe team and implementation partner the context needed to assess compatibility, confirm service scope, and develop a realistic transition plan.
