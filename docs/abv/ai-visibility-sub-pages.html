Baseline & trend view

Visibility Overview

Your scorecard and trendline.

This is the starting page. At a glance it tells you how visible your brand is right now, whether that visibility is improving or declining, where it's coming from, and where the biggest opportunity areas are. If you open AI Visibility and want the fastest answer to "How are we doing?", start here.

What it helps you understand

Think of it as your summary layer — it answers questions like:

  • Are we showing up often in AI answers, or not much?
  • Is our visibility stable, rising, or falling?
  • Which topics are helping us — and which are underdeveloped?
  • Are our own pages getting cited?
  • Are there markets, engines, or categories where we're stronger or weaker?

How to read it — in order

A

Current position

Start with the headline metrics — they describe your present state. How visible are we right now? Is this good relative to what I expected? Are we broadly present, or only appearing in narrow pockets?

This is your baseline
B

Trend over time

Look at how the metrics move. This is where the page becomes more than a static scorecard. Are we gaining momentum? Did visibility improve recently? Was there a drop that needs investigating? Is progress real, or a one-time spike?

This is your trend view
C

What is driving the result

Drop below the top-line into the supporting detail — moving from "what happened?" to "why did it happen?". Look for strong and weak topics, prompts where you appear (and where you don't), and cited pages or sources that may be helping or limiting visibility.

Best for

Executive check-ins Quick health checks Trend monitoring Finding the first opportunities to investigate

Not the place for deep prompt discovery or detailed competitor gap analysis. It shows the landscape at a high level, then points you toward where to dig next.

Mental model

Visibility Overview tells you your current position — and whether it's moving in the right direction.

!

Common mistake

Treating this page as the whole story. If visibility is low, this page tells you there's a problem — but it doesn't fully explain which exact prompts or which competitors are causing it. That's what the next two pages are for.

02
Discover the prompts & topics behind the landscape

Prompt Research

Your topic and prompt discovery workspace.

This is the discovery engine. Where Overview tells you "visibility is weak in this area," Prompt Research helps you find which prompts shape that area, which topics are active in the market, what people are effectively asking AI systems, and where the strategic opportunity is. It's the conversation layer behind the metrics.

What it helps you understand

Stop thinking only in scores; start thinking in real user intent:

  • What kinds of prompts define this market?
  • Which topics seem to matter most?
  • What prompt themes are repeatedly connected to my brand or category?
  • Where are there prompt spaces I should care about but am not yet strong in?
  • Which prompts are worth tracking more intentionally later?

Why it matters: without this page you might know visibility is low — but not low for which topics, which kinds of questions, or where exactly, and not what to act on first. Prompt Research turns a vague problem into a usable set of insights.

How to use it — like a researcher, not a dashboard-watcher

A

Start with a broad topic area

Pick a theme relevant to your brand or category. What prompts exist here? What are people effectively asking? Are these informational, evaluative, comparative, or purchase-oriented?

B

Look for meaningful patterns

Don't scan isolated prompts — look for clusters. Many prompts may point to the same theme; several may reveal the same competitive battleground; certain prompt families carry more business importance than others. You want patterns, not entries.

C

Separate signal from noise

Not every prompt matters equally. Is this strategically relevant? Aligned to our audience? Tied to a business priority? Broad enough to matter, or too niche? The value isn't collecting prompts — it's finding the ones worth caring about.

D

Turn discovery into action

Once you've identified important prompt spaces, decide which deserve closer monitoring, which reveal content gaps, which suggest messaging or SEO opportunities, and which should move into a curated tracking workflow.

Best for

Market discovery Topic discovery Understanding user-intent patterns Building a smarter prompt strategy

Especially useful early on, when you don't yet know what to monitor.

Mental model

Prompt Research explains the questions and topics that create the visibility landscape.

!

Common mistake

Using it as a flat list. It's not most useful as "here are many prompts" — it's most useful as "here are the themes, intents, and opportunities that define the market." Skim individual prompts and you miss the real value.

03
Compare your brand against competitors

Market Comparison

Your competitive gap finder.

This is the competitive lens. Where Overview answers "How are we doing?", this answers "How are we doing relative to others?" — who's ahead, where they're stronger, where the gaps are, and where you're uniquely strong. It turns internal performance into competitive context.

What it helps you understand

A weak score means something different depending on what competitors are doing:

  • Which competitor is outperforming us?
  • Is our weakness broad, or only against specific brands?
  • On which topics are competitors stronger?
  • Where are competitors appearing and we are absent?
  • Where do we overlap — and where do we have unique presence they don't?

Why it matters: a number by itself can mislead. A score can look decent until you see a competitor far ahead; it can look low while competitors are also weak (an immature category); a topic can feel unimportant until you see competitors dominating it. Market Comparison gives the relative frame you need to prioritise correctly.

How to use it — in layers

A

Check overall standing

Compare overall brand performance first. Who leads? Are we close behind, or far behind? Is this a narrow gap or a major one? Are we generally competitive, or only in select areas?

Your competitive baseline
B

Look for topic gaps

Go deeper into topics. Which are competitors owning? Where are we missing? Where do we overlap? Which are uniquely ours? This moves you from brand-level comparison to specific battlegrounds.

C

Look for source gaps

Which cited sources or domains appear to support competitors more than you? Are they surfacing in contexts where you're absent? This points to authority, content, or discoverability gaps — the why behind who gets surfaced.

The gap types worth knowing

Clearest opportunity

Missing

Competitors appear and you don't. Usually means you're absent from an important conversation — or not yet strong enough to surface there.

Improve your share

Shared

Both you and competitors appear. You're in the fight, but not necessarily winning it. This is about improving share, not simply entering the conversation.

Strength area

Unique

You appear and competitors don't. May indicate owned authority, stronger relevance, or a niche where your brand already stands out.

Full set — scan only

All

The complete comparison set. Useful for scanning, but weaker for prioritisation until you break it down further.

Best for

Competitive benchmarking Finding where competitors are ahead Identifying missing topic coverage Prioritising by competitive pressure

The page for the stakeholder questions: Who is beating us? Where are we losing? What should we go after next?

Mental model

Market Comparison shows where you stand versus competitors — and exactly where the gaps are.

!

Common mistake

Focusing only on who ranks higher overall. Useful, but incomplete. Ask: higher because of what topics? Higher because of what sources? Higher in which parts of the landscape? The page is strongest for identifying specific gaps — not for declaring winners and losers.
